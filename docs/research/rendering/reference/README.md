# Look references

Frames the owner has pointed at as the target. They are for *judging* our
output, not for copying: no pixel of them ships. An agent may ingest them
sparingly (a few per session) alongside our own probe screenshots to give a
vibe check that numbers cannot — "does this read as the thing we are aiming
at?" — before handing the visual call back to the owner.

The technique research behind each target lives in the sibling docs; these are
the pictures those docs are trying to reach.

| File | What it shows | Provenance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `waterfall-target-wide.jpg` | A fall from downstream: narrow bright ribbon feathering at its edges, hugging a vertical face, blown-out impact patch on the pool, low mist drifting off the base and over the water | Owner-supplied frame of Skyrim waterfall footage, 2026-09-08 |
| `waterfall-target-close.jpg` | The same fall closer and higher: the lip constrained to a notch between rocks, the body brightening downward, and the plunge as the brightest thing in frame | Owner-supplied frame, same session |

What to read off them when comparing our own frames
([waterfalls-realtime.md](../waterfalls-realtime.md) has the techniques):

- The fall is **narrow for its height** and constrained at the lip, not a wide
  curtain hung across the cliff.
- Its edges are **feathered**, not cut — the ribbon dissolves sideways into
  spray rather than ending on a hard silhouette.
- It is the **brightest thing in frame**, near-white against grey rock, and it
  gets brighter downward as the water aerates.
- The **impact patch is wider than the fall** and blows out white, then fades
  outward across the pool rather than stopping at a rim.
- **Mist is low and horizontal** — it sits on the pool and drifts sideways;
  it does not form a vertical column beside the fall.
- The rock behind and beside the fall is **wet and darkened**, and it shows
  through the thin parts of the body.
