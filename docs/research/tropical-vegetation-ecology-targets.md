# Tropical vegetation ecology: density, composition and spatial-structure targets

**Phase 10 research, 2026-08-30.** Evidence base for procedural vegetation
placement across five exemplar Black Marsh landscape types. Companion to
[vegetation-density-design.md](vegetation-density-design.md) (which sets the
*design* synthesis — legibility, variance, mod reference); this document is
the *ecology* side: what real tropical swamp/forest systems measure, per
stratum, per landscape type, with sources for every number. The game
translation table is at the end (§7).

Reading guide: each landscape section gives (a) vertical structure, (b) stem
densities per stratum, (c) light/canopy numbers, (d) spatial pattern. §6
covers cross-cutting spatial structure (riparian, edges, gaps, microtopography)
and §6.5 lists the counterintuitive facts games usually get wrong.

## 1. Tropical lowland rainforest / jungle (terra firme analogue)

### 1.1 Vertical strata

Classic profile (Richards, *The Tropical Rain Forest*, 1952/1996; confirmed by
modern plot work):

| Stratum | Height | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Emergents | 35–60 m | isolated giants punching through the canopy |
| Main canopy | 20–35 m | continuous crown layer; intercepts ~90 %+ of light |
| Sub-canopy / understory trees | 5–20 m | shade-tolerant trees + juveniles of canopy species |
| Shrub / sapling layer | 1–5 m | saplings, treelets, palms, large herbs |
| Herb / ground layer | 0–1 m | mostly **bare litter** with scattered ferns/herbs/seedlings |

### 1.2 Stem densities per stratum (per hectare)

| Stratum | Density | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Trees ≥10 cm dbh | **300–800/ha; benchmark 500–600/ha** | [ScienceDirect Lowland Forests overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/lowland-forests) ("typically 500–600 trees/ha >10 cm dbh"); Sri Lanka 4×1 ha plots ~825/ha ([Kanneliya/Sinharaja](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S1314283616001123)); central Amazon terra firme ~639/ha ([Biodiv. & Conservation](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1023/A:1008908615271.pdf)); Nigeria Oban 306/ha ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/274375148_Tree_Species_Composition_and_Diversity_in_Oban_Forest_Reserve_Nigeria)) |
| — of which large (≥70 cm dbh) | **~18/ha (2.4 % of stems, 33 % of biomass)** | [Australian wet tropics, PLOS One 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0208377) |
| — of which 10–20 cm dbh | ~60 % of all stems ≥10 cm | same study |
| Small trees / saplings 2.5–10 cm dbh | **~4,900–7,500/ha** | [W. Brazilian Amazon, density by forest type](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Density-individuals-per-hectare-of-forest-components-separated-by-forest-type-a_tbl1_222327300) (open ~7,500, dense ~5,800, ecotone ~4,900) |
| Small saplings 10–50 cm tall | **0.7–7.5/m² (7,000–75,000/ha)** in non-gap forest; La Selva low end, BCI/Cocha Cashu/Manaus 4.8–7.5/m² | [Harms, Powers & Montgomery 2004, Biotropica](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1744-7429.2004.tb00294.x) |
| Lianas ≥2 cm | **150–500/ha** (Sarawak valleys 348, hilltops 164, Proctor 440; Neotropical range 105–1,414/ha) | [Putz & Chai via Xishuangbanna liana study](https://www.scielo.sa.cr/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0034-77442009000100020); [liana-infestation modelling, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6856694/) |
| Lianas ≥1 cm | ~1,077/ha (62 % of them 1–2 cm) | [Central African forest, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11896882/) |

Lianas ≈ **25 % of woody stems** overall, and **63–86 % of trees ≥10 cm dbh
carry lianas** in Neotropical studies ([carbon-stocks liana paper, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3730642/)).
Epiphytes: only **~10–15 %** of lowland rainforest trees carry conspicuous
epiphytes (~15 % mean epiphyte cover), vs 56–100 % of trees in montane/cloud
forest ([French Guiana lowland cloud forest study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230365045_Epiphyte_Biomass_and_Canopy_Microclimate_in_the_Tropical_Lowland_Cloud_Forest_of_French_Guiana_Epiphyte_Abundance_in_Lowland_Cloud_Forest); [SW China montane, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4391920/)).

### 1.3 Canopy closure and light

- Closed moist-forest canopy closure **>80–90 %** (explicit contrast with
  open woodland <25–30 % crown cover: [Frontiers Isoberlinia woodlands](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/forests-and-global-change/articles/10.3389/ffgc.2026.1800379/full)).
- Light at the floor: **1–2 % of full sun** is the standard figure
  ([CSU Stanislaus](https://www.csustan.edu/biology/stan-state-greenhouse/rainforest-understory-adaptations)); measured **~2 % diffuse
  transmittance at 1 m height, rising to >10 % at 9 m**
  ([Montgomery & Chazdon 2001, Ecology](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1890/0012-9658(2001)082[2707:FSCAAL]2.0.CO;2));
  **5.7 % of PAR reaches 1 m** above the floor at Tapajós, 92 % absorbed by
  canopy ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168192319302692)).
- Consequence: **the interior herb layer is thin.** Intact Luquillo (Puerto
  Rico) understory measured **~20 % herbaceous cover and ~70 % bare
  ground/litter**; after hurricanes opened the canopy this flipped to **70 %
  herb cover, 6 % bare** ([Luquillo warming experiment, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7452782/)).
  That flip is *the* number for understory response to light: closed canopy
  ⇒ ~20 % groundcover; open canopy ⇒ ~70 %.
- Eye-level visibility in primary forest interior: **20–30 m sightlines**;
  "a flashlight is more useful than a machete"
  ([worldrainforests.com ground layer](https://worldrainforests.com/05-rainforest-floor.html); [Bouchard, "Is There Really a Jungle Out There?"](https://medium.com/the-philipendium/is-there-really-a-jungle-out-there-17d50f45b8aa)).

### 1.4 Gap dynamics

- Gap definition: canopy openings ≥20–40 m² (Brokaw). **Most gaps are small**:
  <200 m² = 76 % of openings at La Selva; size-frequency follows a power law
  ([gap size distribution, Biogeosciences 2013](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/10/6769/2013/); [env. control of gap sizes](https://bg.copernicus.org/preprints/bg-2016-320/bg-2016-320.pdf)).
- **Fraction of forest area in gap at any moment: ~2–5 %** (lidar: 2.0 % Ducke
  Reserve, 4.8 % Tapajós — [Hunter et al. 2015, PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0132144));
  ~2.5 %/yr of floor gets a new gap above it (PNG montane,
  [Treefall Gap Dynamics, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260184305_Treefall_Gap_Dynamics_in_a_Tropical_Rain_Forest_in_Papua_New_Guinea)). Larger gaps
  are more frequent on floodplains and steep slopes.
- Gap regrowth: stem densities **spike within 3–6 years then self-thin**,
  especially in gaps >150 m² ([Brokaw 1985, Gap-Phase Regeneration](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/records/65df7e754c5aef494fe2ec74));
  pioneers occur **only** in gaps (~1 pioneer species/20 m² in gaps, zero
  under closed canopy), and liana density/richness is significantly higher in
  gaps ([Schnitzer & Carson 2001, Ecology](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1890/0012-9658%282001%29082%5B0913%3ATGATMO%5D2.0.CO%3B2)).
  The **area colonised by pioneers averages 3.4× the projected canopy
  opening** (underestimate 44–515 %) — gap thicket spills past the hole
  ([Los Tuxtlas gap definition study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/231932629_Pioneer_species_distribution_in_treefall_gaps_in_Neotropical_rain_forest_a_gap_definition_and_its_consequences)).
- Elevated gap densities are transient: sapling density only slightly higher
  7 yrs after gap formation, indistinguishable from non-gap at 12 yrs
  ([Kong et al. 2024, Ecology](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ecy.4443)). Some gaps stall as
  **liana tangles** — impenetrable thickets that persist for decades
  ([Marquette liana review](https://epublications.marquette.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1692&context=bio_fac)).
- Gap closure: ~1.1–1.2 m height gain/yr; gaps also close sideways (6–10 % of
  area by lateral ingrowth) ([Hunter et al. 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0132144)).

## 2. Jungle with pools / flooded forest (igapó, várzea, peat swamp)

### 2.1 Density and composition vs flood depth/duration

Amazonian várzea is flooded **up to 210 days/yr under 10–15 m** water columns;
flood tolerance sorts species into sharp bands ([Wittmann et al. 2004, For. Ecol. Mgmt](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378112704002439);
open PDF at [mamiraua.org](https://www.mamiraua.org/documentos/e9ef61d940d61adf49b8ad388657a7a5.pdf)):

| Zone | Flood regime | Structure & density |
|---|---|---|
| **Chavascal** (shrub swamp in old channels/lake margins) | ~180–240 days/yr | dense, **species-poor shrub/low-tree thicket** — the "wall" around still water |
| **Low várzea** | 120–180 days/yr; 3–7 m flood height | 490/ha (early succession) → **~1,000/ha** (early secondary) → 434/ha (late); species 4 → 45 → 91 |
| **High várzea** | <3 m flood | **~407/ha**, up to 172 species — converging on terra firme |

Only **2.6 % of 222 tree species span the whole flood gradient**; 94 species
exclusive to low várzea, 103 to high várzea — composition changes almost
completely across a few metres of elevation ([Wittmann et al., Folia Geobotanica](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02803209)).
Flooding is confirmed experimentally as the primary filter
([seedling zonation, Forests 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/f17040496)).

**Igapó (blackwater) is sparser and lower** than várzea/terra firme: across
five Amazon forest types stems ranged **167–683/ha**, with the highest density
and most closed canopy in várzea and terra firme and the smallest basal area
and biomass in igapó ([várzea vs terra firme, Ecuador](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/278785180_Varzea_forest_vs_terra_firme_forest_floristics_and_physical_structure_in_the_Ecuadorean_Amazon));
igapó trees are shorter and thinner than terra firme, with about half the
species count ([Colombian Orinoco, Forests 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/f12111568)).
So: **more standing water ⇒ fewer, shorter, tougher trees — except the
chavascal shrub ring, which is denser than anything around it.**

Tropical peat swamp forest (the closest Black Marsh analogue): **1,200–1,825
trees/ha** ([restored Kalimantan peat swamp, Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11027-018-9793-0));
stratified with upper canopy at ~35 m, a closed layer 15–25 m and an open
small-tree layer 7–12 m, stilt/buttress roots and abundant pneumatophores
([Tropical Peat Swamp Forests of SE Asia, Springer](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-94-007-4001-3_5)).
Page et al. 1999 mapped **seven forest types in sequence across one peat
dome** by distance from the river (riverine → mixed swamp → low pole →
tall interior → very low canopy), i.e. whole-biome zonation driven by peat
depth/drainage over ~18 km ([Interdependence of peat and vegetation](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11744186_Interdependence_of_peat_and_vegetation_in_a_tropical_peat_swamp_forest)).

### 2.2 Zonation around standing pools (open water → terra firme)

Freshwater macrophyte belts are depth-controlled ([Submerged Vegetation in
Shallow Lakes, Springer](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-1-4020-4410-6_200); [FAO aquatic plants review](https://www.fao.org/4/ac858e/ac858e03.htm); [NH DES BB-44](https://www.des.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt341/files/documents/2020-01/bb-44.pdf)):

| Belt | Water depth | Typical width | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open water | >3 m (light limit 9–14 m in clear water) | — | bare, or free-floating plants |
| Submerged plants | ~0.6–3 m | depends on slope | underwater weed, invisible from shore |
| Floating-leaved | **0.5–3 m** | a few m to ~10 m on gentle slopes | waterlilies/spatterdock; absent on wind-exposed shores |
| Emergent reedbelt | **0–1.5 m** depth, onto saturated soil | 2–10 m (gentle shores more) | reeds/cattails/sedges at **~28–80 live stems/m²** (to 200/m² max) ([Phragmites density studies, PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0173007); [Point Pelee](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/on/pelee/nature/conservation/marais-marsh/diminution-decrease)) |
| Shrub thicket / chavascal | seasonally flooded margin | 5–20 m | dense species-poor scrub |
| Flood-tolerant trees | above frequent-flood line | grades over 10s of m | buttressed/kneed swamp trees, density per §2.1 |

Belt widths are slope-driven: the depth bands above are fixed, so on a 1:20
shore each metre of depth band = 20 m of ring; on steep banks rings compress
to 1–3 m. Wind fetch suppresses floating-leaved and reed belts on exposed
shores ([peat lake macrophyte strategies, Hydrobiologia](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10750-021-04618-6)).

## 3. Mangrove lagoon / salt marsh coast

### 3.1 Mangrove zonation and per-zone density

Mangroves band by tidal inundation, salinity and elevation
([Ellison, Sundarbans zonation, J. Ecology](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1046/j.1365-2745.2000.00500.x)):

- **Seaward fringe (Rhizophora-type, prop roots):** lower elevation, flushed
  daily, moderate salinity (38–57 g/kg in Gulf of Fonseca).
- **Mid/basin (Avicennia-type, pneumatophores):** higher elevation, poorly
  flushed, hypersaline (80–140 g/kg dry season) — same study
  ([Red Sea/Fonseca zonation work](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/238714440_Zonation_Pattern_of_Avicennia_marina_and_Rhizophora_mucronata_along_the_Red_Sea_Coast_Egypt)).
- **Landward (Bruguiera/Ceriops-type):** occasional spring-tide flooding,
  grading to salt flat or terrestrial forest ([SW India zonation, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2287884X1830013X)).

Densities:

| Measure | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Stems >5 cm, SE/E Asia | **195–2,209/ha, mean 765** | [mangrove biomass estimation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0272771420306685) |
| Full range across zones (SW India) | **10–13,846 stems/ha** | [SW India zonation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2287884X1830013X) |
| Riverine vs fringe trees | 740 vs 623/ha | [Muara Kubu, W. Kalimantan](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333103006_Structure_and_composition_of_riverine_and_fringe_mangroves_at_Muara_Kubu_protected_areas) |
| Riverine vs fringe saplings | 3,152 vs 1,980/ha | same |
| Riverine vs fringe seedlings | 20,600 vs 13,875/ha | same |
| Structural gradient | size ↑, density ↓ along transition→basin→fringe; **dwarf/scrub stands = very many tiny stems** | [Brazilian physiographic types](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304377013000922) |
| Canopy fractional cover | **0.17–0.96 within one system** | [gap fraction method](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327391263_ESTIMATING_MANGROVE_FOREST_DENSITY_USING_GAP_FRACTION_METHOD_AND_VEGETATION_TRANSFORMATION_INDICES_APPROACH) |

Caveat: crisp mono-specific banding is common but **not universal** — many
Australian/Thai transects show mosaics rather than bands
([Bunt, Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-94-009-8037-2_8)). For the game: band the archetype, break it with patches.

### 3.2 Salt marsh / salt pan structure

([New Georgia Encyclopedia tidal marshes](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/geography-environment/tidal-marshes/); [Bertness New England zonation](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221958548_Zonation_of_Spartina_Patens_and_Spartina_Alterniflora_in_New_England_Salt_Marsh); [salt pan formation, IOPscience](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/acc418))

- **Creek bank:** tallest cordgrass (to ~3 m) in a narrow strip along channels
  — a miniature riparian effect.
- **Low marsh:** flooded twice daily; near-monoculture cordgrass ~1–2 m tall;
  in SE-US-type coasts this zone can run for **kilometres**; in other settings
  it is a band tens of metres wide.
- **High marsh:** only inches higher, flooded ~1–2 h/day; short grass
  (0.1–0.3 m), mixed rush/saltgrass/glasswort.
- **Salt pans:** bare patches in the high marsh where salinity reaches
  ~100 ppt; nothing grows; form in the lower supratidal, typically metres to
  tens of metres across.
- Zonation mechanism: physical stress sets lower limits, competition sets
  upper limits — bands are sharp because inches of elevation matter.

### 3.3 Seagrass and mudflat gaps

- Tropical lagoon shoot densities **~280–1,990 shoots/m²**; cover classes used
  in the field: unvegetated 0–5 %, low-density 10–25 %, high-density 50–75 %
  ([Mexican reef lagoons, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2024.1320194/full);
  [tropical lagoon benthos, Sci. Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-70318-1)).
- Meadows below **~160 shoots/m²** become erosional — sparse seagrass does not
  persist; expect either meadow or bare mud, not a smooth gradient
  ([Limnology & Oceanography](https://aslopubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/lno.12178)).
- Mudflats between meadows and below the mangrove fringe are **bare** —
  the visual signature of the coast is vegetated bands separated by genuinely
  empty flats.

## 4. Closed-canopy deep swamp (permanently dark, root-dominated)

Analogues: cypress–tupelo swamp (US) and interior peat swamp forest (Borneo).

### 4.1 Stand structure

- Basal area is **huge relative to stem count**: ~50 m²/ha in Louisiana
  cypress-tupelo (vs ~30–35 m²/ha for terra firme rainforest) — fewer, fatter,
  heavily buttressed trunks ([continuous vs seasonal flooding study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/037811279090215W)).
- Second-growth (63 yr) stand: **1,260 cypress + 258 tupelo/ha**, self-thinning
  hard ([USDA Silvics, baldcypress](https://research.fs.usda.gov/silvics/baldcypress)); old-growth stands thin to low hundreds/ha of large trunks.
  Under prolonged ponding trees can be "widely scattered"
  ([Kentucky cypress swamp fact sheet](https://eec.ky.gov/Nature-Preserves/conserving_natural_areas/natural_areas/Natural%20Communities%20of%20Kentucky/Cypress%20(tupelo)%20swamp.pdf)).
- Flood depth up to **1.3 m standing water**, retained through the growing
  season ([FNAI floodplain swamp](https://www.fnai.org/PDFs/NC/Flood_Swamp.pdf)).

### 4.2 Root domination of the ground plane

- **Cypress knees: ~7,800–10,100 per hectare** in flooded old-growth —
  roughly one knee per m² in dense patches, typically ~1.5 m tall (max 4.3 m);
  ~25 knees per tree, clustered within a few metres of trunks
  ([mobile-LiDAR knee carbon study, Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/forests-and-global-change/articles/10.3389/ffgc.2025.1427376/full);
  [Wikipedia cypress knee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypress_knee)). Knee height tracks average water depth.
- Peat swamp equivalent: pneumatophores "abundant on the forest floor",
  plus stilt roots and buttresses ([SE Asia peat swamp forests, Springer](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-94-007-4001-3_5)).

### 4.3 What grows on the floor: almost nothing

Multiple independent community descriptions agree:

- "Understory and ground cover are **usually sparse due to frequent
  flooding**" — occasional buttonbush, lizard's-tail, ferns
  ([Louisiana natural communities](https://www.wlf.louisiana.gov/assets/Resources/Publications/Natural_Communities_Fact_Sheets/Cypress_swamp__Cypress-tupelo_swamp.pdf)).
- "Trees are often buttressed, and the understory and groundcover are sparse"
  ([FNAI floodplain swamp](https://www.fnai.org/PDFs/NC/Flood_Swamp.pdf); [Virginia DCR bald cypress–tupelo swamps](https://www.dcr.virginia.gov/natural-heritage/natural-communities/ncpa1)).
- Mechanism: flooding **prevents seedling germination and survival**,
  truncates species richness, and kills established stems — regeneration
  happens only on hummocks, logs and stumps above the waterline
  ([USDA SRS flooding effects on stand development](https://www.srs.fs.usda.gov/pubs/gtr/gtr_srs175/gtr_srs175_431.pdf)).

So the correct look is: water + root architecture + trunks, with shrubs and
herbs only on raised microsites (hummocks, logs, bases of trees), plus heavy
moss/epiphyte dressing on trunks (humid, stable microclimate — epiphyte logic
of §1.2 applies, boosted by permanent humidity; Spanish-moss-type drapery is
characteristic of cypress swamps). Duckweed/floating plants can carpet still
water surfaces without adding floor clutter.

### 4.4 Hummock–hollow microtopography

- Vertical relief **~0.2–0.5 m** (hummock surface ≥0.2 m above water table);
  horizontal scale **1–10 m**, model default separation ~1 m with **~25 % of
  area as hollows** ([peatland hummock–hollow classification, Biogeosciences](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/16/3491/2019/);
  [ELM_SPRUCE microtopography parameters](https://scholarworks.boisestate.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1656&context=geo_facpubs)).
- In forested wetlands, hummocks sit at exactly the elevation where trees
  grow best, and hollows are open water/mud — **vegetation maps 1:1 onto
  microtopography** ([HESS forested-wetland microtopography](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/23/5069/2019/); [tidal swamp hummocks](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372121890_Presence_of_Hummock_and_Hollow_Microtopography_Reflects_Shifting_Balances_of_Shallow_Subsidence_and_Root_Zone_Expansion_Along_Forested_Wetland_River_Gradients)).

## 5. Upland tropical hills and dry/montane forest edge

### 5.1 Density vs altitude

- General montane trend: **canopy height falls, stem density rises** with
  elevation; Andes transects report increasing stem density with elevation
  ([Costa Rica 2,700 m transect, PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0122905)).
- Same Costa Rica transect: basal area and stem number varied <2× along the
  gradient **except the 2,800 m cloud-forest summit, where both roughly
  doubled**; species diversity peaked at 400–600 m and fell above.
- Ridge-vs-gully: canopy is taller in gullies, stem counts higher on
  ridges/upper slopes ([seasonal tropical forests, Plants 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/plants12061343)) — drainage
  alone produces a short/dense (ridge) vs tall/open-floor (gully) contrast.
- Upper limit: **elfin forest** <10 m tall, gnarled, dense crowns, drenched in
  bryophytes/epiphytes ([NatureServe tropical montane rainforest](https://explorer.natureserve.org/Taxon/ELEMENT_GLOBAL.2.1298972/Tropical_Montane_Rainforest_Ecobiome)).
  Epiphyte host fraction rises from ~10 % (lowland) to **56–100 %** of trees
  (montane/cloud) ([SW China epiphyte community, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4391920/)).

### 5.2 Dry forest vs rainforest vs savanna

| System | Stems | Canopy | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tropical dry forest | **4,600–14,000 stems >2.5 cm/ha**; stems mostly 3–15 cm, often multi-stemmed; can exceed 1 stem/m² | 1–3 layers, LAI 3–7, deciduous part-year | [Dry Forest overview, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/dry-forest) |
| Dry vs moist (Indian sal) | dry 1,006 stems/ha, BA 19.6 m²/ha vs moist 438 stems/ha, BA 56.5 m²/ha | — | same overview |
| Dry woodland (African) | 15–120 trees >30 cm dbh/ha; local sucker clusters >1,000 stems/ha | **crown cover <25–30 %** | [FAO woody formations](https://www.fao.org/4/w4442E/w4442e05.htm); [Frontiers Isoberlinia](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/forests-and-global-change/articles/10.3389/ffgc.2026.1800379/full) |
| Savanna | scattered trees, cover 0–80 % (typically low), **continuous grass layer**, fire-maintained | open | [Current Biology savannas & dry forests](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0960982218303154) |
| Cerrado gradient | woody density **falls 47 %** from cerradão (dry forest) to open scrub savanna | — | [Piauí structural gradient](https://www.academia.edu/2248778/A_structural_gradient_in_cerrado_vegetation_of_Brazil_changes_in_woody_plant_density_species_richness_life_history_and_plant_composition) |

Pattern worth copying: **dry forest is many thin stems, not few thick ones**
— it reads as scrubby and cluttered at chest height with a low broken canopy,
opposite of rainforest (open at chest height, closed overhead). The
forest→savanna transition is fire/soil-mediated and can be abrupt
([Frontiers biome transitions](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2018.00104/full)).

## 6. Cross-cutting spatial structure

### 6.1 Riparian corridors (gallery forests)

- In open landscapes (savanna/grassland), watercourses carry **full closed
  forest in a narrow ribbon**: gallery forests are "sometimes only a few
  metres wide", end **abruptly** (ecotone a few metres), and arch over the
  channel as a green tunnel ([Wikipedia gallery forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallery_forest); [WorldAtlas](https://www.worldatlas.com/articles/what-is-a-gallery-forest.html)).
- Magnitude: the corridor is not "denser grass" — it is a *biome jump* from
  savanna (tens of trees/ha, cover <30 %) to closed forest (400–1,000+
  stems/ha ≥3–5 cm, closed canopy). Call it a **5–20× density multiplier**
  with closed canopy, over a band ~10–50 m each side of the channel,
  with tree height also jumping ([cerrado gallery forest dynamics, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5018118/);
  [gallery forest–savanna gradient](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/235623093_Distribution_of_tree_species_along_a_gallery_forest-savanna_gradient_Patterns_overlaps_and_ecological_thresholds)).
- Inside already-closed forest, the riparian signal flips at the bank itself:
  the channel is a canopy break, so banks get the **edge wall** (§6.2), and
  floodplains get more/larger treefall gaps (§1.4).

### 6.2 Forest edges — the "green wall"

- Wherever light reaches the ground (riverbanks, coasts, clearings, trails,
  forest borders), understory growth explodes into a dense tangled wall;
  the impenetrable-jungle stereotype comes from explorers seeing riverbank
  walls and assuming the interior matched ([Jungle, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle); [worldrainforests.com](https://worldrainforests.com/05-rainforest-floor.html)).
- Quantified edge influence: edges have elevated densities of small stems and
  lianas; most structural change occurs **within 100 m**, detectable to
  ~200 m, microclimate altered 100–300 m in; 19 % of the world's tropical
  forest is within 100 m of an edge ([fragmentation & carbon, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5357863/);
  [edge effects in fragmented forests](https://www.academia.edu/23886046/Edge_effects_in_fragmented_forests_implications_for_conservation);
  [liana edge effects, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5916267/)).
- Light response magnitude — reuse the Luquillo numbers (§1.3): ground cover
  ~20 % under closed canopy → ~70 % where the canopy is opened. The *wall*
  (shrub/sapling/liana curtain 0–5 m up) is concentrated in the **first
  ~10–20 m** from the light source; the tail to 100 m is compositional, not
  visually dramatic.

### 6.3 Gaps as a fraction of closed forest

From §1.4: hold **2–5 % of closed-canopy forest area in gaps**, mostly
20–200 m² (76 % under 200 m²), rare 400–700 m² blowdowns, more/larger gaps on
floodplains and steep slopes; each gap carries a young thicket footprint
~3× its canopy hole.

### 6.4 Hummock–hollow wavelength

From §4.4: amplitude 0.2–0.5 m; horizontal scale 1–10 m (default spacing
~1 m in bogs, longer in forested swamps where buttress-driven); ~25 % hollows.
For game noise fields: **wavelength ~4–8 m** reads correctly at player scale;
put trees/shrubs on hummocks, water/mud/knees in hollows.

### 6.5 Counterintuitive facts games get wrong

1. **Primary rainforest interior is open at eye level** — 20–30 m sightlines,
   1–2 % light, thin herb layer, walkable floor. The machete jungle is edge,
   gap and secondary growth ([worldrainforests](https://worldrainforests.com/05-rainforest-floor.html), [Bouchard](https://medium.com/the-philipendium/is-there-really-a-jungle-out-there-17d50f45b8aa), Richards 1952).
2. **Density inverts between strata**: rainforest = closed overhead, open at
   chest height; dry scrub = open overhead, cluttered at chest height.
3. **Water edges are walls, water interiors are voids**: the densest
   vegetation in a swamp is the ring around open water (chavascal, reedbelt,
   green wall), while under the darkest canopy the floor is nearly bare.
4. **Deep swamp floor is roots, not plants**: up to ~10,000 knees/ha but
   almost zero shrubs/herbs in the water; greenery lives on raised microsites
   and on the trunks (moss/epiphytes).
5. **Most trees are small**: ~60 % of stems ≥10 cm dbh are 10–20 cm; giants
   are ~2 %/ha (≈18 emergents/ha). A forest of uniformly huge trunks reads
   wrong.
6. **Variance beats mean** (see [vegetation-density-design.md](vegetation-density-design.md)):
   real canopy cover in one mangrove system spans 0.17–0.96; gaps, pans,
   mudflats and thickets are the structure, not noise on top of it.
7. **Bands are sharp**: metres of elevation (flood/salt gradients) or inches
   (marsh) flip the community entirely — transitions should be abrupt, not
   smooth blends.

## 7. Game translation

Assumptions: 1 placed tree asset ≈ **2.5 real stems** (owner brief: 2–3);
1 shrub asset ≈ a clump of several real shrubs/saplings; herb layer is the
runtime groundcover system (% cover target), not placed objects. Saplings
2.5–10 cm dbh are visually merged: represent ~5–10 % of real sapling stems as
placed small-tree/shrub assets, the rest is implied by groundcover + the
canopy assets' own visual mass. Emergent giants use distinct large assets.

### 7.2 Spatial rules

| Rule | Value | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Gap fraction** (closed-canopy types 1, 2, 4, 5a) | 2–5 % of area in gaps of 20–200 m² (76 % of gaps <200 m²), rare 400–700 m²; more + larger on floodplains/steep slopes | §1.4 |
| **Gap fill** | inside a gap: shrub clumps ×4–8, herb cover 60–80 %, 1–3 pioneer saplings/20 m²; thicket footprint = 3× the canopy hole | §1.4 |
| **Edge wall** | within 10–20 m of any canopy break (river, lake, coast, clearing, road): shrub clumps ×4–8, herb cover 60–90 %, +lianas; decay to interior values by 50–100 m | §6.2, §1.3 |
| **Riparian multiplier (open landscapes 5b/5c)** | watercourses carry a gallery-forest ribbon 10–50 m each side at **closed-forest density** (use type-1 or type-2 column), with an abrupt (≤5 m) outer boundary | §6.1 |

| **Sightline check** (type 1, 4 interiors) | between placed shrub clumps, keep median eye-level open distance ~10 m, p95 ~30 m — this matches both the shipped-mod measurement and real interior visibility | [vegetation-density-design.md](vegetation-density-design.md), §6.5 |

These targets deliberately sit below full real-stem fidelity (a real hectare
of jungle carries 500–600 canopy stems + thousands of saplings; no browser
budget or navigation design survives that) but preserve the *ratios* between
landscape types and strata, which is what the eye actually reads. The
variance/wavelength/legibility rules in
[vegetation-density-design.md](vegetation-density-design.md) apply on top of
everything here.
