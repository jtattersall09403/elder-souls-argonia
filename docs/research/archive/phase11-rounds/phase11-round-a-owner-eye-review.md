# Round A, owner-eye review of the five exemplar blueprints (2026-09-07)

An agent stood in for the owner and looked at the five maps the way the owner
would: first with no checklist, asking what looks odd, gridded, too regular,
mis-sited or back to front; then walking (or paddling) in along every designed
approach; then checking the map against the design record and the answers the
owner accepted on 2026-09-07 (decision 0041 § Review 2026-09-07). Eight images
were opened in total: the five full maps, two Lilmoth district crops before the
changes and one after. Everything else was checked in the data.

What was found is fixed in the blueprints and their records, or written up
below as left alone with a reason. Rules that apply to every village or city
are rows in the Taste ledger of decision 0041, dated 2026-09-07. Things no
blueprint edit can fix are one line each under B8 in
[phase11-gap-plan.md](phase11-gap-plan.md).

A small tool change made the district views possible: the map renderer takes
`--crop x0,z0,x1,z1 --name <stem>` (world metres), so a quarter of a city can
be drawn at a scale where huts and lanes are legible.

## Lilmoth (the capital)

**What the map showed.** The bones are right: the road comes in from the west
round the pond, the arch stands square across it, the track climbs the
shoulder with the Hist court on the left, the council bench sits above the
bluff with the bell on its lip, the stair drops to Pusbottom, the quay deck is
the node at the water and the drowned quarter lies out under the shallows with
a dredged channel to the roadstead. Two things looked wrong at once. Pusbottom
was a grid: two dead-straight lanes 15 m apart running north to south, three
columns of huts at exactly 15 m steps, every hut canted a few degrees but all
of them on the same two bearings. The shore quarter was a ruler row of seven
huts along one straight line. Both are the "model village" look the owner has
ruled out, and both were declared `straight` in a culture whose walkways are
supposed to follow the ground.

**The walk in.** By the Blackrose road: the Hist crown shows over the crest,
the gate tower hides it, the arch takes you through, the tree comes back on the
left, the bell shows ahead at the top of the shoulder. That works. By lighter
from the roadstead: three lit tiers stacked, the ridge block hides the lamp for
a moment, the pier head comes into view. That works. Along the shore from the
south: the bell over the bluff, the pole wall and the fishers' roofs, the lane
between the last hut and the wall, then the corner of Pusbottom under the bell.
That works too, until you stand at the gate: from the gate yard the fishers'
roofs are in plain view 120 m below, and the only way to them was 500 m round
by the stair and the loop. A walking player would try to go straight down the
slope and find no path.

**What changed.** Pusbottom's loop now bends eleven times along the bluff foot
and out over the shallows, and the cross lane is a skewed link; both are plank
runs, straight from pile to pile between their bends, which is what laid planks
do. The fifteen huts hang off the lanes at their own offsets (5.5–6.1 m) and
their own spacing (12–19 m), each turned its own way with its door on the lane
side; no bearing is shared by more than two huts. The shore quarter is three
huts north of a bending lane and four south of it, staggered so none face each
other. A fishers' path now leaves the gate yard at the lantern and drops down
the slope (13 m over 120 m, no stretch over 20°) to the west end of the shore
lane, so the gate, the shore and Pusbottom make one network. The gate is called
the Blackrose gate, for the road it faces; the ids keep the old survey name
because ids are stable. The accepted answers on the drowned quarter (1–15 m
under your own walkway) and on free climbing are written into the record.

**What was left and why.** The lighter channel is a straight dredged line from
the pier head to the roadstead. It is the one honest straight line at the
water: a lightering port across a 1 m shelf dredges a marked cut, and the
record says so. The council bench keeps its single surveyed straight walkway.
Pusbottom's hut count stands at fifteen because the owner's question on its
density (warren or opened out) has no ruling yet. Eleven Pusbottom and quay
huts of the second bamboo-hut kind, the two kiosks and the gate lodging still
show door warnings that belong to the interiors pass another agent is running
today; their doors are re-derived when that pass lands.

## Nine-Trunks (a Hist village)

**What the map showed.** Nine trunks on a perfect circle at exactly 40°
intervals, eight huts filling eight identical gaps with identical 1.9 m woven
panels each side, the ring walk a true circle. It read as a compass drawing,
and the record described it that way (chord 14.37 m, gap 9.63 m, 1.9 m each
side). A ring of trunks that grew from one root system is not a nonagon.

**The walk in.** From the road spur: the elder trunk shows over the mangrove
canopy, the track bends, the arch is the one gap in the wall, the round house is
straight across the clearing. From the pier: the trunks stand above the reeds
like a comb, the landing path climbs to the gap. Both work; the accepted
answers (houses grown into the trunks, canvas pitches outside the gate for the
delegations, 22.6 m as the village default, the 149 m move) are all in the
data.

**What changed.** The ring was warped by a seeded jitter of up to 6.5° and
2 m per trunk, smoothly round the circle so that every hut still sits in its
gap. Chords now run 13.4–16.1 m, the clear gaps 7.6–10.3 m, and the woven
panels are cut to each gap (0.8–2.2 m), so no two are alike. The huts, the root
swells, the ring walk and the panels moved with the trunks; the doors were
re-derived and every hut still faces the ring walk within the ruling. The
record now says 22.6 m is an average, never an exact chord.

**What was left.** The pitch district is still a hand-drawn box and the
boundary a compass circle; neither is visible in the game and both are covered
by the gap plan's derived-boundaries batch.

## Mazzatun (a stone village on a slope)

**What the map showed.** The haul road climbs from the Hist in two hairpins to
a gate that stands across it, the yard runs to the great stair, the courses
rise along the two built risers, the pens sit on the top step behind the upper
riser. The accepted answers (a building site of rising courses, the Hist kept
below with the conduit climbing, pens behind the rise, the switchback haul
road) are all there. What looked wrong was a second way in: a footpath from the
western ridge straight down into the pens, past the shelf gate rather than
through it.

**The walk in.** From the gorge floor: the conduit pillar on the lip, the amber
line down the face, the first hairpin turns you away, the second brings the
gate wall across the road, the yard, the stair, the carved figures. That works.
From the ridge track: the watch column first, the track drops below the rise,
the dais at the shelf head and the gate behind it. Then the pens are meant to
be a reveal from the head of the pens stair. But the record's own text sent
the ridge walker down the shoulder footpath to the rack and the pens first,
which spoils the reveal and walks round the gate; and measured on the ground
that path had 42° in its first draft and 50–58° in every re-route the router
could find, because the rise west of the pens is a 4 m rock step.

**What changed.** The shoulder footpath is out. The record describes the ridge
approach through the gate, with the pens hidden behind the built riser until
the pens stair; the rack and the pens texts no longer speak of a path arriving
from the west. The raiders' back way (the lore of the taken brought in from
behind) is kept as an idea and handed to the gap plan as a terrain request: a
cut step in the rise, after which the path can return.

**What was left.** The three district boxes are hand-drawn rectangles (gap
plan B3). The three 3 m "roads" stay narrower than the 4.3 m class width for
the reason the record gives (the gate opening and the cut).

## The Standing Charge (the wamasu pond)

**What the map showed.** A small closed pan with its offering shelf, cairns,
dead trees and the hunter's stand on the knoll; a poling lane up the creek from
the south-east with four marker poles; a path from the landing up to the bank.
Nothing gridded, nothing mis-sited. The pond is small on the map because the
pool request that deepens it to 2.5 m has not yet been carved.

**The walk in.** By boat: the mouth pole, the poles up the creek under closed
canopy, the landing, the trodden path uphill toward the bare crowns, the
offering bank where the path meets the water. On foot from the west rise: the
scarred crown of the north dead tree 8 m over the canopy, the ramp of the stand
facing the walker on the knoll. Both work.

**What changed.** Only the record. The owner's four rulings (2.5 m and a
swimmable fight; charged water as heavy damage over time; a loud clearing,
the detour abandoned and travel times dropping; the offering-makers right and
the hunter wrong) were not written anywhere in the place's own record. They
are now its answers and two new lair rules (7 and 8), and the stand's and the
platform's own texts say who was right.

**What was left.** Pole densification along the channel and the stand's
support piece are compiler rules that do not exist yet (already listed).

## The Licensed Stage (the sap camp)

**What the map showed.** The hero Hist at the centre, the stage on its
channel face, the licence board at the landing where a canoe reads it first,
the track from the south-east bending twice to die at the stair foot, the mud
hut on its pad behind the trunk. Nothing wrong on the ground.

**The walk in.** From the road: the crown over the canopy from 870 m out, then
lost overhead under closure, the pale trunk at 30 m, the hut door and the mule
line at the first bend, the stair and the stage at the second. By canoe: the
crown over the channel, the board's roof over the reeds, the plank walk from
the landing to the stage foot. Both work.

**What changed.** The data already carried the accepted answers (the 54 m
tree, the mud hut, the board readable from the water, the 190 m² clearing),
but the prose did not: the record and half the `why` texts still described a
tent, its open end and a walk-in shell interior. All of that now says hut and
doorway, the asset table gives the hut's real size and yaw, and the five open
questions are closed with the rulings. The spur's id keeps its draft name
(`tent-spur`) because ids are stable; the record says so.
