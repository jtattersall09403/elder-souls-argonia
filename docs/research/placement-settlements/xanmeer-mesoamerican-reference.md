# Xanmeers: Mesoamerican and wet-tropical archaeological reference

Real-world grounding for xanmeer sites, ruin exteriors and dungeon interiors
(dungeon family 1 in [docs/world/70-dungeons-interiors.md](../../world/70-dungeons-interiors.md) §47;
landmark family for Phases 11–13). This is **inspiration, not prescription** —
use these patterns to make placement causal and ruins archaeologically honest,
not to copy Maya iconography. **Canon always wins over analogy.**

## 1. Canon first — what the dossiers already fix (cite, don't re-derive)

Read before designing any xanmeer:
[material-culture.md](../../../world/sources/lore/topics/material-culture.md) § Building,
[duskfall.md](../../../world/sources/lore/duskfall.md),
[hist-and-sap.md](../../../world/sources/lore/topics/hist-and-sap.md),
[lost-peoples.md](../../../world/sources/lore/topics/lost-peoples.md).

Fixed by canon (UESP Lore:Xanmeer, Lore:Duskfall, Lore:Xit-Xaht):
- Stepped stone pyramids ("Stone-Nests") linked by **stone bridges**; interiors
  deceptively large and mazelike; traps, relics, working millennia-old defences
  powered by **vakka stones**; **each xanmeer housed its tribe's Hist in an
  interior chamber**; mechanisms include the bijum door-release (pull = floor
  drops you deeper), the tutan-wei counterweight puzzle, and fire-spout traps;
  Flint Vine grows in dark interiors (rope-ladders, hauling).
- Built pre-Duskfall by ancient Argonians under **Nisswo priest-kings** as
  monuments appeasing **Sithis the Destroyer**, with mortal sacrifice; modern
  Argonians judge them a moral error (*shunatei* — fear of death/forgetting)
  yet **maintain them as warnings**, and some tribes still inhabit them
  (Hissmir) for their walls and defences (UESP Lore:Xanmeer).
- **Construction methods are lost and canonically mysterious** — even the
  Empire cannot build lasting stone on the marsh ("half-sunken villas and
  decaying prisons", UESP cites A History of Lilmoth). Imperial scholars
  misattribute xanmeers to the Yespest (lost-peoples.md) — a usable in-world
  "mysterious builder race" trope we can play *against*.
- Named canon xanmeers to reuse for naming grammar and spread: Murkmire —
  Teeth of Sithis, Ixtaxh, Lakemire, Xinchei-Konu, Xul-Thuxis, Vakka-Bok;
  Shadowfen — Hissmir, Bogmother, Atanaz, Chid-Moska, Xal Ithix, Xal Thak,
  Hatching Pools; Blackwood — Rockgrove, Xi-Tsei, Xal Irasotl; plus Haj Uxith,
  Norg-Tzel, Xith-Izkul (UESP Lore:Xanmeer § Known Xanmeers). Ruins reach
  southern Morrowind's coast, so province-wide placement is legitimate
  (duskfall.md).

Everything below is **real-world analogy** feeding design; where it collides
with the above (e.g. Maya pyramids are mostly solid — canon xanmeers are
mazelike inside), canon wins.

## 2. Site planning: a xanmeer is never alone

The lowland Maya centre is the best single model for what surrounds a pyramid
(Angkor is the model for what *waters* it, §3).

- **Site cores are plaza compositions, not lone monuments**: temple pyramids,
  range buildings ("palaces"), ballcourts and stelae arranged around paved
  plazas on artificial platforms and acropolis mounds, with processional axes
  and sight-lines ([Fiveable: Maya site layout](https://fiveable.me/maya-art-and-architecture/unit-6/principles-maya-site-layout/study-guide/lgmPqvL0MZugueNU)).
  An acropolis is *accreted height*: centuries of buildings raised on the
  rubble of predecessors (Copán). → A "xanmeer" placement should usually be a
  **precinct**: main pyramid + plaza + 2–5 subsidiary platforms + the canon
  stone bridges, giving exteriors approach grammar, combat spaces and multiple
  entrances instead of one lonely mesh.
- **E-Groups**: the earliest standardised Maya monumental form — a western
  viewing platform facing a long eastern tripartite platform, tied to solar
  horizon events; nearly every civic core has one ([Šprajc 2021, PNAS/PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8078784/);
  [Aimers & Rice 2006](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/ancient-mesoamerica/article/abs/astronomy-ritual-and-the-interpretation-of-maya-egroup-architectural-assemblages/E872AA5228F4BD8CEFD22E3FCC21F58E)).
  → Precedent for a small **standard astronomical/calendrical sub-complex** at
  xanmeer sites — canon hands us the peg: **Xinchei-Konu**, the stone calendar
  monument in Murkmire (material-culture.md § Calendar). A recognisable
  "calendar court" repeated across sites teaches players to read ruins.
- **Sacbeob (raised white causeways)**: rubble-cored, stone-walled, plaster-
  capped roads raised above flooding, from intra-site links to the 100 km
  Cobá–Yaxuná sacbe; recent lidar found 8,000+ structures strung along that
  one road ([mesoweb: Yaxuná–Cobá causeway](https://www.mesoweb.com/publications/CAA/09_text.html);
  [Archaeology Magazine 2020](https://archaeology.org/news/2020/02/25/200226-mexico-maya-sacbe/)).
  Chichén Itzá's core connects to its Sacred Cenote by a 300 m sacbe
  ([Wikipedia: Sacred Cenote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Cenote)).
  → Maps directly onto our canon **stone-flagged causeways** (Stormhold→
  Bogmother, "firmer than a Breton road", material-culture.md). Treat surviving
  causeway *segments* as the pre-Duskfall route layer: routes, dungeon
  approaches and POI chains should follow them, and a causeway fragment in the
  swamp is a **foreshadowing device** — it always leads somewhere.
- **Lidar-revealed density**: the 2018 PACUNAM survey (2,144 km² of Petén)
  found 61,480 structures — dense settlement, terracing, canalised wetlands,
  causeways and fortifications underneath "empty" jungle; cities were nodes in
  a continuously engineered landscape ([Canuto et al. 2018, Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aau0137)).
  → The ground *between* xanmeers should carry low-relief traces: house-mound
  clusters, field bunds, silted canals, causeway stubs. Cheap terrain-layer
  features that make the pre-Duskfall civilisation feel like a civilisation,
  not scattered set-pieces.

## 3. Water engineering: the causal answer to "why does this xanmeer exist HERE"

- **Tikal**: gravity-fed system of paved catchments, sand-filtration, sluices
  and reservoirs made from its own building-stone quarries, incl. the largest
  known Maya dam (80 m long, ~10 m high); the city ran on captured rain and
  died with the 9th-century droughts ([Scarborough et al. 2012, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1202881109);
  [Lucero 2023, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2306870120)).
  Quarry→reservoir is a beautiful loop: **the hole the pyramid came out of
  becomes the water the city drinks**.
- **Edzná**: a low, flood-prone valley made urban by ~31 canals and ~25
  reservoirs (22+ km of canals) that drained floods, stored the dry season,
  irrigated and farmed fish ([Encyclopedia.com: Edzná](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/edzna)).
- **Raised/wetland fields**: canal-and-platform field systems (Birds of
  Paradise, Belize: 14+ km² of fields and canals under forest) fed cities and
  *outlived* them — communities persisted on the wetland fields through the
  Classic collapse ([Beach et al. 2019, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1910553116);
  [2026 follow-up, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.2521892123)).
- **Angkor**, the other great wet-tropical monumental culture: a ~1,000 km²
  low-density "hydraulic city" of moated temple-mountains (Mount Meru
  cosmograms), giant barays (West Baray ~8×2 km), canals and embankments —
  simultaneously functional and ritual; its degradation contributed to the
  city's fall ([Fletcher et al. 2008, Antiquity](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/abs/water-management-network-of-angkor-cambodia/EC4E312C23A724E6B629B4A252FF15D9);
  [Buckley et al. 2010, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0909096107)).
  The Angkor Wat approach grammar — cross a moat on a long axial causeway,
  through concentric enclosures, to the mountain — is the strongest real
  template for a major xanmeer approach sequence.

→ **For causal records tied to our hydrology fields**: every xanmeer should
answer "what water did it master?" — a dammed arroyo, a drained backswamp with
relict raised fields, a spring, a canalised confluence, a moat. Pre-Duskfall
hydraulic works, now broken, then *explain the modern landscape*: a silted
baray is today's lake or reedbed; a breached dam is today's braided channel; a
canal is today's suspiciously straight bayou. The canon "hydraulic lower
levels / flooded lower strata / drains" of the dungeon family (§47) are the
interior half of this same system — drains should visibly exit to the exterior
waterworks. And per Birds of Paradise, **modern tribal villages sitting on
still-functioning ancient field systems** is archaeologically realistic and a
good settlement rationale.

## 4. Pyramid biography: construction, interiors, and what "intact" looked like

- **Pyramids are nested**: Mesoamerican builders periodically entombed a
  temple inside a larger successor — Copán's acropolis holds five main phases,
  and the **Rosalila** temple (c. AD 571) was buried *intact*, stucco and
  paint preserved, with the dynasty founder's tomb beneath its axis
  ([mused: Temple 16 & Rosalila tunnels](https://mused.com/guided/221/temple-16-and-rosalila-tunnels-copan/);
  [Copán Sculpture Museum](https://mayan.org/ruins/museums/copan-sculpture-museum/)).
  → Perfect precedent for **layered dungeon interiors**: an older, smaller,
  better-preserved (even still-painted) xanmeer *inside* the ruined outer one,
  reached through collapse gaps or archaeologist-style tunnels; oldest = deepest
  and most sacred. This also gives the canon Hist-chamber a natural location —
  at the buried core, on the original axis.
- **Interior space is corbel-vaulted**: walls step inward to a capstone, so
  rooms are high, dark and *narrow* (~1–3 m span; long galleries subdivided,
  little ventilation) ([mayan.org: corbel vault](https://mayan.org/civilization/corbel-vault/);
  [World History Encyclopedia: Maya architecture](https://www.worldhistory.org/Maya_Architecture/)).
  → Authentic texture for corridors, shrine rooms and the canon mazelike feel —
  but flag every fight space per §49 (rollClearance/sweep), and justify large
  chambers as courts, cisterns, or vertical shafts rather than wide vaults.
  Canon's "deceptively large" interiors are the licence to exceed the real
  limit *deliberately* (lost methods, vakka-stone engineering) — make players
  feel the transition from cramped real-vault space into impossible space.
- **Tombs, caches, stairways**: royal tombs sit deep inside/beneath (Pakal's
  crypt under Palenque's Temple of the Inscriptions, reached by a hidden
  interior vaulted stair from the summit sanctuary, with a "psychoduct" tube
  linking tomb to temple; [Wikipedia: Temple of the Inscriptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_the_Inscriptions));
  dedication caches (eccentric flints, censers) under floors and stelae
  (Rosalila finds, above); Copán's Hieroglyphic Stairway makes the staircase
  itself a 2,200-glyph dynastic text ([Wikipedia: Copán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cop%C3%A1n)).
  → Loot logic: treasure is *cached and entombed on the axis*, not scattered;
  stairways can carry readable history (Xinchei-Konu-style glyph stairs the
  jekka-wass can translate — quest hook).
- **They were red**: finished buildings were fully stuccoed and painted —
  dominantly red with polychrome accents — with tall openwork **roof combs**
  bearing giant painted stucco portraits ([Penn Museum Expedition: Temple of the Red Stela](https://www.penn.museum/sites/expedition/the-temple-of-the-red-stela/);
  [mayan.org: Yaxchilán](https://mayan.org/ruins/yaxchilan/)).
  → Visual-state rule: **grey stone = ruin state, not architecture**. Intact
  or maintained xanmeers (Hissmir; flashbacks; the buried Rosalila-layer)
  should show red/ochre stucco and crest-like roof combs; ruined ones show
  bare grey steps with stucco ghosts in sheltered corners. Cheap, legible
  storytelling contrast across ruin tiers, using recolour/decal only (no new
  art — kit selection problem).

## 5. Taphonomy: what centuries in a swamp actually do (D2 "known ruin" → D5 "swallowed ruin")

- **Unexcavated = a steep forested hill.** Collapsed vaults + slumped fill +
  litter make a soil-covered mound; only regular shape, exposed cut stones and
  looters' cuts betray it ([Texas Monthly: The Lost Pyramids](https://www.texasmonthly.com/travel/the-lost-pyramids/);
  [Nat Geo on Xultún looting](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/culture/article/140808-maya-guatemala-looter-antiquities-archaeology-science)).
- **Corbel vaults fail first**: roofs pancake into rubble choking the rooms;
  facades sheet off; perishable superstructures vanish entirely — what
  *survives* is platforms, stair cores, lower walls, and anything buried.
- **Trees are the destroyer-preserver**: at Ta Prohm (Angkor), silk-cotton and
  strangler-fig roots pry mortarless masonry apart yet also bind it — remove
  the tree and the wall falls ([Atlas Obscura / culturalheritageonline: Ta Prohm](https://culturalheritageonline.com/places/ta-prohm/)).
  The root-clenched doorway is *the* icon of a swallowed ruin — and in our
  world the roots can be **Hist or Flint Vine**, making "the swamp reclaiming
  shunatei" literal theology, and root-cavern↔xanmeer dungeon links visible on
  the surface.
- **Surfaces stain**: bare limestone in the humid tropics grows black/grey
  cyanobacterial crusts, green algae on drip lines, moss on shaded steps —
  weathered ruins read *streaked and blotched*, not uniformly grey.
- **Looters' trenches** gouge pyramid flanks toward axial tombs, let rain in
  and accelerate collapse — and sometimes stop metres short of the prize
  ([Archaeology Magazine: El Zotz royal tomb](https://archaeology.org/issues/online/features/royal-tomb-el-zotz-guatemala/)).
  → Ready-made: alternate dungeon entrances, evidence of prior expeditions
  (Imperial/Dres/treasure-hunter — Gideon's fortune-diggers are canon,
  lost-peoples.md), part-emptied caches, and unstable trench hazards.
- Suggested tiering (analogy → our ruin states): **D2 known ruin** = steps and
  plaza cleared/maintained by watcher-tribes (canon says Argonians maintain
  xanmeers as warnings), stucco ghosts, standing lower storeys, open axial
  entrance. **D3–D4** = partial mound, tree-crowned summit, root-bound doors,
  looter trenches, interiors part-choked. **D5 swallowed ruin** = forested
  mound in backswamp, entered only via sinkhole, root cavern, looters' tunnel
  or underwater drain; the "pyramid" reveal happens *inside*.

## 6. Caves, cenotes and underwater entrances

- Maya caves were **portals to Xibalba**: pilgrimage, offerings and sacrifice
  deep inside (Actun Tunichil Muknal's calcite-sealed skeletons and "kill-holed"
  pots; [Nat Geo: Belize caves](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/ancient-maya-underworld-belizean-caves)).
- **Cenotes are drowned shrines**: Chichén Itzá's Sacred Cenote — a 60 m
  sinkhole linked to the core by sacbe — received gold, jade and human
  offerings for centuries ([Met Museum: Sacred Cenote](https://www.metmuseum.org/perspectives/golden-kingdoms-sacred-cenote-chichen-itza);
  [Wikipedia: Sacred Cenote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Cenote)).
- **Pyramids are built over caves**: Teotihuacan's Pyramid of the Sun sits on
  a tunnel-and-cave "place of emergence" that likely dictated the monument's
  placement and axis ([Heyden 1975 interpretation, PDF](https://www.latinamericanstudies.org/aztecs/Teotihuacan-cave.pdf)).
- → Direct precedent for the dungeon family's **hidden water entrances** and
  for connected cave↔root↔xanmeer systems (§47): let the *cave choose the
  site* — a xanmeer stands where it does because of the sinkhole/spring/root-
  mouth beneath it, its lower levels ritually engage the water table, and the
  underwater or cave route is the *older, sacred* way in. Offering-floors of
  sunken valuables make canonical underwater loot. Sithis-the-Destroyer cult
  (Nisswo sacrifice canon, §1) maps cleanly onto the underworld-portal role —
  without importing Maya religion, the *spatial* logic transfers whole.

## 7. Open questions / canon gaps noticed (do not resolve silently — dossier or decision first)

1. **How xanmeers were built is canonically a mystery** (lost at Duskfall).
   No canon supports Hist-directed stone-shaping or magical construction
   methods. If a quest or interior wants to show the method (murals,
   Rosalila-layer paint), that's an EXTRAPOLATED design decision — record it,
   and consider leaving it deliberately unanswered (wonder budget,
   duskfall.md).
2. Interior *layout* canon is thin: mazelike + traps + Hist chamber + two
   named mechanisms is all we have. Room-function programmes (shrine, cistern,
   tomb, calendar court, sap-store?) are ours to define — worth a short
   xanmeer-interior-grammar decision when Phase 12 starts, drawing on §4–§6.
3. Canon doesn't say whether xanmeers held **burials**. Nisswo sacrifice is
   canon; Argonian soul-returns-to-sap theology may make royal tombs wrong for
   Argonians — resolve before copying the Maya axial-tomb pattern (grave-stake
   practice in material-culture.md is *modern*; pre-Duskfall death practice is
   open).
4. The **stone source** is unaddressed: quarries (→ reservoirs, §3) are a
   great causal feature but nothing in the dossiers locates Black Marsh stone.
   Ties into the ground/rock layers of the terrain plan.
5. Relationship between xanmeer causeway network and the canon stone-flagged
   causeways (Stormhold→Bogmother) — same pre-Duskfall system? Plausible,
   unconfirmed; a decision record should say so if we build the route layer
   on it.
