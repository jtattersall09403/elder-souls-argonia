# Marsh settlement morphology — real-world models for causal settlement authoring

Inspiration for **Phase 11 (causal locations and settlement authoring)** agents:
how real wetland peoples site, build and organise villages and towns, so ours
feel causally real. This is a **menu of evidence, not a prescription** — every
actual placement still goes through the causal model in
[world/40-causal-authoring.md](../../world/40-causal-authoring.md) (§28, and the
flood-cycle binding at its head), must satisfy
[quests/20-world-provisions.md](../../quests/20-world-provisions.md), and must fit
the two canon building kits in
[material-culture.md](../../../world/sources/lore/topics/material-culture.md)
(Shadowfen mud/wattle vs Murkmire reed/stilt, never blended; "villages move";
stone is a moral error post-Duskfall). Region classes and settlement tiers:
[world/20-province-design.md](../../world/20-province-design.md) §14–16; counts:
[morrowind-content-density.md](morrowind-content-density.md).

Claims below are **well-evidenced** unless flagged *(speculative)* or
*(design inference)*. Researched 2026-08-28.

## 1. Living marsh settlements (ethnographic models)

### 1.1 Iraqi Marsh Arabs (Ma'dan) — the all-reed economy

The single richest model for a Murkmire-kit tribal village.

- **Made land.** Houses stand on artificial islands: a reed fence ~6 m high
  encloses a patch of marsh, packed with reeds/rushes (**kibasha** — a raft-like
  temporary island, topped up when it sinks); capped with dredged mud it becomes
  a permanent **dibin** — and a family that leaves a dibin unoccupied for over a
  year *loses their right to it*. Natural islands are **tuhul**.
  ([Encyclopedia.com — Ma'dan](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/madan-marsh-arabs);
  [Wikipedia — Marsh Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh_Arabs))
- **The mudhif** (guest house) is the social centre: a parabolic-vaulted hall of
  bundled *Phragmites* reeds, no nails or wood, up to ~10 m tall and 30 m long,
  usually owned by the sheikh; an **odd number of pillar-arches** so the host
  sits centred; hospitality refused to no one; the form is attested on Sumerian
  artefacts from c. 3200 BC.
  ([Texas Monthly](https://www.texasmonthly.com/style/mudhif-houston-rice-university-iraqi-marsh-arabs/);
  [Ancient Origins](https://www.ancient-origins.net/history-ancient-traditions/mudhif-0016908))
- **Dwellings and layout.** Family houses are small arched reed huts (~2×6 m),
  one per nuclear family; each islet also carries an animal pen, a haystack, and
  the *only clay structure — the baking oven* (fire discipline on a flammable
  island). Villages are scattered, secluded, boat-access only; all movement by
  bitumen-coated canoe (**mashuf**). Reed structures rot in 7–10 years; owners
  cut off the rotted base and live in a slightly lower house rather than
  rebuild — architecture as a consumable.
  ([everyculture.com](https://www.everyculture.com/wc/Germany-to-Jamaica/Ma-dan-Marsh-Arabs.html))
- **Why there:** fishery, reed harvest, water-buffalo herding — and refuge: the
  marshes sheltered dissidents for centuries, which is why Saddam's regime
  drained them in the 1990s.
  ([Native Threads](https://nativethreads.co/blogs/blog/the-marshlands-of-iraq-the-vanishing-world-of-the-marsh-arabs-and-mandaeans))

*Game reading:* the mudhif is a ready-made analogue for a tree-minder's hall or
root-herald's guest house; kibasha→dibin gives a visible "age gradient" within
one village (new raft-islets at the edge, mudded old cores at the centre), and
the abandonment rule is a quest hook (*design inference*).

### 1.2 Ganvié (Benin) — the refuge town and the market on water

- ~20–30,000 Tofinu people on stilts in Lake Nokoué; founded 16th–17th c. to
  escape Fon slave raiders, whose **religious prohibition against fighting on
  water** made the shallow lake a sanctuary — the name means "we survived".
  ([Wikipedia — Ganvie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganvie);
  [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/1000658/the-floating-village-of-ganvie-a-model-for-socio-ecological-urbanism))
- ~3,000 houses on rot-resistant stilts in 1–2 m of water; **everything is a
  boat errand**: the market is vendors' pirogues rafted together; homes double
  as storefronts. Small artificial-earth islets (soil ferried from shore) hold
  the school, livestock, even a football pitch — dry land as scarce
  infrastructure. **Acadja** brush-park fish farms (branches planted on the lake
  bed whose rotting leaves bait fish) turn the town's surroundings into owned,
  tended fishery plots.
  ([Field Study of the World](https://fieldstudyoftheworld.com/ganvie-africas-largest-lake-village/);
  [visitganvie.com](https://visitganvie.com/en/histoire))

*Game reading:* the strongest model for a **refuge-founded** settlement whose
cause is political (escaped slaves, war refugees, tax evaders) rather than
economic; canon parallel: Argonians raided for slaves by Dunmer for millennia.
Owned fish-park "fields" of brush visible around a village read instantly as
agriculture without land (*design inference*).

### 1.3 Kampong Ayer (Brunei) — the water *city*

- A cluster of **40+ named villages** over the Brunei River, linked by **38 km
  of stilted boardwalks**; ~10,000 residents; over a thousand years old and the
  Bruneian Empire's *capital and primary port* in the 15th–18th c. — proof a
  stilt settlement can be a state capital, not just a fishing hamlet. Each
  district keeps its own school/mosque; wealth shows in house size and boat
  quality; fire brigades and police travel by speedboat.
  ([Culture Trip](https://theculturetrip.com/asia/brunei-darussalam/articles/welcome-to-kampong-ayer-the-worlds-largest-floating-village);
  [History Rise](https://historyrise.com/bruneis-historic-kampong-ayer-water-village-civilization/))

*Game reading:* the template for scaling boardwalk morphology to **city tier**
— a federation of named boardwalk quarters, each with its own shrine/school and
identity, matching the Blackrose island-core + boardwalk-districts template in
[40-causal-authoring.md](../../world/40-causal-authoring.md).

### 1.4 Tonlé Sap, Cambodia — designing for a 10 m water amplitude

- The Mekong flood **reverses the Tonlé Sap river**, swelling the lake ~5× in
  area and from ~1–2 m to ~10 m deep every year. Kampong Phluk's answer:
  houses on **6–10 m stilts** that are towering ladders in the dry season and
  waterline porches in the wet; genuinely **floating** platforms for markets,
  schools, fish farms; and **the temple on the one patch of high ground**, the
  village's dry gathering place at peak flood. Some villagers migrate seasonally
  — temporary dry-season houses out on the exposed lakebed, back to the stilts
  when the water returns.
  ([Travellocal](https://www.travellocal.com/en/articles/cambodia-s-waterworld);
  [Smiling Albino](https://www.smilingalbino.com/blog/life-on-the-tonle-sap-lake-stilted-and-floating-villages-of-cambodia/);
  [siemreap.net](https://www.siemreap.net/attractions/sightseeing-nature/floating-villages/))

*Game reading:* this is the direct real-world validation of our flood-cycle
binding: one settlement, **two legitimate seasonal states**, with the sacred
building deliberately sited to stay dry. The mixed strategy (stilts for homes,
floats for infrastructure, high ground for the shrine) is a checklist any
wet-season blueprint state can be audited against.

### 1.5 Uros (Lake Titicaca) — the fully floating village

- 60–70 **floating islands** built of buoyant totora-reed root blocks lashed
  together and anchored by poles and rope; 5–10 families per island; fresh reeds
  added every few weeks, whole island rebuilt in ~25 years; origin defensive
  (fleeing Inca, then Spanish, control). Islands **merge, split and move** —
  including a mass relocation shoreward after an 1986 storm.
  ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/travel/people-peru-live-manmade-islands-constructed-plants-180956218/);
  [CIB paper — Uros reed floating islands](https://www.irbnet.de/daten/iconda/CIB21650.pdf))

*Game reading:* the licence for canon's drifting Alten Meerhleel made concrete:
a floating village that maintains itself weekly, splits after feuds, and can
turn up at a city's gates. The **anchor pole** is the settlement's one fixed
asset (*design inference*).

### 1.6 Cross-cutting patterns from the living examples

- **Founding causes recur as a short list:** fishery access, reed/timber
  harvest, refuge/defence (water as moat or taboo), evasion of state/tax/slaver
  reach, trade-route position. Every one maps to a `foundingCause` entry.
- **Circulation is tiered:** boats everywhere (Ganvié, Ma'dan) → boardwalks
  once density justifies the timber (Kampong Ayer) → causeways where seasonal
  dry ground allows. Tier correlates with wealth and permanence.
- **The social centre is architecturally exceptional but same-material** (the
  mudhif is just a bigger reed hut) — status through scale and placement, not
  imported materials. Fits canon's anti-permanence doctrine.
- **Sanitation logic is positional:** waste goes downstream/down-tide; clean
  activities (drinking draw; our egg pools, causal example §29.3) sit upstream.
  Real stilt villages drew drinking water from the lake they fouled — chronic
  disease was a real cost ([ArchDaily on Ganvié](https://www.archdaily.com/1000658/the-floating-village-of-ganvie-a-model-for-socio-ecological-urbanism)).
  Canon's *dried flower buds as water purifiers* could be a village-scale prop
  at the drinking steps (*design inference*).
- **Dry land is infrastructure.** Made or found dry ground is rationed for:
  ovens/forges (fire), livestock, children, the dead, the shrine. Audit
  question for any blueprint: *what did they spend their scarce dry ground
  on?* (*design inference*)

## 2. Historical and archaeological wetland settlement

### 2.1 Terps/wierden/warften (Frisian coast) — the communal mound

- Artificial dwelling mounds of sods, clay and dung raised on salt-marsh levees
  from c. 650 BC–AD 1050, growing from single-farm house terps (30–50 m across,
  1–2 m high) into **village terps** as families joined and mounds coalesced —
  settlement growth literally recorded in layers of communal earth-moving.
  Pliny mocked the dwellers as "sailors at high tide, shipwrecked at low tide."
  ([Wikipedia — Terp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terp);
  [Ealdlar — Terps](https://ealdlar.com/history/terps);
  [PSU EARTH 107](https://courses.ems.psu.edu/earth107/node/1082))
- **Feddersen Wierde** (the fully excavated example, 1st c. BC–5th c. AD) shows
  the classic **radial plan**: longhouses arranged like spokes around a central
  open space, with a debated chiefly compound (*Herrenhof*) at one edge — the
  village raised the mound higher each time storm-flood levels rose. Primary
  reference: Haarnagel, *Die Grabung Feddersen Wierde* (1979); critique of the
  Herrenhof reading in
  [Nösler/Schmid via academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/3077098/Haarnagel_und_der_Herrenhof_der_Feddersen_Wierde_Anmerkungen_zu_einem_sozialtopographischen_Konzept);
  overview: [Wikipedia — Feddersen Wierde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feddersen_Wierde).

### 2.2 Crannogs (Scotland/Ireland) — the household island-fort

- Small artificial islands in lochs — timber piles driven into the bed, packed
  with peat, brush and stone (all-stone in the treeless Hebrides) — used from
  the Neolithic to the 17th c.; ~2,000 in Ireland alone. Access by boat or by a
  **narrow causeway often hidden just under the surface**. Function blends
  farmstead, refuge and **status display**: one household, conspicuously
  moated. The Loch Tay reconstruction needed 168 alder piles and 12 days of
  pile-driving for a 15 m platform — household-scale, not village-scale,
  labour. ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/technology/crannog);
  [Wikipedia — Crannog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crannog))
- Contrast: the UNESCO **Alpine pile dwellings** (5000–500 BC, 111 sites) were
  mostly built on marshy *shores* on piles against occasional floods — later
  lake-level rise submerged them, creating today's "drowned village" sites.
  ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1363);
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_pile_dwellings_around_the_Alps))
  *Game reading:* a natural causal story for underwater ruins that were never
  "sunken cities" — just shorelines that moved (*design inference*).

### 2.3 The English Fenland before drainage — the fen-island economy

The best-documented *medieval* wetland society, and the closest era-parallel to
our tech level.

- **Islanded institutions:** monasteries deliberately colonised the clay
  islands rising from the peat — Ely ("Eel Island"), Ramsey, Thorney, Crowland,
  Peterborough — first for solitude, then growing rich as owners of vast fens.
  ([Britannica — Fens](https://www.britannica.com/place/Fens);
  [britishheritage.com](https://britishheritage.com/travel/britains-sinking-lands-exploring-fens))
- **Rents paid in eels** — literally: a "stick" was 25 eels; Ely paid Thorney
  26,275 eels to rent a fen; Ramsey paid Peterborough 4,000 eels/yr *for access
  to building stone* — wetland protein traded for upland materials.
  ([Atlas Obscura — eel rents](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/medieval-eel-rent-map-england))
- **Common-right seasonal economy:** regulated rights (not open access) to
  summer grazing on seasonally flooded pasture, peat-digging, reed/sedge
  cutting, wildfowling, eel-trapping; branching **droveways** linked fen-edge
  villages to the pastures since Roman times; rents also paid in *ditch-digging
  labour*. Fen communities were cutting drainage ditches by c. 650.
  ([Cambridge/Oosthuizen](https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/casting-light-on-the-dark-ages-anglo-saxon-fenland-is-re-imagined);
  [Lincolnshire heritage — Roman drove](https://heritage-explorer.lincolnshire.gov.uk/Designation/DLI566))
- **Water infrastructure had names and owners:** canals (**lodes**), landing
  places (**hythes**), meres — a whole toponymic layer for us to imitate.
  ([Medieval Histories — Fenlands](https://www.medieval.eu/medieval-fenlands/))

*Game reading:* eel-rents and stone-for-eels trades are exactly the kind of
concrete economic relation `economy[]` in the causal model wants; a fen-island
temple-estate that *owns* the surrounding marsh rights is a whole faction
economy in one building (*design inference*).

### 2.4 Chinampas (Xochimilco) — farming as land-making

- Rectangular raised beds built inside woven-post fences filled with lake mud
  and vegetation, anchored by living willows, separated by canoe canals ~1.5 m
  deep and 4–6 m wide; canal dredgings renew the beds' fertility. ~10,000 ha
  fed Tenochtitlan's ~200,000 people at up to 7 crops/year.
  ([FAO GIAHS](https://www.fao.org/giahs/giahs-around-the-world/mexico-chinampas-agricultural-system/en);
  [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/Chinampas/))

### 2.5 Llanos de Mojos and Amazonia — landscape-scale wetland engineering

- Pre-Columbian societies engineered ~100,000 km² of seasonally flooded savanna:
  **raised fields, ~20,000 habitation mounds (lomas), ring-ditches, canals,
  arrow-straight causeways linking forest islands, and zigzag fish weirs** with
  basket-gaps at each V — a floodplain fishery at territorial scale. The
  Casarabe culture (AD 400–1400) tied monumental mound sites together with a
  dense causeway-canal network.
  ([Erickson — Lomas de Moxos](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/~cerickso/fishweir/articles/EricksonLomasMoxos2000.pdf);
  [Nature Sci. Rep. — pre-Columbian fishery](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-24454-4);
  [LAC Geo — Llanos de Moxos](https://lacgeo.com/llanos-moxos-archaeological-region))
- **Bluff model** (Denevan 1996): Amazonian riverine settlement concentrated on
  **bluff tops beside the floodplain**, not in it — farming the rich but risky
  várzea seasonally while living above flood reach; enriched **terra preta**
  soils mark the old bluff villages. (Recent work shows dense várzea occupation
  too — the model is contested at the edges.)
  ([Denevan 1996, *Annals AAG*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-8306.1996.tb01771.x))

*Game reading:* Mojos is the precedent for **xanmeer-era landscape works** —
ancient causeways, weirs and raised-field grids surviving as half-legible
earthworks under the marsh, matching canon's stone-flagged causeways and the
xanmeer water-management reading in causal example §29.5 (*design inference*).

### 2.6 The pre-industrial wetland engineering vocabulary

Everything above was built without machines. The full kit available to our
builders: **dwelling mounds** (terp), **reed-raft islands** (kibasha/Uros),
**mudded permanent islets** (dibin), **timber piles and stilts**, **revetments/
woven-fence retained fill** (chinampa, kibasha), **causeways** (earth or
timber; hidden/submerged for defence), **canals/lodes**, **hythes** (named
landings), **sluices and ditch networks**, **fish weirs and traps** (zigzag
earthen, wicker), **brush-park fish farms** (acadja), **raised fields**,
**living-tree anchors** (willow/mangrove — or Hist roots), **drainage
ditch-digging as rent/corvée**. Each item implies labour scale: a crannog is
one household's work, a terp or causeway is a village's, a Mojos causeway
network or xanmeer hydraulic complex is a polity's (*design inference*).

## 3. Era-parallel town morphology (the medieval small town)

For our larger, part-Imperialised settlements (Gideon, Stormhold tier).

- **Conzen's town-plan analysis** gives the descriptive vocabulary: a town =
  ground plan (streets, plots, building block-plans) + building fabric + land
  use; readable as **plan units**, each a distinct morphological phase
  (Alnwick had 14). **Burgage plots** — long narrow plots with a short street
  frontage — fill in from the street backwards over centuries (the burgage
  cycle: institutive → repletive → climax → urban fallow).
  (Conzen, *Alnwick* (1960), [IBG Publ. 27](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/240739085_Conzen_MRG_1960_Alnwick_Northumberland_A_study_in_town-plan_analysis_Institute_of_British_Geographers_Publication_27_London_George_Philip);
  [Whitehand — Conzenian urban morphology](http://spacesyntaxistanbul.itu.edu.tr/papers/invitedpapers/Jeremy_whitehand.pdf))
- **Organic vs planted:** organic towns accrete at castles, fords, monasteries
  (irregular plans, Norwich); **planted** towns are founded at a stroke with
  gridded plots and a charter (500+ French bastides, 13th-c. peak; equal plots
  around a central market square, founded to control frontier country and raise
  tax). ([ORB — planned or planted towns](https://the-orb.arlima.net/encyclop/culture/towns/townint5.html);
  [about-france.com — bastides](https://about-france.com/tourism/bastide-towns))
  *Game reading:* Argonian settlements read organic; Imperial foundations read
  planted — plan geometry itself communicates who founded a place, and a
  planted grid **sinking into the marsh** (canon: "Imperial houses built on the
  same soil sank") is a visual thesis statement (*design inference*).
- **The core cluster:** market place adjacent to church/temple at the crossing
  of through-routes — the near-universal nucleus of small towns (Alnwick's
  market square + church + castle relationship is the textbook case).
- **The edge:** water-fouling and noxious trades (tanneries, dyeing, butchery)
  pushed **downstream and downwind** by ordinance; mills strung along leats;
  **leper houses and gallows outside the gates on approach roads** (320+
  English leper houses, all extramural, sited where alms could still be begged).
  ([Historic England — leprosy](https://historicengland.org.uk/research/inclusive-heritage/disability-history/1050-1485/time-of-leprosy/);
  [INRAP — medieval tanners of Troyes](https://www.inrap.fr/en/medieval-tanners-troyes-12130))
- **Harbour grain:** quay → quay-street → warehouses/customs/inns behind, plots
  running back from the waterfront; some ports planted on grids (New
  Winchelsea), most accreted along the shore.
  ([Port Towns in the Middle Ages](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391984048_Port_Towns_in_the_Middle_Ages_1000-1500))
- **Central-place spacing, light touch:** English law (Bracton, 13th c.) spaced
  markets ≥ 6⅔ miles apart — a day's walk split into thirds (go, trade,
  return); most people lived ~4 miles from a market; neighbouring towns
  **staggered market days** so traders could circuit them. Christaller's
  hexagonal market-area theory formalises the same intuition; treat it as a
  sanity check on market-town spacing (adjusted for boat speeds), not a law.
  ([Britannica — central-place theory](https://www.britannica.com/money/central-place-theory);
  [Frodsham market charter history](https://frodsham.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/History-of-the-Frodsham-Market-Charter-and-Market-Rights.pdf))

## 4. Wetland ecology zonation as siting logic (brief)

Vegetation bands follow flood frequency and salinity, and each band is a
different larder — so the zonation fields we already compute *are* the economic
map.

- **Freshwater hydrosere:** open water → reedswamp (*Phragmites*/*Cladium*) →
  fen → carr (wet alder/willow wood) → raised bog (rain-fed, acid, poor).
  ([Encyclopedia.com — hydrosere](https://www.encyclopedia.com/earth-and-environment/ecology-and-environmentalism/environmental-studies/hydrosere);
  [Hughes & Barber 2003, *J. Ecology* — Tregaron](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1046/j.1365-2745.2003.00762.x))
- **Coastal bands:** inundation frequency/duration sets mangrove and saltmarsh
  zones along tiny elevation gradients — daily-flooded pioneer fringe up to a
  rarely-flooded upper marsh.
  ([Tidal regime, salinity and salt marsh plant zonation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0272771404002173);
  [Inundation characteristics of mangrove and saltmarsh](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0272771421004030))
- **Economic reading** (*design inference from the above + §1–2 evidence*):
  reedswamp = building material + thatch + eel/fish nurseries; fen = summer
  grazing, sedge, wildfowl; carr = the only local timber and coppice; raised
  bog = peat fuel and preserved dead, but nothing to eat — settlement-hostile,
  danger-appropriate; mangrove fringe = crabs, shellfish, rot-proof timber.
  A village's position across these bands should predict its diet, exports,
  fuel, and what it must import (grain, stone, metal) — i.e. its `economy[]`
  and `ecology[]` relations should be derivable from the hydrology/salinity/
  flood fields at its site.

## 5. Suggested settlement archetypes (a menu, NOT decisions)

Causal preconditions keyed to our hydrology fields (flood frequency FF, tidal
range TR, channel depth CD, dry-season exposure). Kits per canon: Shadowfen
mud/wattle (M), Murkmire reed/stilt (R).

| Archetype | Real model | Preconditions | Form notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reed-island tribal village** (R) | Ma'dan | Permanent shallow water, dense reedbeds, low TR | Kibasha/dibin islets, one family each; grand mudhif-hall for the tree-minder; clay oven the only masonry; boat-only |
| **Stilt/boardwalk village** (R) | Kampong Phluk, Murkmire canon | High seasonal FF amplitude, firm bed for piles | Stilt height ≥ max flood; shrine/Hist on the one high spot; floating market rafts; two seasonal blueprint states |
| **Floating cluster** (R) | Uros, canon Alten Meerhleel | Deep calm water, reed supply, anchor points | Mobile; splits/merges; anchor pole as the fixed asset; may drift to city gates |
| **Refuge water-town** (R) | Ganvié | Broad shallows an enemy can't/won't enter; founding threat in `history[]` | Dense stilts, boat-market, acadja fish parks, made-earth islets for school/livestock |
| **Mound (terp) village** (M) | Frisian terps | Salt/estuary marsh, storm surge, no high ground | Radial longhouses on communal mound; mound layers = village age; drove access at low water |
| **Household island-fort** | Crannog | Small loch/backwater, timber, a family with enemies | Single compound, hidden underwater causeway; status display; POI-scale not village-scale |
| **Fen-island temple estate** | Ely/Ramsey | Rare true island in peat fen | Institution owns surrounding marsh rights; rents in eels/labour; lodes + hythes; pilgrim/market satellite |
| **Levee/bluff river town** (M) | Denevan bluff model; §29.1 | Natural levee or bluff above várzea-like floodplain, navigable channel | Town above flood line, seasonal fields below; quay-street-warehouse grain at the hythe |
| **Terp-ringed market town** (M/Imperial) | Frisian + medieval market towns | Route crossing + CD for barges + spacing from rivals (≈ day's-travel rule) | Market square + temple nucleus; burgage-like plots; tanneries/gallows/leper-house analogues downstream and outside |
| **Drained-fringe colonial town** (Imperial) | Bastides + Fen drainage | Province edge, drainable fen, an outside power funding sluices | Planted grid, ditches and sluices — and visibly losing: subsiding plots, wet cellars (canon: Imperial houses sank) |
| **Chinampa garden fringe** | Xochimilco | Shallow fresh lake margin by a large settlement | Not a settlement itself: a productive canal-grid skirt around a town, canoe circulation |
| **Weir-and-causeway relict landscape** | Llanos de Mojos | Seasonally flooded savanna regions | Xanmeer-era earthworks half-reused by living tribes; ancient causeways as the only dry routes |

All *(design inference)*: forms are evidenced above; their mapping to Argonia is
ours to decide in Phase 11 against provisions, lore and density targets.

   with canoe travel on channels, what spacing do our boat-graph speeds imply
   for market settlements? (Needs the route/boat graph from Phase 9/10.)
