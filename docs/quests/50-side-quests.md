# Part IX — Standalone and local quests

## 44. Local-quest rules

Local quests bring the province to life and should be authored from causal world data:

```text
geography + hydrology + ecology
+ history + culture + politics
+ individual/institutional motivation
→ why the situation exists here
→ layout, inhabitants, evidence, choices and consequences
```

No local quest should consist only of collecting an arbitrary number of objects. A recovery objective is acceptable when ownership, route, danger, interpretation or disposition creates the real problem.

# Part IX-B — Mythic, artifact and Daedric quests

## 44-B. Role and rules

Daedric and artifact quests are consistently among the most-loved content in all three reference games, and they carry a disproportionate share of each game's strangeness and memorability. This section supplies worked concepts for the mythic/Daedric content family in the target table. Rules:

- each is a **standalone L- or M-tier quest**, not a chain, discovered through rumour, shrine, book or strange encounter rather than a quest board;
- Daedra in Black Marsh should feel like intrusions into a Hist-and-Sithis world, not the local religion; Argonians in these quests react from their own cosmology;
- rewards are fixed unique artifacts (existing Skyrim artifact meshes re-materialled/renamed where lore permits, or mod weapons from A04–A05);
- each preserves the wonder budget: at least one element stays unexplained.
