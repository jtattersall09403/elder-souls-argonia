# Main quest: The Eye and the Root — structure, cast, endings

> Module of the quest/narrative master plan (see [README](README.md)).

# Part IV — Main quest: _The Eye and the Root_

## 16. Story thesis

The story asks:

> **Can a people be protected by a bond they are not free to refuse — and when
> the fight over that bond is finished, what do you do with what you are
> holding?**

The second half is the ending's question. The Eye is canonically a king's
jewel; the Scalded Throne is canonically held empty (if it existed at all),
with institutions still pointing at it. By the sanctuary, the player holds the jewel, the city it
opens, and the gratitude or terror of everyone involved. The three ending
families (§24) are the three answers: take the cult, take the throne, or save
the marsh and put it all down.

Supporting questions:

- Does the state’s use of sacred institutions invalidate the institutions themselves?
- Is severance liberation when communities depend upon what is being severed?
- Who may interpret the will of the Hist?
- Can an intelligence service defend sovereignty without defining dissent as disloyalty?
- Does an outsider or legally dead prisoner become neutral, exploitable, or uniquely dangerous?
- Is a treasure important because of what it does, or because people believe it grants legitimacy?
- What does Sithis require when every faction claims change on its own terms?

## 17. Fan inspiration and transformation

The principal fan-story inspiration is Matthew Aaron Evans’s Gamesas pitch, which proposed:

- a treasure hunt for the Eye of Argonia;
- affected Hist;
- an interior Sithis cult;
- conflict involving the An-Xileel;
- Helstrom and the Lost City as the culmination.

The game should credit Evans in its community-inspiration references if this core is retained.

Additional credited fan interpretations shape the Eye:

- Valerie Marie: the political-legitimacy reading of the Eye (the king's-jewel identity itself is canon — L10/L11; her credited contribution is what that identity can be made to *do*);
- Matt Gammond: the Eye as cipher/translator/puzzle key;
- Soraya Davy: the Eye as a perceptive “third eye”;
- FirDaus LOVe farhana: the Eye as translator and diplomatic/perceptual device.

The design departs from the fan pitch in four deliberate ways: no Imperial Expeditionary Force in the main quest, Helstrom separated from the Lost City, “rogue trees” recast as deliberate finite acts of ritual interference, and every consequence constrained to feasible static-world implementation.

## 18. Stakes

### How the stakes read to a brand-new player (binding presentation rule)

Assume the player knows no Elder Scrolls lore beyond, at most, having played
Morrowind (see 60-writing-and-lore §45d — nothing is assumed). The stakes must
therefore land on the surface, in the first two hours, in one sentence a
stranger could repeat:

> **A death cult is murdering the tree-gods of the marsh. When a tree dies, the
> village's unborn die in the egg that night, and the souls of its dead lose
> their way home. Nobody can stop them, because the throne that once commanded
> the marsh has been empty for centuries.**

Kill the gods, kill the children, unhome the dead — death and enslavement of a
whole people, felt in the opening quest (the withdrawn Hist and the dead clutch
in MQ01, the Owing gang the player is processed into). That is the Morrowind
model: blight storms and cursed dreams first, "Dagoth Ur has a point" much
later. **The nuance below is the truth the player discovers; it is never the
pitch.**

### The truth underneath

The Unbound Root has tested methods for making selected Hist withdraw, fall silent or reject established intermediaries. Its final ritual would act at the Root of Accord in the Lost City.

If the cult succeeds:

- several influential Hist communities lose established communication and ritual continuity;
- egg, adoption, burial and identity practices become uncertain;
- An-Xileel authority suffers a legitimacy crisis;
- some Lukiul and rootless residents experience genuine liberation from coercive classification;
- other communities experience the act as spiritual mutilation;
- violence and opportunism follow;
- the Unbound Root gains ideological momentum but cannot control all consequences.

If the Veiled Reed obtains exclusive control:

- the immediate attack is prevented;
- warning, records and coordination remain;
- state surveillance and classification become harder to challenge;
- dissenting communities may be pressured into continued participation.

The hard-to-achieve reform outcome preserves the living accord while removing state-only controls and restoring periodic local consent. It requires evidence, allies and optional work across the questline.

### 18b. The cult's method is canon, and it is an object

*(Lore pass 2026-08-25. Previously the Unbinding was an unspecified project-original
ritual; canon already supplies the exact tool, which costs nothing and raises the
stakes to something the player can hold, steal, break or hand over.)*

The **Mnemic Egg** is canon: when a Hist dies its death "births it backwards" into
a seed of pulsing sap holding all that tree's and its people's memories, and
because the Hist share one mind an Egg is a gateway into the whole collective —
and, in the wrong hands, **a tool for severing the Argonian–Hist connection**. The
Aldmeri Dominion used one in 2E 582 to stop a generation of eggs hatching (lore:
topics/hist-and-sap.md).

Consequences, all of them improvements:

- **The Unbinding needs an Egg**, and Eggs come only from dead Hist. The cult's
  method is therefore inseparable from the deliberate withdrawals of MQ04 and
  MQ15 — the "tests" are not experiments in the abstract, they are **harvest
  attempts**, which is far worse and far clearer.
- **Act III gains a physical object to fight over** instead of an abstract rite.
  MQ21's laboratory is where they have been trying to force an Egg from sap and
  volunteers because they cannot bring themselves to kill a Hist outright — yet.
- **The precedent is Altmeri**, which makes the Veiled Reed's paranoia about
  foreign hands *justified* rather than merely institutional. The Thalmor have no
  presence in the province and every reason to be interested at arm's length
  (lore: extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md §5). The possibility that the
  Unbound Root's method reached them from outside is raised in Act III, is
  genuinely unresolved, and would **appal the cult**, who would be the last to
  know. Do not confirm it.
- The Root of Accord remains the target; the Egg is how the severance is
  *delivered*. Ending families are unchanged.

### 18c. The Reed's oldest secret is not about the cult

*(Lore pass 2026-08-25; canon-supplied, previously used only in the Sunken Archive
line where it is worth far less.)*

Canon: **Hierem**, the Imperial minister who made the secret 4E 47 trip to Black
Marsh and sealed the deal at the Lilmoth city tree with a ritual involving the
word "Umbriel", was a member of the **Synod** and "reportedly held vast influence
over it" (lore: topics/guilds-and-orders.md §2).

The Veiled Reed's most heavily classified file is therefore not about the Unbound
Root at all. It is a hundred-and-fifty-year-old file about a foreign minister and
a foreign magical order, and Holds-the-Reed keeps it classified because it is the
only document in the building that justifies the existence of his office. Surfaced
during the MQ20 evidence work; usable in the MQ25 council; and it is the single
best answer available when the player asks the Director *why any of this needs to
be secret.*

### 18d. The endgame is visible from the first hour

*(Owner directive 2026-08-26: the player must see the shape of the end early,
the way the Imperial-invasion alternative would have shown its general in scene
one. Four devices, all cheap:)*

1. **The final antagonist leads the opening attack in person.**
   Cuts-the-Old-Knot is at MQ01's raid — distinctive, unhurried, seen taking
   the courier's satchel — and she looks at the player and decides they are not
   worth killing: to her, they are already dead. The whole game is the road
   back to the person from the first hour.
2. **The dreams.** From the night of the attack the player receives brief,
   recurring sap-dreams — a circle of trees, a door that has not been built
   yet, and a voice they will much later recognise as hers. Fog-dressed reuse
   of existing interiors, text-led; our version of Morrowind's Dagoth Ur
   dreams, and the wonder budget's main-quest entry.
3. **The empty throne is a place.** Helstrom keeps the Hall of the Scalded
   Throne — an old seat, centuries empty, that may never have been filled,
   still swept, still argued about (canon: "empty for centuries, if it existed
   at all"; the Hall itself is project interpretation — canon supplies the
   contested throne, not a room for it; see 10-political-frame). The MQ05 arrival walks the player past it. Whoever the player
   chooses to become at the end is standing in that room from Act I.
4. **The lethal centre is on the horizon.** The D5 basin beyond Helstrom's
   gates, discussed and visible long before the player can survive it —
   already in the plan; kept.

## 20. Branch-state model

Use a small, inspectable set of variables:

```ts
interface MainQuestPoliticalState {
  veiledReedTrust: number;      // 0–100
  unboundRootTrust: number;     // 0–100
  coverIntegrity: number;       // 0–100
  publicKnowledge: number;      // 0–100
  rootTalkStanding: number;     // 0–100 — the tribes' reward track (§24b.3)
  independentLeverage: number;  // 0–100 — what you kept, not what you were given (§24b.4)
  trackLockedAtTierFour: "none" | "reed" | "cult" | "rootTalk" | "independent";
  eyeCustody: "player" | "reed" | "cult" | "hidden";
  accordEvidence: Set<string>;
  affectedHistOutcomes: Record<string, string>;
  expeditionSupport: Set<string>;
}
```

Do not fork the world into separate campaigns. Roughly 75–85% of locations and stages remain shared.

**Custody handoff — how the Eye is present at MQ23 and MQ29 under each `eyeCustody` value:**

| Value | At MQ23 / MQ29 |
|---|---|
| `player` | Direct: the player carries and uses the Eye. |
| `reed` / `cult` | The holding faction's expedition carries it and grants the player supervised use at each mark and at the door — the player is indispensable as the only one who can read it. |
| `hidden` | The player retrieves it from the cache they chose before the route stage begins. |

In every case the Eye reaches the sites where it is required; custody changes who else is standing there, never whether the reading can happen (§23's cannot-be-lost rule).

### Practical paths

1. **Veiled Reed loyalist** — honest reports, cult sabotage, state expedition.
2. **Loyal infiltrator/reformer** — remains formally loyal while gathering evidence and building safeguards.
3. **Unbound Root double agent** — falsifies reports, preserves cult assets and enables the Unbinding.
4. **Independent witness** — protects the Eye and plays both organisations against one another.
5. **Failed double agent** — cover collapses; progress continues through force, Nisswo help or documentary routes with reduced options.

The player is never forced to select “join cult” from a conspicuous menu. Loyalty is expressed through what they report, preserve, destroy, reveal and whom they save.

## 21b. Act II is the plan's biggest boredom risk — how it is handled

Regional leads with the same shape (arrive in city, find document, learn a
thing) are the structural failure the whole treasure hunt is exposed to, however
good each quest is individually. Four mitigations, in force (lead count reduced
to four authored + two optional side investigations, 2026-08-26):

1. **One verb each, and no repeats.** MQ09 burglary/bargain, MQ10 dive, MQ13
   urban infiltration under residents' eyes, MQ14 climb and night-sail; the
   optional side investigations keep their own verbs (MQ11 walk the unmapped
   road, MQ12 dive-and-hearing). A lead may not be re-briefed with a verb
   another lead already owns.
2. **One lead costs something.** At least one of the four can only be taken by
   giving the Collector something real, burning a relationship, or accepting an
   Owing on somebody else's behalf. The player should finish Act II having *paid*,
   not merely collected.
3. **One lead is wrong.** One fragment is a forgery — a good one, planted early —
   detectable only by cross-referencing against a different lead, an optional
   side investigation, or Sings-Over-Stone's archive. Taking only the minimum
   three can therefore mean triangulating from a lie, which does not block MQ15
   but sends the player to a wrong first mark in MQ23 and costs them a real
   journey. Over-collection is rewarded with something better than completion.
4. **The cult is racing, and losing the race has a cost the player sees.** Already
   in the plan via the Collector, the MQ13 reversal and the MQ15 second
   withdrawal; under §18b those withdrawals are now **harvest attempts for a
   Mnemic Egg**, so falling behind is not a missed lead, it is a dead tree.

**Binding open-world rule:** every race/urgency consequence — Collector
appearances, lens-frame loss, the second withdrawal — fires on quest-stage
transitions only, never on elapsed play time.

## 22. Pacing

- **Prologue and Act I (MQ01–MQ05):** processing and the attack — the villain seen, the clutch dead, the dreams begun; recruitment; first Hist case; early Helstrom and the empty throne.
- **Act II (MQ06–MQ16):** treasure hunt across outer cities, four leads, the Collector shadowing, the second withdrawal. The player is encouraged to build a cover through local and faction work.
- **Act III (MQ18–MQ22):** possession of the Eye, infiltration, moral exposure of both sides, the handler purged, the loyalty pivot filed to a woman who cannot be lied to.
- **Act IV (MQ23–MQ26):** route revelation and the Shadowscale manhunt, the council under attack, the Accord twist.
- **Act V (MQ27, MQ29, MQ31–MQ32):** expedition and crossing, Lost City with the Last Warden, the sanctuary confrontation, the choice, the epilogue.

The full crisis is foreshadowed early, while urgency becomes immediate only after the Eye and cult plan are understood. This protects open-world pacing.

## 23. Critical NPC and fail-forward policy

**All NPCs are killable by the player — no invincibility anywhere** (owner
decision 2026-08-28). Killing an NPC flagged essential to the main quest or to
a faction line shows a doom-warning message in the Morrowind tradition, with
our own spin:

> **With this death, a root the story grew along is severed. The Hist will no
> longer dream this tale to its ending. Restore a saved moment to rejoin the
> dream, or persist in the doomed world you have made.**

Each main role needs:

- primary NPC;
- successor or documentary fallback;
- altered dialogue/outcomes if primary dies;
- stable body/corpse/document location if death is meaningful;
- death always possible — the fallback, never an invincibility flag, is what protects the line;
- explicit quest-debug indication of which fallback is active.

This section's fallbacks also govern deaths caused by **other quest lines**: a
faction or side quest that can kill a shared principal maps to the same declared
fallback here — and faction lines never *require* a principal's death.

The Eye cannot be permanently lost. If dropped, stolen or placed in faction custody, the runtime must preserve a recoverable reference or transfer its state through a documented handoff.

# Part V — Ending architecture

## 24. Ending families — three, named by what the player takes

*(Rewritten 2026-08-26, owner directive. The old five families — Guarded, Open,
Hidden, Unbinding, Pruned — were institutionally framed ("custody",
"accords") and read as dull from the player's seat. The endings are now framed
by the player's own intent, with the old families' machinery surviving inside
them as variants. The question the game has been asking since the empty throne
room in Act I: you hold the jewel, the city, and the outcome — what do you take?)*

### 24.1 CUT — The Overlord of the Last Door *(cult victory)*

The player enables the Unbinding. The Root of Accord is severed; several Hist
fall silent; rites, eggs and identities fracture across the affected
communities — and the movement that did it is suddenly the most feared power in
the marsh, holding the only door to the sanctuary.

- **The overlord variant (default presentation):** the double-agent path ends
  with the player *taking the movement* — Cuts-the-Old-Knot dead in the
  ritual's cost, defeated, or bound to the player by everything the player
  knows — and ruling what the severance made. The epilogue is momentum, fear,
  and the first arguments about what the Unbound Root is *for* now that it has
  won.
- **The believer variant (hidden):** complete the severance and refuse to rule
  it — walk out of the sanctuary and let the doctrine loose without a head.
- This is a fully supported ending, not a joke "evil choice." It requires real
  double-agent work across Acts III–IV and carries serious, played aftermath.

### 24.2 CLAIM — The Scalded Throne *(the player takes power)*

The cult is defeated — and the player does not hand anything back. Canon left
this door open on purpose: the Eye is **a king's jewel**; the Scalded Throne
has been **empty for centuries (if it existed at all)**; and what survives of
the kingship by 4E 201 — living memory of the royal court's execution writs,
and the common oath **"By the Throne of Aphicles"** — still points at the
vacancy. The bearer who
walks out of the Lost City holding the jewel, commanding its door, credited
with saving (or sparing) the Hist, can **press the claim**.

- The claim must be *built*, not clicked: legitimacy evidence across the game
  (the Eye's king's-jewel account from MQ06, Root Talk standing, saved
  witnesses, expedition debts) determines who backs it.
- **The sub-choice — whose king are you?** The coalition that carries the
  claim shapes the reign, and each is personified by people the player has
  known all game: the **state apparatus** (a Reed reformed under the player —
  Spills-The-Ink's or Never-Writes-Twice's institution now answers to you),
  the **Root Talk and the tribes** (Kaska-Meen's and Ei-Tuja's world, a
  crowned servant of the trees), or the **open water and the cities**
  (the ports, the pilots, the ordinary traffic — Walks-Against-Current's
  people). Every option is sympathetic; every option is somebody else's
  defeat.
- The epilogue is **genuinely triumphant with genuine unease** — a coronation
  at Helstrom in the hall the player first walked in Act I, offices bending or
  fracturing, letters of fealty and letters of refusal. It is never undercut
  into a joke; canon's doubt that the throne ever existed is allowed exactly
  one dry line, from Sings-Over-Stone.

### 24.3 MEND — The Marsh Keeps Its Own *(the selfless ending)*

The cult is defeated, the harm is mended as far as it can be, and the player
hands the levers to others and walks away — the Morrowind ending, and the
hardest to do well.

- **Mending** draws on the old reform machinery: consent restored where the
  Accord had been made compulsory, the concealed state controls disabled,
  affected communities reconciled where the player's Act II–IV work allows.
- **The sub-choice — whom do you trust with it?** The same three sympathetic
  sides as 24.2, now as *custodians* rather than coalitions: reformed state
  custody, the plural Root Talk arrangement, or **sealing the sanctuary beyond
  anyone's use** (the old Hidden Eye). Each argued for by people the player
  cares about; none of them safe.
- **The Kept Key (hidden dark variant, unchanged):** publicly walk away,
  privately keep the route records or the Eye. Same public epilogue, plus a
  private thread of leverage, blackmail risk and ominous pursuit.

### 24.4 Rules across all three

- The three families are **player intents**, reachable through the existing
  practical paths (§20); no conspicuous "pick your ending" menu — the
  sanctuary scene offers only what the player's accumulated state has enabled.
- Every family requires the MQ31 confrontation resolved first (speech or
  duel), so the ending is always *earned over a named opponent*.
- The player never becomes supreme leader of anything **automatically** — the
  Overlord and the Throne are built from evidence, trust and debts, quest by
  quest, consistent with the frozen leadership rule.
- Sub-choices must present **sides the player can genuinely sympathise with**:
  each is voiced by a principal the player has spent the game alongside, with
  a real case and a real cost, never a strawman.

## 24b. Allegiance reward tracks — what each side pays you

*(Owner directive 2026-08-26. Research: [../research/quests-and-cast/tes-quest-and-faction-rewards.md](../research/quests-and-cast/tes-quest-and-faction-rewards.md).
These are **not new factions or characters** — they are the four sides the main
quest already contains. What is new is that each pays, escalating, so the
player has skin in the game and is choosing with their build as well as their
conscience.)*

### The four tracks and their DNA

| Side | Standing variable | Reward DNA — the one-line version |
|---|---|---|
| **The Unbound Root** (→ CUT) | `unboundRootTrust` | **Power with a price.** Raw personal strength taken from the tree, paid for in what it costs you |
| **The Veiled Reed** (your employer) | `veiledReedTrust` | **Access, information and leverage.** The state never makes you stronger; it makes doors open and tells you where things are |

Five binding rules, from the research and the frozen decisions:

1. **Fixed and announced** (decision 0004). Every reward is tied to a named
   quest or standing threshold, nothing scales to level, and the ladder is
   legible early — that legibility is what makes it motivating.
2. **Stack low, lock high** (owner 2026-08-26). Tiers 1–3 of all four tracks
   are freely stackable; **tiers 4–5 are mutually exclusive across tracks** and
   must be telegraphed before commitment (§29 incompatibility rule pattern).
   Curiosity is rewarded; commitment still costs something.
3. **Build coverage.** Across the four tracks every character build gets a
   meaningful ladder — heavy combat, light/stealth, speech/personality, magic
   and non-combat exploration are each served by at least two tracks.
4. **Locks are acts, not thresholds** (owner 2026-08-28). Every tier-4 lock is
   an explicit, telegraphed **opt-in act**, never a passively crossed
   threshold: cult — the deep rite; Reed — a formal commissioning oath sworn
   before the Director; Root Talk — accepting a village's adoption rite;
   Yourself — declaring the claim aloud before a named witness. Standing and
   evidence only make the act *available*; only performing the act locks. And
   locks gate tiers 4–5 rewards and who backs the player at the endgame —
   they **never** constrain which sanctuary resolution or ending family the
   player may choose at MQ31.
5. **Variable ownership.** The §20 variables are written by main-quest stages
   only. Faction and local content maintains its own reputation, which the
   main quest may read — never the reverse (40-factions §30b).

### 24b.1 The Unbound Root — power with a price

Canon does the work: sap **increases combat prowess**; Amber Plasm made a tribe
"grow faster and stronger" at the cost of madness; sap-poisoning is a real,
described condition (lore: topics/hist-and-sap.md).

The costing model is **Morrowind's corprus, exactly** (owner decision
2026-08-26): each rite **raises Strength and Endurance and drains Willpower and
Personality**, deepening per tier. That is *pure stat modifiers* — no bespoke
art, no new system, and it is the reason the Sixth House material is beloved.
Visible physical marking (gold tongue, bark scale) stays **flavour text and

### 24b.5 The stronghold — one base, three phases, four flavours

Owner decision 2026-08-26: **one** player base, not four. A single reoccupied
site (a xanmeer or an abandoned river station — Phase 11/12 reserves it) built
in **three phases** on the Morrowind Great House model: a contract, a claim, a
construction, a check on progress, then two more phases that add rooms,
services and population.

Which track is highest at each phase **re-skins** it — cult sanctum, Reed
safehouse, adopted village hall, or your own seat — changing who staffs it,
what services appear and how it is dressed. Authoring is **compositional, not
combinatorial**: **3 additive phase layers plus 4 allegiance overlay sets**
(7 authored pieces, not 12 authored states), and the skin re-evaluates **only
at phase checkpoints**, never mid-phase. One build, four presentations, every
player gets one. Services scale with phase: storage, a bed and a crafting
station at phase 1; a merchant, a trainer and a healer by phase 3; the
track-specific service (agents, informants, guides, retainers) at phase 3 only.

### 24b.6 Feasibility

Everything above is items with enchantments, stat modifiers, flags that open
doors, dialogue conditions, price modifiers, fast-travel node unlocks, map
markers, an income timer, and one base with three state variants — **all D-A or
D-B**. The two least-common pieces are the informant map-marker grant and the
off-screen service request; both are data, not behaviour.

**This is a demand on workstream S** (stats design, module 76): the corprus-style
trade-offs, the enchanted-gear ladders and the ability grants all need the stat
scale to exist and to be able to express them. Registered in module 76 §103.1's
completeness sweep.

## 25. Low-complexity implementation

All endings reuse:

- the same Lost City;
- the same Last Warden encounter;
- the same sanctuary;
- the same Eye and Root assets;
- the same Helstrom council location;
- the same main cast where alive.

Differences consist of:

- which 2–4 actors reach the sanctuary, and which named principal opposes;
- the MQ31 duel or speech resolution;
- ritual VFX/material toggles;
- local sanctuary state (severed / claimed / mended / sealed);
- selected Hist audio/particle/NPC variants;
- faction-office schedules and banners;
- the Helstrom throne-hall state (empty / coronation / memorial — the memorial state belongs to the MEND family: the hall kept as a memorial to what was mended or refused);
- letters, rumours and epilogue text.

Three families with declared variants replace the previous five families —
fewer validated branches, and the variants inside a family share that family's
epilogue skeleton.

## 26. Persistent regional consequences

Each ending chooses only a bounded set from:

- opening Hist settlement status;
- Helstrom Veiled Reed leadership;
- the Hall of the Scalded Throne's state;
- Helstrom council composition;
- one Lilmoth/urban response;
- one northern response;
- one western civic response;
- access to the Lost City after completion;
- selected Hist silence/reconciliation presentation;
- surviving main characters and their roles.

The epilogue may describe broader change beyond the playable state.
