# Cast — characters, relationships and the province-wide roster

> Module of the quest/narrative master plan (see [README](README.md)).
> **Read this before writing any named NPC.** It holds the character-design
> rules, the naming system, the principal cast, the recurring cast of every
> faction line, the cross-line faces and the oddities roster.
> Dialogue *style* (Jel register, topics, staged scenes) lives in
> [60-writing-and-lore.md](60-writing-and-lore.md); this module is about *who*.

---

## 51. Why this module exists

The plan was strong on institutions and thin on people. The audit that produced
this module (2026-08-25) found four specific failures, and every rule below
exists to close one of them:

1. **Faction lines named almost nobody.** Across ~90 faction quests the player
   met "a dying contract-killer", "a respected labour broker", "a scholar", "a
   Tree-Minder" — a province of strangers. Recurring relationships are where an
   RPG cast becomes memorable; the plan had two (Nesh-Deeka, the Collector).
2. **Name-form monotony.** Seven of nine principals shared one construction
   (Verb-the-Noun) and three used the same reed/root/water imagery. Canon offers
   at least five distinct Argonian name forms, and *which form a character
   carries is political information* — the cheapest characterisation available.
3. **Register monotony.** Nobody in the cast was young, funny, physically
   distinctive, non-Argonian, or wrong in a way the player could sympathise
   with. The wonder budget covered strange *places* and no strange *people*.
4. **No cast-level view.** Characters were designed one quest at a time, so
   nothing prevented two lines from independently producing the same person.

Research anchors: the "one distinctive thing, one clear want, doing something"
formula and the von Restorff 2–3 vivid traits rule; the archetype-plus-
contradiction fix for stereotype; the rival-versus-foil distinction (a rival
*opposes*, a foil *exposes*); the ensemble-slot diagnostic that two characters
in the same archetypal slot means one gets quietly written out; and the budget
rule — *if an NPC can be replaced by a door, a sign or a die roll, they do not
need a backstory*. Sources are listed in
[99-sources-credits.md](99-sources-credits.md).

## 52. Depth tiers — effort proportionate to role

Depth is rationed the same way production cost is (the S/M/L tiers in
[00-overview.md](00-overview.md) §4). **A character gets backstory in proportion
to the player's chances of discovering it.**

| Tier | Who | Required | Forbidden |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1 — principal** (≈12) | Main-quest cast; the player knows them for the whole game | Full sheet: want, contradiction, quirk, private life, voice rules, an opinion about the player, a fail-forward path, an on-screen change between Act I and Act V | Being summarisable by their job title |
| **C2 — recurring** (≈40) | Faction patrons/peers/nemeses; cross-line faces. Seen in ≥3 quests | Want, contradiction, one quirk, one relationship type, one lore anchor, fail-forward | Being interchangeable with another C2 in the same line |
| **C3 — named local** (≈150) | Quest-givers and subjects of single M/L quests | One want, one vivid trait, one specific detail of place or trade. **No backstory the quest doesn't surface.** | Delivering lore they have no reason to know |
| **C4 — texture** (hundreds) | S-tier quest-givers, shopkeepers, guards, villagers | One line of specificity from the material-culture kit (§59). Nothing more | Any authored history at all |

**Discovery rule.** Every C1 and C2 character must have at least one thing about
them the player can find but is never told — a document, a habit, a room, an
object, a person who knew them. Depth the player cannot reach is depth we did
not need to write.

## 53. The six rules

### 1. Contradiction is not the same as quirk — every C1/C2 needs both

- The **contradiction** is a conflict inside the character's *want*. It creates
  drama and drives branch potential. ("Believes records expose power; alters
  records to protect people.")
- The **quirk** is a surface behaviour that makes them findable in memory. It
  should be *cheap* — a habit, a possession, a speech tic, an animal, a craft.

A character with only a contradiction is a position paper. A character with only
a quirk is a mascot. This plan's default failure is the first.

### 2. The one-quirk rule

**One quirk per character, and it must do a job.** A quirk earns its place if it
(a) is a scene the player can walk into, (b) reuses an existing prop, animation
or system, and (c) tells you something true about the person. Ei-Tuja sculpting
mud he will then kick apart *is his theology*. A character with three quirks is a
sketch show.

### 3. The cliché budget

Familiar shapes are allowed and sometimes wanted — the province needs a
swashbuckler, a scheming councillor, a hardbitten spymaster. The budget:

- **at most one per faction line**, and it must be flagged in that line's cast
  table as the deliberate one;
- **every cliché carries one subversion that changes how it plays**, not a
  cosmetic tag. The pirate captain who runs her crew as a cooperative *and reads
  out the minutes* is a different quest to write. A pirate with an eyepatch is
  not;
- **no cliché in a C1 role.** The principal cast may contain no stock types at
  all.

### 4. Somebody has to be right, and it must not always be the sympathetic one

The plan's politics only work if antagonists are competent and allies are
mistaken. Required across the cast:

- at least **three C1/C2 characters whose position is correct and whose manner
  makes it hard to accept** (Never-Writes-Twice is the model);
- at least **three whose position is wrong and whose reasons are honourable**;
- **no character who is wrong about everything**, including cult leadership;
- one character per major line who is **right about their institution and wrong
  about the player**.

### 5. Relationship variety — the anti-sameiness tool

Every C2 carries exactly one **relationship type**. A faction line must contain
**at least four distinct types** among its recurring cast, and no line may be
Patron + Peer + Nemesis only.

| Type | The player's experience |
|---|---|
| **Patron** | Employs you; you want their approval |
| **Peer** | Climbs beside you, at your rank, with their own ambitions |
| **Mentor (wrong)** | Teaches you something real and is mistaken about something else |
| **Nemesis** | Opposes you competently and is not evil |
| **Dependent** | Needs you and cannot repay you |
| **Friend** | Likes you, wants nothing, is not useful |
| **Ex** | A relationship already damaged before the player arrived |
| **Ward-turned-judge** | Someone whose fate you shaped, who later assesses you |
| **Institution-in-a-person** | Cannot be separated from their office; their office is dying |
| **Oddity** | Makes no sense, is fine, and is never explained |

*Rival vs foil*: a rival **opposes** what the character wants; a foil **exposes**
what the character is. Our best C2s are both — Never-Writes-Twice blocks the
player *and* is the honest version of Nesh-Deeka.

### 6. Recurring faces are the point

- Every major faction line has **3–4 C2s present from its first quest to its
  last**, and their standing changes with the player's conduct.
- Every **region packet** must reuse at least two C2s from elsewhere. A face in
  two cities is worth more than two faces in one.
- **At least one C2 per line must be able to die, leave or turn**, with a
  fail-forward path per [30-main-quest.md](30-main-quest.md) §23.
- No character may be introduced in the quest where they die.

## 54. Naming — five canon forms, and what each one says

The single cheapest cast-diversity lever, and it encodes politics for free.
Canon anchors: Jel has no past or future tense and thrives on idiom; names are
given at **Chukka-Sei** maturity trials by the *naheesh*, who hold the naming
secrets; **lukiul** is the An-Xileel's slur for the assimilated; nicknames are
attested throughout (lore: topics/material-culture.md; topics/hist-and-sap.md).

| Form | Canon examples | Who carries it | What it signals |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jel** | Nesh-Deeka, Heita-Meen, Na-Kesh, Tsono-Xuhil, Keshu, Nurwul, Nuetepa | Interior, tribal, marsh-born | Rooted. Untranslated on purpose |
| **Translated Tamrielic** | Deep-In-His-Cups, Only-He-Stands-There, Derkeethus | City Argonians, outward-facing offices, anyone who deals with *ojel* | A working name for foreigners; often literal, often faintly comic, sometimes given *at* you |
| **Nickname / epithet** | Red Bramman, One-Eye, Drakeeh the Unchained | Criminals, sailors, soldiers, the notorious | Earned by an event; the person may not like it |
| **Chosen name** | — (`EXTRAPOLATED` from Chukka-Sei practice) | Lukiul returning, freed debtors, converts, the reinvented | A claim. Somebody's records still hold the old one |
| **Foreign-inflected** | Nibenese, Dunmeris, Ta'agra forms | Gideon Lukiul, Thorn/Stormhold Dunmer, Soulrest Khajiit, Archon sailors | Two centuries without an Empire; a family that kept its name kept something else too |

**Binding style rules**

1. **No more than one third of any cast list may use the Verb-the-Noun
   translated form**, and no two characters in the same quest may share it.
2. **Do not reuse the same imagery.** Reed, root, water, stone and shadow are
   each allowed **once** in the principal cast; the validator flags collisions.
3. **A character's name form must match their politics**, or the mismatch must
   be deliberate and discoverable (Holds-the-Reed's translated name is a chosen
   one; the Gideon registry still holds what he was entered as).
4. Non-Argonian names follow their own province's conventions, and **Argonia-born
   foreigners drift**: a third-generation Stormhold Dunmer speaks better Jel than
   Dunmeris and knows it.

## 55. The principal cast (C1)

Supersedes the summary table in [30-main-quest.md](30-main-quest.md) §19. Every
entry: **want · contradiction · quirk · lore anchor · fail-forward**.

### The Veiled Reed

**Nesh-Deeka** — field handler. *Jel name. Alten Corimont, mid-40s.*
The game's Caius Cosades: the one personal relationship in an institutional
cast, and the player's feelings about the Reed will largely *be* their feelings
about her.
- **Want**: to be the person who noticed. She joined from a harbour clerkship
  because she found a discrepancy and could not leave it alone.
- **Contradiction**: believes records expose power, and alters records to protect
  people — without seeing that this is her Director's crime at a smaller scale.
- **Quirk**: she reconciles other people's ledgers, unasked, for free, in taverns,
  because it is how she thinks. She keeps three feral **Corimont Mouth-Plovers**
  (canon: fang-cleaning birds kept by *high-status* Argonians) which is a joke
  the city makes about her and which she enjoys.
- **Against the cliché**: a spy handler who is bad at lying to your face and says
  so — *"I extend the claw of welcome. I am also not telling you two things."*
  Her honesty about withholding is itself a technique she learned from her
  Director, and the player should eventually work that out.
- **Lore**: Alten Corimont's principled neutrality with a price list; the state's
  working offices at Stormhold.
- **Fail-forward**: her case files and her flat survive her; the ledgers are
  legible.

**Holds-the-Reed** — Director of the Veiled Reed. *Chosen translated name.
Gideon-born, elderly, frail, personally kind.*
- **Want**: to finish proving something that cannot be finished.
- **Contradiction**: he is **lukiul** — hatched outside Black Marsh, unable to
  hear the Hist — and he directs the intelligence service of the nativist
  successor state whose predecessor coined the slur for people like him. Fifty
  years of loyalty tests he set himself. He classifies dissenters because he has
  spent his life being classified.
- **Quirk**: he asks agents to describe what a Hist *sounded* like, and listens
  the way a man listens to a letter from home. He has never pretended to hear one.
- **Discoverable**: the Gideon civic registry still holds the name he was entered
  under. Finding it is optional and changes nothing officially; it changes every
  conversation afterwards.
- **Lore**: lukiul as An-Xileel coinage; Gideon's Lukiul majority killed by the
  state's own weapon in 4E 48; Hist-less Argonians cannot hear the Hist at all.
- **Fail-forward**: a successor Archwarden; his private file survives either way.

**Never-Writes-Twice** — Reed hardliner, the player's institutional rival.
*Translated name; it means she never revises a report.* **New C1** (the plan had
an unnamed "Reed hardliner" in MQ25 only; she now runs MQ03→MQ31).
- **Want**: an honest service.
- **Contradiction, and the point of her**: she is the only officer in the Reed who
  files truthful reports, and this makes her the player's most dangerous enemy.
  She correctly identifies the player as an uncontrolled security risk — because
  they are. Nesh-Deeka is sympathetic *because* she lies. The player should find
  that uncomfortable and should never be given a clean way out of it.
- **Quirk**: she reads her own reports aloud once before filing them, to hear
  whether they sound like something she would say under oath.
- **Relationship**: Nemesis and foil. **Never write her as corrupt.**
- **Fail-forward**: if killed or disgraced she is replaced by someone worse and
  less scrupulous, and at least one NPC says so.

**Spills-The-Ink** — Reed records clerk, early 20s. *Translated name given as a
joke by an office he cannot leave; it stuck three years ago.* **New C1.**
- **Want**: to be sent somewhere.
- **The arc**: he idolises the player and is poor at fieldwork. **He changes to
  match the player's conduct**, with no choice menu anywhere: model deceit and he
  becomes an efficient, hollow operator; model restraint and he is the
  institutional inheritor the reform ending needs. He is the branch-state model's
  strongest single use, and he is the only character who visibly *ages* across the
  game.
- **Quirk**: he practises Nesh-Deeka's turns of phrase, badly, in front of the
  player, who is the only person who will not tell him to stop.
- **Fail-forward**: if he dies, the epilogue's reform variant loses its named
  inheritor and gains a document instead — which is, deliberately, worse.

### Helstrom and the Hist

**Ei-Tuja, "the Third Answer"** — Nisswo interpreter. *Jel name.*
(Renamed from *Speaks-in-Reeds*: reed imagery collision, per §54.)
- **Want**: for the plural tradition to survive a century that wants answers.
- **Contradiction**: he preaches a different sermon in every city and means all of
  them; he also enjoys the influence ambiguity gives him, and knows it.
- **Quirk**: he sculpts in mud and sand while he talks and destroys it at the end
  — canon Nisswo practice (making art and then unmaking it). Every important
  conversation with him happens over something being built that will not survive
  the scene. One prop, one swap, enormous character.
- **Register**: the cast's comic voice. He is genuinely funny and his jokes are
  load-bearing theology. He is also the one who names the Unbound Root's error in
  Argonian terms — that severance is *shunatei*, holding on to an injury so
  tightly you would destroy the thing that caused it. That speech is his.
- **Fail-forward**: three of his students carry different halves of his position,
  which is exactly what he would have wanted and he would say so.

**Kaska-Meen** — Helstrom Tree-Minder. *Jel name. Early 30s — the youngest senior
figure in the province.* (Renamed from *Moss-Beneath-Stars*, per §54.)
- **Want**: to not be the reason something ancient ends on her watch.
- **Contradiction**: she inherited the post early when her predecessor died, is
  demonstrably excellent at it, is resented for her age, and overcompensates by
  being immovable. She will override individuals for the collective and is
  sometimes right to.
- **Quirk**: she keeps her predecessor's **grave-stake** (canon *xul-vaat*, carved
  with the dead one's entire life) in her own house and reads it when she cannot
  decide — consulting a dead woman's biography as an oracle.
- **Lore**: tree-minder as default arbitrator; grave-stakes; the Helstrom assembly.
- **Fail-forward**: the stake survives her and can be read by anyone.

**Sings-Over-Stone** — Helstrom archivist. *Translated name. Very old, physically
enormous, substantially deaf, a **jekka-wat** (canon: an Argonian who tracks time
at all).*
- **Want**: to be left alone with the dates.
- **Contradiction**: he is the world's leading authority on the Eye of Argonia and
  believes the entire treasure hunt is a category error — the Eye's importance is
  a fact about people, not about the gem. He is largely correct and it does not
  help anybody.
- **Quirk**: he can date any document by its idiom in three seconds and cannot
  stop correcting people, including in the middle of other people's emergencies.
  His deafness plus his pedantry is the archive's comic register and is
  *mechanically load-bearing*: he is how the player disambiguates the six
  contradictory Eye accounts.
- **Fail-forward**: his catalogue is complete and legible; his marginalia are
  better than the catalogue.

### The Unbound Root

**Opens-the-Last-Door / "the Collector"** — recruiter and philosopher.
*Translated name; the Collector is a chosen alias.* Former Miredancer
**sap-speaker**.
- **Want**: for no one else to give what he gave.
- **The body is the argument** (`CANON_EXPLICIT` mechanism): he spent years among
  the roots on his tribe's behalf and came out with canon **sap-poisoning** — gold
  tongue (permanent golden mouth pigmentation), bark scale (thickened darkened
  scales), unwanted hallucinations. Then the community he did it for set him
  aside. He is physically marked by devotion and was discarded for it. His
  doctrine is *earned*, not argued, and it costs one material variant.
- **The disguise works because of it**: as the Collector he keeps his mouth
  covered and speaks little. **The unmasking at MQ18 is the moment he smiles.**
- **Quirk against the cliché**: he is a compulsive gift-giver. Across Act II he
  leaves the player small, well-chosen, genuinely useful presents. He recruits by
  kindness.
- **The rule that makes him frightening**: *he never lies to the player, not once,
  in the entire game.* Every terrible thing he says is true.
- **Discoverable**: he has already carved **his own grave-stake** and keeps it. A
  man who has decided in advance how he will be remembered. Never remark on it.
- **Counterweight**: **Miril-Tei** (§56, Many-Root) is the sap-speaker who did the
  same thing and *was* honoured. They know each other. Neither will discuss it.
- **Fail-forward**: his writings and his gifts survive him; the gifts are worse to
  keep afterwards.

**Cuts-the-Old-Knot** — leader of the Unbound Root; former Veiled Reed officer.
*Translated name.*
- **Want**: to undo one specific thing she built.
- **Contradiction, sharpened**: she did not suffer the classification apparatus —
  **she designed it**. The sap-dream testimony system the player exposes in MQ20
  is hers, in her handwriting, and she watched what it became. "I know exactly
  what it can do. I know because I specified it." Victim and architect are the two
  routes to the same doctrine, and she and Opens-the-Last-Door are one of each:
  the movement's public face and its brain are different people, which is why the
  Reed keeps arresting the wrong one.
- **Quirk against the cliché**: she is a *terrible* speaker — visibly
  uncomfortable, dry, self-deprecating, exhausted — and delegates all preaching.
  A cult leader with no charisma is a far better cult leader.
- **Fail-forward**: her specification documents survive and are, on their own,
  enough to convict the Reed. The plan's "absent architect whose writings survive"
  branch is now literal.

### Independents

**Walks-Against-Current** — pilot and Waykeeper contact. *Translated name.*
The one relationship in the game with no institution behind it, and the cast's
**warm** register: he likes the player and wants nothing.
- **Quirk**: he plays the **vossa-satl** (canon frog-pipes — a valved clam-shell
  with a live frog in each chamber; the happier and better-fed the frogs, the
  better the music). His frogs are named, grossly over-fed and appalling. He talks
  to them. He grieves when one dies. It is funny for twenty hours and then it is
  not, because he is a candidate casualty of MQ27–MQ28 and the player will have
  learned the sound.
- **Contradiction**: values route freedom over every ideology, and has therefore
  carried state prisoners *and* cult fugitives, and will not apologise for either.
- **Fail-forward**: his route notes and his instrument. Someone else can play it
  badly.

**Ahnjazzi** — Khajiit marine underwriter. *Ta'agra name. Soulrest, Archon,
Lilmoth.* **New C1** — the plan's only non-Argonian principal, and the fix for a
main cast with no outsider in it. Promoted from the unnamed "Khajiit broker" of
MQ10.
- **The job**: she prices risk. In the most dangerous province in Tamriel, she
  will tell you your odds and charge you for the telling. She is therefore a
  **diegetic danger-tier oracle** — §49's requirement that D4/D5 warnings come
  from people, wearing a hat.
- **Against the cliché**: not a smuggler and not a haggler. Scrupulously,
  tediously honest, because in her trade honesty is the competitive advantage.
- **Contradiction**: her cargo is legal and the crews she must hire are not — a
  live thread into the Owing (lore: topics/labour-and-bondage.md), indentured dock
  crews and the Reed-Sail line.
- **Quirk**: she has already calculated the player's survival odds and updates the
  figure aloud, unprompted, every time they meet. It is meant kindly.
- **Fail-forward**: her books; another underwriter with worse manners.

**The Last Warden** — the Wamasu guardian of the sanctuary.
Not a character with a want; a **condition of the place**. Bound to the Accord's
consent conditions, not to any modern faction. Canon: wamasu electrify the water
around them. Its one characterful act is that it will not attack a bearer who
has understood the consent records — it *waits*, which is far more frightening.

### The Sunken Archive

**Premise correction** (lore pass): canon says magic in Argonia is *folk
literacy, not a profession*, and every magical institution here was
foreign-founded and none took root. The Archive's membership must therefore be
tree-minders, alchemists, grave-singers and *jekka-wats* with one or two
foreign-trained scholars — and its internal fight is **whether to become an
institution at all**.

## 59. Texture NPCs at near-zero cost (C4)

Morrowind's density came from thousands of people with one line each. The trick
is **specificity of trade and place**, not backstory. Every C4 gets exactly one
detail, drawn from the canon material-culture kit (lore:
topics/material-culture.md), and no more:

- an occupation that only exists here — bone-worker, chime-maker, egg-tender,
  frog-catcher (who selects frogs by tone), depth-stick seller, slug-shaper,
  scale-groomer, *pahnjees* renderer, grave-stake carver, mouth-plover keeper;
- a food opinion — trodh must be eaten alive; Hissmir Fish-Eye Rye taken with the
  nostrils plugged; whether Gideon's cooking-for-foreigners is hospitality or
  shame;
- a game or a team — Teeba-Hatsei, Teeba-Enoo (whose rules keep changing and
  confuse even Argonians), Shells and Stones, Archon's Jaw Beetle Fight;
- a grooming or fashion detail — copper horn caps, silver frill clips (a lukiul
  fashion, and therefore a statement), Thornmarsh's Four-Spike Wattle, Gideon
  teenagers' multi-spiked crests;
- a month — twelve canon months each with a social character, so *when* the
  player meets them changes what they are doing;
- one **Jel register** habit per [60-writing-and-lore.md](60-writing-and-lore.md)
  §45b: the emotional qualifier ("I erect the spine of…"), "Stay moist.",
  "Hist piss!", "Host of Stormhold."

**Forbidden in C4**: opinions about the main quest, exposition about the Hist or
the An-Xileel, and any history the player cannot verify. A C4 who explains the
world is a C3 we did not budget for.

## 60. Cast audit and validation

Checks added to [80-technical-architecture.md](80-technical-architecture.md) §63:

1. **Depth tier declared** on every named NPC record.
2. **No duplicate archetype slots** inside a faction line (relationship type +
   institutional role + register must not both collide).
3. **Relationship spread**: ≥4 distinct types per major line, ≥3 per compact line.
4. **Name-form spread**: ≤⅓ Verb-the-Noun per cast list; no repeated core image
   (reed/root/water/stone/shadow) within a list; every non-Argonian name matches
   its province's conventions.
5. **Recurrence**: every major line declares ≥3 C2s appearing in ≥3 of its
   quests; every region packet reuses ≥2 C2s from elsewhere.
6. **Fail-forward**: every C1 and C2 has a successor or documentary path.
7. **Cliché budget**: ≤1 flagged cliché per line, 0 in C1, and each flagged one
   declares its subversion.
8. **Register spread** per line: not all grave, not all warm.
9. **Right-and-unpleasant**: ≥3 cast-wide characters flagged `correct-but-hard`
   and ≥3 flagged `wrong-but-honourable`.
10. **C4 discipline**: texture NPCs carry no authored history and no main-quest
    opinions.
