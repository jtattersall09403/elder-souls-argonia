# Cast — the character rules

> Module of the quest/narrative master plan (see [README](README.md)).
> **Read this before writing any named NPC.** It holds the character-design
> rules: depth tiers, the six character rules, the naming system, the texture
> kit and the validation checks. The rosters themselves — principal cast,
> per-faction desks, shared places, oddities — live in
> [36-cast-roster.md](36-cast-roster.md), **lookup-only**: consult the
> lines/places your brief touches. Dialogue *style* lives in
> [text/culture-registers.md](../text/culture-registers.md) (the layered race +
> upbringing + region + faction model) and
> [60-writing-and-lore.md](60-writing-and-lore.md) (topics, staged scenes);
> this module is about *who*.

## 51. Why this module exists

The plan was strong on institutions and thin on people. The audit that produced
this module (2026-08-25) found four specific failures, and every rule below
exists to close one of them:

1. **Faction lines named almost nobody.** Across ~90 faction quests the player
   met "a dying contract-killer", "a respected labour broker", "a scholar", "a
   Tree-Minder" — a province of strangers. Recurring relationships are where an
   RPG cast becomes memorable; the plan had two (Nesh-Deeka, the Collector).
2. **Name-form monotony.** Seven of nine principals shared one construction
   (Verb-the-Noun) and three used the same reed/root/water imagery. Canon offers
   at least five distinct Argonian name forms, and *which form a character
   carries is political information* — the cheapest characterisation available.
3. **Register monotony.** Nobody in the cast was young, funny, physically
   distinctive, non-Argonian, or wrong in a way the player could sympathise
   with. The wonder budget covered strange *places* and no strange *people*.
4. **No cast-level view.** Characters were designed one quest at a time, so
   nothing prevented two lines from independently producing the same person.

Research anchors: the "one distinctive thing, one clear want, doing something"
formula and the von Restorff 2–3 vivid traits rule; the archetype-plus-
contradiction fix for stereotype; and the budget rule — *if an NPC can be
replaced by a door, a sign or a die roll, they do not need a backstory*.
Sources are listed in [99-sources-credits.md](99-sources-credits.md).

**The structural model is Morrowind's, mined from UESP** (owner directive
2026-08-26; evidence with citations in
[../research/quests-and-cast/morrowind-cast-structure.md](../research/quests-and-cast/morrowind-cast-structure.md)).
The five inferred principles, which §53 turns into rules:

1. **Desks, not companions.** Every Morrowind faction is 3–7 quest-givers, each
   fixed at one hall in one town with their own quest list (Eydis Fire-Eye:
   nine quests from one desk in Balmora). Recurrence is the player returning,
   never an NPC travelling.
2. **The line's story is the disagreement between its desks.** Sjoring vs
   Percius, Sarethi vs Venim, the Telvanni councilors vs each other — each desk
   has a distinct ethic the player samples by working it, and the finale forces
   the player to resolve the disagreement.
3. **Nothing friendly recurs across lines.** What crosses lines is the *same
   conflict seen from both sides* (the Fighters/Thieves war, waged from either
   guild), *shared places* (Balmora holds five factions' desks), and one
   unjoinable **shadow network** met from every angle (the Camonna Tong: backs
   the corrupt guildmaster, mortal enemy of the thieves, controls Hlaalu
   councilors through Orvas Dren, sympathises with the main quest's enemy).
4. **The main quest is a relay**: a vivid stationary handler → single-use
   informants → institutions with their own casts → summit figures. And the
   handler is *removed at the midpoint* (Caius, recalled to the Imperial City),
   forcing independence exactly when the player can bear it.
5. **Depth is budgeted by rank and act; single-use characters get a handle,
   not a history.** The strange deep characters are late-game; early desks are
   vivid but simple; the huge named cast of targets and victims runs on
   memorable one-line handles (Tongue-Toad, the Mad Lord of Milk, Sugar-Lips
   Habasi) — the nickname *is* the characterisation.

## 52. Depth tiers — effort proportionate to role

Depth is rationed the same way production cost is (the S/M/L tiers in
[00-overview.md](00-overview.md) §4). **A character gets backstory in proportion
to the player's chances of discovering it.**

| Tier | Who | Required | Forbidden |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1 — principal** (≈12) | Main-quest cast; the player knows them for the whole game | Full sheet: want, contradiction, quirk, private life, voice rules, an opinion about the player, a fail-forward path, an on-screen change between Act I and Act V | Being summarisable by their job title |
| **C2 — recurring** (≈40) | Faction desks and recurring line cast; fixed shared-place figures. Seen in ≥3 quests | Want, contradiction, one quirk, a **fixed post**, an ethic legible from their quest list, one lore anchor, fail-forward | Being interchangeable with another C2 in the same line; travelling between lines |
| **C3 — named local** (≈150) | Quest-givers and subjects of single M/L quests | One want, one vivid trait, one specific detail of place or trade. **No backstory the quest doesn't surface.** | Delivering lore they have no reason to know |
| **C4 — texture** (hundreds) | S-tier quest-givers, shopkeepers, guards, villagers | One line of specificity from the material-culture kit (§59). Nothing more | Any authored history at all |

**Discovery rule.** Every C1 and C2 character must have at least one thing about
them the player can find but is never told — a document, a habit, a room, an
object, a person who knew them. Depth the player cannot reach is depth we did
not need to write.

## 53. The six rules

### 1. Contradiction is not the same as quirk — every C1/C2 needs both

- The **contradiction** is a conflict inside the character's *want*. It creates
  drama and drives branch potential. ("Believes records expose power; alters
  records to protect people.")
- The **quirk** is a surface behaviour that makes them findable in memory. It
  should be *cheap* — a habit, a possession, a speech tic, an animal, a craft.

A character with only a contradiction is a position paper. A character with only
a quirk is a mascot. This plan's default failure is the first.

### 2. The one-quirk rule

**One quirk per character, and it must do a job.** A quirk earns its place if it
(a) is a scene the player can walk into, (b) reuses an existing prop, animation
or system, and (c) tells you something true about the person. Ei-Tuja sculpting
mud he will then kick apart *is his theology*. A character with three quirks is a
sketch show.

### 3. The cliché budget

Familiar shapes are allowed and sometimes wanted — the province needs a
swashbuckler, a scheming councillor, a hardbitten spymaster. The budget:

- **at most one per faction line**, and it must be flagged in that line's cast
  table as the deliberate one;
- **every cliché carries one subversion that changes how it plays**, not a
  cosmetic tag. The pirate captain who runs her crew as a cooperative *and reads
  out the minutes* is a different quest to write. A pirate with an eyepatch is
  not;
- **no cliché in a C1 role.** The principal cast may contain no stock types at
  all.

### 4. Somebody has to be right, and it must not always be the sympathetic one

The plan's politics only work if antagonists are competent and allies are
mistaken. Required across the cast (the counts are strong defaults, the
principle is not):

- at least **three C1/C2 characters whose position is correct and whose manner
  makes it hard to accept** (Never-Writes-Twice is the model);
- at least **three whose position is wrong and whose reasons are honourable**;
- **no character who is wrong about everything**, including cult leadership;
- one character per major line who is **right about their institution and wrong
  about the player**.

### 5. The faction shape: desks, ethics, one live argument

This is Morrowind's actual anti-sameiness mechanism, and it replaces the
earlier relationship-type quota (2026-08-26; evidence in
[../research/quests-and-cast/morrowind-cast-structure.md](../research/quests-and-cast/morrowind-cast-structure.md)).
Every major faction line has:

- **3–5 stationary patrons ("desks")**, each fixed at one post in one
  settlement, distributed across at least two settlements where the faction
  spans them. A desk never travels; the player returns to it. Recurrence is
  five-plus visits to the same person in the same room.
- **A distinct ethic per desk.** Working different desks is how the player
  learns the institution's moral range *before* any choice is asked of them —
  one patron's jobs are honest, another's are technically legitimate and
  quietly rotten. No two desks in a line may be interchangeable in a blind read
  of their quest lists.
- **One live argument between two named desks** — the line's story *is* this
  disagreement. It must be audible from the player's first rank (each patron
  has an opinion of the other on day one), it must force **at least one
  mid-line moment where the player must pick a desk** and the unpicked desk
  reacts, and the **finale resolves the argument** — usually with the player as
  the instrument.
- **A cast that comes and goes around the desks**: targets, victims, witnesses
  and contacts are single-use, per rule 6.
- **At least one desk that can die, leave or turn**, with a fail-forward path
  per [30-main-quest.md](30-main-quest.md) §23. No character may be introduced
  in the quest where they die.

### 6. Nothing friendly recurs across lines

There are **no travelling recurring faces**. Cross-line texture comes from
exactly three Morrowind-proven sources, and region packets should use them
instead of importing characters:

- **Shared places.** A fixed figure sits where several lines happen to go
  (Balmora holds five factions' desks). Ahnjazzi never leaves Soulrest harbour;
  the main quest, the Reed-Sail line and the heists all *come to her*.
- **The same conflict from both sides.** When two lines collide, they collide
  over the *same named people and the same stakes*, seen from opposite ends —
  our Fighters/Thieves-war analogues, declared per line in
  [36-cast-roster.md](36-cast-roster.md) §56.
- **Shadow networks** (36-cast-roster §57): one or two unjoinable organisations with named
  members in real places, met from a different angle by every line that
  touches them, with one spider the player hears about long before meeting.

Single-use side characters (rule 5's come-and-go cast, and every C3) get **a
handle, not a history**: one memorable name or nickname, one want, one vivid
detail. The nickname is the characterisation.

## 54. Naming — five canon forms, and what each one says

The single cheapest cast-diversity lever, and it encodes politics for free.
Canon anchors: Jel has no past or future tense and thrives on idiom; names are
given at **Chukka-Sei** maturity trials by the *naheesh*, who hold the naming
secrets; **lukiul** is the An-Xileel's slur for the assimilated; nicknames are
attested throughout (lore: topics/material-culture.md; topics/hist-and-sap.md).

| Form | Canon examples | Who carries it | What it signals |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jel** | Nesh-Deeka, Heita-Meen, Na-Kesh, Tsono-Xuhil, Keshu, Nurwul, Nuetepa | Interior, tribal, marsh-born | Rooted. Untranslated on purpose |
| **Translated Tamrielic** | Deep-In-His-Cups, Only-He-Stands-There, Derkeethus | City Argonians, outward-facing offices, anyone who deals with *ojel* | A working name for foreigners; often literal, often faintly comic, sometimes given *at* you |
| **Nickname / epithet** | Red Bramman, One-Eye, Drakeeh the Unchained | Criminals, sailors, soldiers, the notorious | Earned by an event; the person may not like it |
| **Chosen name** | — (`EXTRAPOLATED` from Chukka-Sei practice) | Lukiul returning, freed debtors, converts, the reinvented | A claim. Somebody's records still hold the old one |
| **Foreign-inflected** | Nibenese, Dunmeris, Ta'agra forms | Gideon Lukiul, Thorn/Stormhold Dunmer, Soulrest Khajiit, Archon sailors | Two centuries without an Empire; a family that kept its name kept something else too |

**Style rules** (strong defaults — depart with a recorded one-line reason)

1. **No more than one third of any cast list may use the Verb-the-Noun
   translated form**, and no two characters in the same quest may share it.
2. **Do not reuse the same imagery.** Reed, root, water, stone and shadow are
   each allowed **once** in the principal cast; the validator flags collisions
   as warnings.
3. **A character's name form must match their politics**, or the mismatch must
   be deliberate and discoverable (Holds-the-Reed's translated name is a chosen
   one; the Gideon registry still holds what he was entered as).
4. Non-Argonian names follow their own province's conventions, and **Argonia-born
   foreigners drift**: a third-generation Stormhold Dunmer speaks better Jel than
   Dunmeris and knows it.

## 58. The oddities — the wonder budget, for people

[00-overview.md](00-overview.md) §4 requires one purely strange, non-political
encounter per region. That rule covered places; the oddities are the people,
and the roster of instances lives in [36-cast-roster.md](36-cast-roster.md)
§58.

**Rules**: an oddity has **no faction stakes** and gives **no quest reward that
matters**. At least one per region. At least two must be genuinely funny. None may
be explained by any NPC, including in the epilogue.

## 59. Texture NPCs at near-zero cost (C4)

Morrowind's density came from thousands of people with one line each. The trick
is **specificity of trade and place**, not backstory. Every C4 gets exactly one
detail, drawn from the canon material-culture kit (lore:
topics/material-culture.md), and no more:

- an occupation that only exists here — bone-worker, chime-maker, egg-tender,
  frog-catcher (who selects frogs by tone), depth-stick seller, slug-shaper,
  scale-groomer, *pahnjees* renderer, grave-stake carver, mouth-plover keeper;
- a food opinion — trodh must be eaten alive; Hissmir Fish-Eye Rye taken with the
  nostrils plugged; whether Gideon's cooking-for-foreigners is hospitality or
  shame;
- a game or a team — Teeba-Hatsei, Teeba-Enoo (whose rules keep changing and
  confuse even Argonians), Shells and Stones, Archon's Jaw Beetle Fight;
- a grooming or fashion detail — copper horn caps, silver frill clips (a lukiul
  fashion, and therefore a statement), Thornmarsh's Four-Spike Wattle, Gideon
  teenagers' multi-spiked crests;
- a month — twelve canon months each with a social character, so *when* the
  player meets them changes what they are doing;
- one race marker per [text/culture-registers.md](../text/culture-registers.md)
  §1 — for the ~72% who are Argonian that is the **Jel register** habit
  ([60-writing-and-lore.md](60-writing-and-lore.md) §45b): the emotional qualifier ("I erect the spine of…"), "Stay moist.",
  "Hist piss!", "Host of Stormhold."
  A C4 of another race takes their own row instead — the non-Argonian share of
  a settlement's texture tier should match its demographic mix
  ([world/92 §82](../world/92-demographics.md)), which is how a Gideon crowd
  and a Helstrom crowd sound different before anyone says anything;

**Forbidden in C4**: opinions about the main quest, exposition about the Hist or
the An-Xileel, and any history the player cannot verify. A C4 who explains the
world is a C3 we did not budget for.

## 60. Cast audit and validation

Checks added to [80-technical-architecture.md](80-technical-architecture.md)
§63, which also marks each check's rule strength (README's hard-rule /
strong-default / target vocabulary) — checks 4 and 9 below are strong
defaults, not hard rules:

1. **Depth tier declared** on every named NPC record.
2. **The faction shape** (§53.5): every major line declares 3–5 desks with
   fixed posts, distributed across ≥2 settlements where the faction spans
   them (compact lines: ≥3 desks or a declared canon exception such as
   itinerant Nisswo); a named argument between two desks; a mid-line
   pick-a-desk moment; and a finale stage that resolves the argument.
3. **Distinct desks**: no two desks in a line interchangeable — their quest
   lists must read as different ethics, and their registers must differ.
4. **Name-form spread**: ≤⅓ Verb-the-Noun per cast list; no repeated core image
   (reed/root/water/stone/shadow) within a list; every non-Argonian name matches
   its province's conventions.
5. **Nothing travels**: every C2 record declares a fixed post (or a declared
   canon-itinerant exception scoped to its own line); no character appears in
   another line's quest table (scoped to C2 — the C1 protected registry in
   [36-cast-roster.md](36-cast-roster.md) §55 is the declared exception list);
   region packets cite cross-line texture from 36-cast-roster §57's three
   sources (shared place, declared collision, shadow network) and never by
   importing another line's cast.
6. **Fail-forward**: every C1 and C2 has a successor or documentary path.
7. **Cliché budget**: ≤1 flagged cliché per line, 0 in C1, and each flagged one
   declares its subversion.
8. **Register spread** per line: not all grave, not all warm.
9. **Right-and-unpleasant**: ≥3 cast-wide characters flagged `correct-but-hard`
   and ≥3 flagged `wrong-but-honourable`.
10. **C4 discipline**: texture NPCs carry no authored history and no main-quest
    opinions.
