## What was wrong and what changed

- **Riverwalk's prose and quests.** The record said "a coast lane between Stormhold and Alten Corimont"; no such lane exists and both cities are inland. The cause was that nothing checked a record against the world, the quests or the build: the only gate compared a size band. Now there is a record-coherence step in the place skill: a tool builds one reader packet per place (record, routes within 3 km, neighbours, its quests, lore dossier names, the compiled build), readers flag issues across five dimensions, one agent decides ONE change set across records, quests and the build, then one text review. A gate fails on any named place or route that does not exist or does not relate to the place, on a "between A and B" with no route, on a quest premise naming a feature the place does not have, on door counts that differ from the build, and on one-way relations. It was red on all three places; it is green on all three after one change set. Riverwalk is now the cove halt at the mouth of the pilgrim water channel below Hissmir, under a kilometre from Thorn; its quests now have Thorn and Hissmir claiming the toll, and "the channel silted" became the cove silting overnight. Claywater and Greenspring had smaller fixes (door counts, two-way relations, a toll on the Blackwood road). Five neighbouring records were edited to match. The 2D map popup reads the same record.
- **WebGPU.** One cause: buildings far away get merged into one distant mesh, and that merge unpacked normals into a 3-byte layout WebGPU rejects. One rejected pipeline voids the whole frame, so every building and tree vanished whenever a far merge came into view and returned when it left. That was the loop, the 6 fps and the low GPU-memory reading (nothing was drawing). Fixed in the merge on both branches; the kits were never at fault. The headless boot check now boots every place and spins the camera for 60 s, failing on any GPU error; the three places pass with zero errors. The two remaining console lines are library deprecation warnings (fiber, rapier) queued for their upgrades. GPU memory: a scene that draws holds roughly 0.6 to 0.8 GB at default quality on the M2; 412 MB of that is uncompressed ground rasters, which is the real GPU-memory budget item and is now a backlog row.
- **Chairs.** Yaw was hand-set with no rule. A seat-facing check now reads the chair mesh's own forward axis and requires a seat near a wall to face away from it toward open ground, and a chair at a table to face the table. It failed on seven chairs (three at Riverwalk) and all are fixed.
- **Idle sockets.** They are "idle spots": places where a villager will later stand, sit, sleep, cook or work. Each label now says what the spot is for and which building it belongs to (for example "idle · sit · crews-house"). They exist only in the studio's sockets overlay and are invisible in the game. A check now requires each spot to stand at the thing it names (a seat, a fire, the water, a bed or a workbench); two were wrong and are fixed.
- **Signs.** Skyrim hangs shop and inn boards on a wall bracket beside the door and uses signposts with arms at junctions; we had the junction signposts but no building boards. A rule now requires a board at every inn, shop, smith or stable parcel, mounted by the sign's own mined wall pair (or, for the two Argonian boards, by a vanilla twin whose beam and hook geometry match within a centimetre). Claywater's station-house has a general-goods board; the Greenspring lodge and the Riverwalk crews' house have the wordless Argonian sun board.
- **Dressing review and road dressing.** A second review of yard and scene dressing against Skyrim and the mods listed what we still lack, each with its asset and pool; it is now a reference in the place skill and new checklist rows. Road dressing between places (low walls kept or tumbled, roadside rocks, milestones, cairns, shrines, signposts, varying along a route by its condition) is written up as a research doc with the rule set, and is now part of the type-10 slice (road structures) so it is proved on one leg before rollout.
- **Performance.** The water, rain and overlay passes reused the frame's matrices instead of walking the scene four times; settlement draws no longer update matrices; a soft glow (bloom) now sits on flames, lanterns and candles indoors and out, tuned so nothing away from the lights changes; `?quality=high` exists for comparison (4096 shadow map, 1.25x pixels). GPU time of the glow shows on the HUD.
- **Wind.** The two-sine sway is replaced by a hierarchical wind: trunks bend by height squared, branches sway, leaves flutter near you, and gust fronts roll downwind as a drifting noise field driven by the weather's wind speed and gustiness. Same cost class (vertex shader only).
- **Ways of working.** The speed review found the review loop had gone blind (fix rounds never reached the build ledger), long-context agents cost 41% of the spend, leads did the work themselves, and look-ups were still common. Eight changes are in as hooks and rules (context cap at 150/180 turns, leads only plan, batched look-ups, wait loops refused, one close per batch, fix rounds measured, weekly-usage pacing). One needs a line pasted by you (below).

## Your calls, answered

- **Phase 14 redundant?** Partly right. Its "deployment" never meant Pages: it means the production chunk format, dependency-aware streaming, worker offload, device-class budgets, and the budget lock before rollout. The performance techniques routed there are being done as we go (four this round), and I propose that stays the rule: a standing performance lane, each item taken when it is the cheapest win, and Phase 14 shrinks to the budget lock and the chunk format at the end (it must follow Phase 13 and 12b because they consume the budget it locks). If you agree I will rewrite the phase that way.
- **3D clouds**: feasible cheaply as lit sprite-cluster cumulus (a dozen to thirty clouds of 8 to 16 sprites, low altitude and on peaks, off at overcast), works on WebGL2 too, estimated under 1 ms on the phone. Full volumetrics are out (36 to 53 fps on an iPhone 13 alone). Not built yet; the puff texture is a sourcing job. Say yes and it is a lane.
- **Extras at 60 fps**: soft glow yes (built), colour grade possible but changes the look (left out), foliage edge smoothing needs MSAA on the scene target (not on today), crease shading (ambient occlusion) only on the M2; for the phone the right answer is baked vertex ambient occlusion in the kit build plus a ground blob under buildings. Research doc: docs/research/rendering/cheap-sky-and-post-effects-for-target-devices.md.
- **GPU machine**: keeping this machine and renting RunPod GPUs by the hour adds about $20 to 25 a month; an AWS GPU server costs $200 to 460 a month at 8 to 12 h/day; only a Mac runs Metal (your own Mac free, a rented Mac mini $110 to 180 a month). Doc: docs/research/infrastructure/gpu-dev-machine-cost-analysis.md. No change made.
- **Speed review**: docs/research/phase16/method-review-r6.md. Two things for you: paste this line into PreToolUse in `.claude/settings.json` so the context cap fires: `{ "matcher": "*", "hooks": [{ "type": "command", "command": "python3 \"$CLAUDE_PROJECT_DIR\"/tooling/repo-standards/agent_guard.py", "timeout": 10 }] }`; and set the real weekly usage limit in `tooling/repo-standards/weekly_limit.json` (`limitUnits`, then `enforce: true`), since the gate only nudges until then.
