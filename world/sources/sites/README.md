The dossiers committed here cover the nine settlement anchors at 500 m. They
are cheap (≈ 5 s each) — generate one for any ground you are about to reason
about rather than guessing, and **cite it in the siting record** (module 40
§28). The tools live in `tooling/world-generation/worldgen/`:
`site_fields.py` (the shared loader), `site_dossier.py`, `terrain_scour.py`.

## What these are, and are not

The scour is **supply**, not a plan: a landform is a candidate, not a
commitment, and the sweep deliberately over-supplies. Part 1 derives demand;
Part 3 (`worldgen.macro_plot`) matches the two, adding a seeded lattice of
plain ground (firm / shallow marsh / channel bank, plus a roadside strand
along every route) for records that want no particular landform. The scoring
weights at the top of `macro_plot.py` are the plot's siting grammar in one
place: if a family keeps landing wrong, change the weight and re-run — never
hand-move a dot. The dossier **reports** the land; it does not judge it.

IDs in `candidate-sites.json` (`site.scour.<region>.<landform>-<n>`) are
registered in `tooling/repo-standards/id-registry.json` and checked by
`npm test` (engineering standard 2). Re-running the scour with the same seed
reproduces them; changing a detector's thresholds will renumber them, so treat
a scour ID as stable only once a catalogue record has adopted the site.

## What the province turned out to be like

Read these before assuming a landform is plentiful — they are properties of
our terrain, not of the detectors:

- **Long sightlines barely exist below the mountains.** Nothing 12 m tall is
  visible from Lilmoth at 1.4 km; the marsh is flat but gently rolling, and the
  median site sees ~18 % of its 1.2 km surroundings. Landmarks will have to be
  *tall* or *close* to work as landmarks.
- **Enclosed clearings and sinkholes are rare** (single figures and low tens).
  The interior relief is too gentle for natural rooms, and the hydrology pass
  fills closed depressions by design (priority-flood). Both must be *authored*
  where a design wants them; they cannot be found.
- **Border mountains hold a quarter of all candidates.** The interesting-ground
  supply is strongly biased to the rim, which is exactly where the fewest
  people live. Expect Part 3 to have to *make* interest in the interior —
  water, vegetation and built form, not landform.
- Region classes are not water: the region raster calls some dry upland ground
  "lake & standing water". The water compiler's surface raster is the
  authority on where water actually is (see `terrain_scour.build_ctx`).
