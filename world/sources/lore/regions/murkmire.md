## Geography — CANON_EXPLICIT

- Coastal region of southern Black Marsh with access to **Oliis Bay**; **Lilmoth**
  at the bay's mouth where it meets the Southern Sea.
- North of Lilmoth's gate: **rainforest** leading inland toward Bright-Throat
  Village and eventually the **Keel-Sakka River**.
- **Deepmire**: a largely inaccessible plateau in the north-northeast corner —
  uninhabited, "dangerous and cursed", presence of Sithis said to be strong.
  Argonians call it **"the Refuge"**: it is not to be visited except to shelter
  in times of need, and is tended by a caretaker few. Swamp-leviathan skeletons
  and several pre-Duskfall xanmeers lie in it.
- Coastal edge: "the land gives way and fades into the water"; legend says
  Murkmire once extended further south and **was swallowed by the Southern Sea**.
  → canon licence for extensive **underwater ruins off the south coast**.
- A **wall of mangroves** about ten miles from Lilmoth, "nigh-impenetrable … like
  thousands of giant spiders with their legs interlocked" — folklore says they
  *were* giant spiders, transformed by the Hist's wrath.
- **Norg-Tzel** ("Forbidden Place"), a cursed island off the coast SE of Lilmoth,
  holds a xanmeer that imprisoned the **Golden Skull of Beela-Kaar** (an ancient
  Argonian sorcerer who turned necromancer; his power sealed into his gilded
  skull; taken by Cyrodilic Collections in 2E 582).
- Rare fish of Oliis Bay: Fat Sleeper, Longlure Eelfin, Spotted Seatrout;
  **sea-drakes** (crocodilian, paddle-legged) in the deep water off Lilmoth.

## Politics — CANON_EXPLICIT (era-tagged)

- **No central authority.** That is the region's defining political fact and it
  persisted through both the 2E and 3E: Murkmire "remained autonomous" of the
  Ebonheart Pact despite Heita-Meen's claim; local tree-minders and warchiefs
  handle their own territory; Murkmire has "a less-structured justice system"
  where small disputes go to an arbitrator both parties choose — usually the
  tribe's tree-minder. In Lilmoth people look to elders, including retired
  Shadowscales.
- **All tribes of Murkmire participate in the Shadowscale tradition**; the
  Black-Tongues dedicate most of their resources to it.

## The Umbriel catastrophe — CANON_EXPLICIT, the region's defining 4E fact

In **4E 48** the floating city of Umbriel — summoned by the rogue Hist of Lilmoth
at the An-Xileel's urging — "passed over the region … killing all inhabitants
below and resurrecting them as undead soldiers under Umbriel's command."
The "Slaughter at Lilmoth" culminated in **the city's destruction**. Umbriel then
travelled north-west up the core of Black Marsh into Morrowind.
→ Murkmire is the region canon says was *depopulated within living memory of
1½ centuries ago*. See [../extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md](../extrapolation/argonia-4e201-state.md) §3
for the 4E 201 recovery model.

## Build implications

- Murkmire is our **tribal-density region**: "at least twelve distinct tribal
  groups in Murkmire alone … scores more exist". Settlement grammar here should
  be *many small tribal villages with strong individual identity*, not a road
  hierarchy. There is no capital to route to.
- Deepmire is a canon-sanctioned **high-danger interior plateau that even locals
  avoid** — an ideal D4/D5 pocket that isn't the Lost City, plus a legitimate
  refuge-of-last-resort in a crisis quest.
- Underwater play has explicit canon hooks here: sunken southern coast, Lakemire
  Xanmeer, sea-drakes, the sunken shrine of Xhon-Mehl in Lilmoth.
- The Xinchei-Konu (weather-changing calendar) is a strong systemic artefact if we
  ever want a *diegetic* explanation for weather/season state.
- The mangrove wall is a natural, canon barrier for gating the Lilmoth approach.
