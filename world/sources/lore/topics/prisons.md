# The prison province

Covers quest-plan lore source L30 (Blackrose Prison). Sources:
Lore:Blackrose Prison, Lore:Blackrose, Lore:Lilmothiit, PGE3/Argonia,
Lore:Argonian, Lore:Stormhold, Lore:Gideon, Lore:Black Marsh
(fetched 2026-08-24).

## The premise — CANON_EXPLICIT

After 1E 2837 the Empire could not work out what Black Marsh was *for*, so it
used it as a dumping ground:

> "The land that had once been the home of freedom for Tamriel's criminals became
> its greatest prison state. Anyone considered too dangerous to hold in
> 'civilised' dungeons in other Provinces was sent to Black Marsh." — PGE3

Named famous convicts: the axe-murderer **Nai**, the heretic **Devir-Mir**, and
Empress **Tavia** (Gideon, 1E 2899). Named prisons: **Blackrose Prison**,
**White Rose Prison**, and the prison at **Stormhold**.

## Blackrose Prison ("the Rose") — CANON_EXPLICIT

- **Not in Blackrose city.** It lies **a day's march south of it, in northern
  Murkmire**, toward Dead-Water Village, surrounded by other abandoned Imperial
  settlements.
- Built in the **early Second Era** by Potentate **Versidue-Shaie**, executed by
  his battlemage **Pelladil Direnni**, who raised it **in a single day** using an
  army of **stone atronachs** out of the rubble of the extinct **Lilmothiit**
  community of ancient Blackrose.
- Sited deliberately for two reasons: **the climate kills non-Argonians** past a
  certain point in the marsh, and **the Naga**, whose aggression made escape into
  the surrounding swamp suicidal. Rebellious Naga also became a large share of
  the inmates — which eventually made the place ungovernable.
- **Abandoned** when the Second Empire collapsed after Savirien-Chorak's death;
  reclaimed by the swamp. The **Blackguards** — descendants of its inmates — took
  it over and became **the largest criminal organisation in Murkmire**, hated by
  the neighbouring tribes and working as highwaymen. By 2E 582 it held Blackguard
  bandits above and **vampires and undead in its lower levels**, under the Naga
  *raj-kaal* **Drakeeh the Unchained**, a shamanistic necromancer who left the
  path to his sanctum open to test challengers.
- **Reclaimed by the Third Empire**, active under Uriel VII, holding **Jagar
  Tharn's surviving associates** to die there. Still "the most secure and
  notorious prison of our own time" in 3E 432 —
- **but in 3E 432 it broke.** "Some of the worst murderers, thieves, and
  **political revolutionaries** had already escaped into the swamps" before it was
  sealed and mended. The Empire believed none of them could survive inner Black
  Marsh. → That is the second population of prison-descended outlaws canon has
  seeded into Murkmire, one era after the first.
- **Canon says nothing about the Rose after 3E 432.**

### The Rose in 4E 201 — `OWNER DECISION Q3, BINDING`

**A ruin reoccupied by the Blackguards' heirs.** The cycle canon already ran
twice — abandoned by a collapsing empire, occupied by its inmates' descendants —
ran a third time after **Umbriel passed directly over it in 4E 48**, on its way up
from Lilmoth into northern Murkmire.

- **Who is there**: prison-born families three generations deep, exiles, outlaws,
  and people with nowhere else to go. They have their own idea of legitimacy and
  **resent being called a prison**.
- **What is below**: Umbriel's undead, still in the lower levels, alongside the
  vampires and undead canon already placed there in the 2E.
- **Its relationship to Blackrose city**: a day's march apart, no shared
  government, and every outsider assumes otherwise. That confusion is the city's
  standing external problem and is worth playing.
- **The state does not hold it.** A people who spent 1,300 years as the Empire's
  prisoners did not inherit the Empire's prison and keep it running. If the owner
  later wants a live coercive institution, the recommendation is a **separate
  state work-camp**, sited at White Rose — see below and
  [../extrapolation/settlement-register.md](../extrapolation/settlement-register.md) §6.

## White Rose Prison — CANON_EXPLICIT (thin)

Named alongside Blackrose as one of the several prisons the Imperials built in
Black Marsh after 1E 2837 (Lore:Argonian). Cited again in Argonian funerary
lore: Argonians who die away from the Hist, "**even in stone prisons such as
White Rose**", can still return to the Hist if their bones are brought to the
dirt. No UESP article; location unstated. → A free second prison site, with a
canon emotional hook already attached: **the bones of Argonians who died there
still need carrying home.** That is a quest, a pilgrimage and a dungeon in one.

## Stormhold's prison — CANON_EXPLICIT

Infamous in the late Third Era (c. 3E 427) under Warden **Quintus Varus**, "a
reputed lunatic" who broke into the old Ayleid tunnels beneath the town hunting
**Stormhold Crystals** and set favoured prisoners — "**Tunnel Rats**" — digging
for them in the haunted passages in return for limited freedom. The prison became
corrupted: prisoners and overseers went mad and turned into beasts, the corruption
held at bay only by storing it in **Hollow Crystals**. Varus's real goal was the
**Storm Crystal**, into which he meant to store all the evil in the Empire. A
prisoner (the "Master Tunnel Rat") beat him to it and used it to undo the
corruption. → Beneath Stormhold there is a canon **prison-into-Ayleid-ruin
dungeon** with a documented magical contamination history.

## Blackrose city itself — CANON_EXPLICIT

Distinct from the prison but tarred by it: "a city surrounded in infamy … many
have entered its walls but few ever leave it." Built by the **Lilmothiit**;
Kothringi and Yespest also lived in the region; **Red Bramman's bandit kingdom**
was nearby in 1E 1033; rebuilt in Imperial style after 1E 2837; rumoured "in
ruins" in 2E 582; **rebelled against the Third Empire in the late 3E** and raised
a large army, broken by Empress Morihatha's battlemage **Welloc** using
*vampiric cloud* (a spell that displaces the rebels' strength and skill to their
opponents).

## Build implications

- The province's carceral history gives us **four distinct outlaw populations
  with provenance**, at four different depths of history: Blackguards (2E
  inmates' descendants), the 3E 432 escapees, Blackrose city's broken rebel army,
  and Stormhold's mad Tunnel Rats. Loot, grudges and hideouts follow directly.
- Prisons are the Empire's **only durable architecture** in the province. Where we
  want big foreign stonework standing in the marsh, it should be penal.
- "Argonian bones in stone prisons still need to come home" is the most
  emotionally legible quest premise canon offers, and it works at any scale from
  a single side-quest to a faction line.
- **How the province punishes now, with no prisons and no courts**, is in
  [labour-and-bondage.md](labour-and-bondage.md) — **the Owing**. Warden Varus's
  "Tunnel Rats", who dug for crystals "in return for limited freedom", are the
  canon precedent it is built on.
- The Rose's siting logic (**the swamp itself is the wall**) is exactly our
  fixed-danger model, stated by canon. Reuse it as the justification for why our
  D4–D5 interior needs no fences.
