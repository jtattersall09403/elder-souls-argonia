# The lost peoples of Black Marsh

Five non-Argonian peoples lived here and are gone. Their ruins, names and
inheritances are a large part of what makes the province feel deep. Sources:
Lore:Kothringi, Lore:Lilmothiit, Lore:Orma, Lore:Yespest, Lore:Horwalli,
Lore:Barsaebic Ayleids, Lore:Knahaten Flu, PGE3/Argonia, Lore:Blackrose,
Lore:Gideon, Lore:Stormhold (fetched 2026-08-24).

## Kothringi — the Lustrous Folk

**CANON_EXPLICIT.** Silver-skinned humans, believed descended from the **Nedes**;
once thought the only humans native to Tamriel. Present before the most ancient
records. Extinguished by the **Knahaten Flu** (2E 560).

- **Where**: the **Gideon region in large numbers**; **Stillrise Village** and
  **Zuuk** in Shadowfen; the Blackrose region; and **"the vast complicated ruins
  of the Kothringi capitals"** in the deep interior (The Argonian Account Bk 4) —
  a major, largely untouched ruin family that is *not* on the coast.
- **Culture**: they preferred to go naked. Worshipped **Z'en** (God of Toil; the
  Bosmer got him from Kothringi sailors), plus **Dibella, Mara and Kynareth**;
  earlier they venerated **Clavicus Vile**. **Music was central**: hollow logs as
  wamasu-sized drums, cliff-strider sinew bow-harps, and above all **chimes** —
  one Kothringi hung hundreds of tin and copper chimes from branches. They invited
  Argonians to feasts and played for them; cultural exchange is attested, and the
  **Naka-Desh** of Blackwood loved their music.
- **Craft**: "peerless crafters"; wove metal strands into stronger nautical rope;
  idiosyncratic shipbuilding (unusual paddle placement, shallow draft *and* a
  sail) that could navigate **both swamps and coastal waters**; abacuses with
  varnished sphere-scarab beads; multi-colour woven reed mats; dream catchers;
  **humming dolls** (air bladders and reeds inside, hum when squeezed); glass
  beads worn as wearable instruments; moonstone statuary. Throwing knives, axes
  and harpoons. Their spellcraft was "unusual to the point that an experienced
  mage may miss its presence".
- **Death**: buried in **cairns**, adorned with glass beads.
- **Legacy in our era**: **Gideon's Nibenese share the regional Kothringi devotion
  to Dibella**, and their Dibellaseum is the old Martius manor. Residents claim
  Kothringi spirits linger in the town; fortune hunters dig for Kothringi
  treasure. **Kothringi knights made up a large part of Gideon's Imperial
  garrison** — including **Zuuk**, Empress Tavia's knight and co-conspirator, who
  went home to Shadowfen and founded the town named after him.
- **Survivors**: **Stillrise Village** traded its people to Clavicus Vile for
  undeath and still stood in 2E 582; **Drillk** of Zuuk survived by absorbing a
  keystone; some Kothringi serve the **Ideal Masters** in red robes and skull
  masks. The **Crimson Ship** sailed west with refugees and was never seen again;
  Hammerfell keeps the **Day of Shame** (20th of Rain's Hand) against its return.
  → Canon leaves the door open a crack. A single surviving Kothringi in 4E 201 is
  defensible; a community is not.

## Lilmothiit — the Fox-Folk

**CANON_EXPLICIT.** Vulpine beast-folk, possibly related to the Khajiit; nomadic
and tribal, so they left few enduring marks. **They founded Lilmoth and
Blackrose** and held the whole southern coast. In long decline, they withdrew
from the coasts inland toward Blackrose; scarcely seen by the 2E; finished by the
Knahaten Flu around 2E 560 (Murkmire's remnant fled inland toward Blackrose,
fate unclear). A tribe of them once chose to **hide with their kits rather than
fight** elven invaders, unlike the Fauns. Their ruined community at Blackrose
became the **fabric of Blackrose Prison**. Blackwood children still fly
**Lilmothiit Fox-Kites** — orange paper, with a real fox's tail.
→ Two of our eight cities are built on Lilmoth**iit** foundations, and their
substratum is *nomadic*: light, wooden, low, easily overbuilt — which is why so
little survives and why the prison could be quarried out of it.

## Orma

**CANON_EXPLICIT.** A tribe of Men native to Black Marsh, **born without eyes** —
their skulls lack eye sockets — and consequently masters of their other senses,
especially touch. Present through the First and Second Eras; believed gone from
the province by the late Third. One known individual, **Catchica**, was with
Reman's army at Caer Suvio and later worked at a Nibenay resort in a plot against
the Emperor. An Orma skull was used as a door-marker for a room of powerful
artefacts in Scourg Barrow.
→ The most usable of the lost peoples for *dungeon design*: an eyeless people
build and mark spaces differently. Tactile door-marks, no light wells, sound-
oriented layouts.

## Yespest (Yerpest)

**CANON_EXPLICIT, thin.** A tribe of Men native to Black Marsh, active **around
Blackrose**; possibly contemporaries of the Barsaebics. Some Imperial scholars
**erroneously credit them with building the xanmeers** — that error is itself
useful: it is the standing Imperial position that Argonians could not have built
their own monuments.

## Horwalli

**CANON_EXPLICIT, thin.** A tribal race of Men native to Black Marsh; scholars
theorise they were sent as refugees or prisoners, or settled the coastal
waterways and adapted. Present through the First and Second Eras; believed gone
by the late Third.

## Barsaebic Ayleids

**CANON_EXPLICIT.** Aedra-worshipping Ayleids exiled after the **Scouring of
Wendelbek (1E 198)**. They occupied north-eastern Black Marsh and parts of
Blackwood and founded **Silyanorn** (Stormhold), **Twyllbek** (Gideon),
**Archon** and **Thorn**, plus **Loriasel** and **Gandranen**. They fought the
Argonians, Kothringi, Orma, Yespest, Horwalli and Lilmothiit for a foothold;
**enslaved Argonians as labourers and "flesh sculptures"**; and at Loriasel made
**keystones** out of "Argonian magicka, harnessed from their captives through
torment and fear". At Root-Whisper they harvested a whole tribe's souls, which is
why the Remnant of Argon exists.

They refused King Glinferen's plea in 1E 242 and so were spared Alessia's wrath
(though the Temple of Sul in Shadowfen was sacked). Local legend says **the
Ayleids of Stormhold were conquered by the Hist**.

**Fenlords** — Barsaebic necromancer kings who survived as undead mummies, "said
to have once ruled Black Marsh". When their slaves rose, they had them executed
and raised as undead guardians. Thought to have vanished, or to have used the
**Crown of Ascension** to leave Nirn — but "in truth, some Fenlords continue to
dwell in the depths of Barsaebic ruins, ruling over the undead servants that
still guard their tombs."
→ Our canon Ayleid-ruin boss family, and the reason Argonians treat those ruins
as tainted rather than merely old.

## Cantemiric Velothi

**CANON_EXPLICIT, thin.** Dunmer dissident settlers from the north who occupied
the **eastern seaboard between Archon, Arnesia and Thorn**. Extinct — the
Knahaten Flu. Distinct from the Boethiah-worshipping dissidents who built
**Ten-Maur-Wolk** after 1E 700, and distinct from the *later* Dres-era Dunmer
whose descendants form the modern northern minority.

## Build implications

- **Every one of the eight majors sits on someone else's bones**: Lilmothiit
  (Lilmoth, Blackrose), Barsaebic Ayleid (Stormhold, Gideon, Archon, Thorn),
  Argonian pre-Duskfall (Helstrom, Soulrest and everywhere else). That is the
  "historical layers stay distinguishable" acceptance rule handed to us ready-made.
- The **cause of the emptiness is one event**: the Knahaten Flu killed the
  Kothringi, the Lilmothiit and the Cantemiric Velothi in a single generation.
  Villages of three different peoples went quiet in the same decade and were never
  reoccupied. That is a coherent, dateable reason for widespread abandoned
  non-Argonian settlement.
- Kothringi ships (swamp-and-sea capable, shallow draft, paddle-and-sail) are the
  best canon model for **our player-sailable boats' visual language** — a native
  design for exactly our water.
- Kothringi chimes and Bright-Throat chimes are the same instrument in two
  cultures. Given the attested exchange, the Bright-Throats' Hist-listening
  chime tradition can be read as an inheritance. (`EXTRAPOLATED`, low risk.)
