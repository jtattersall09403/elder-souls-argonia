## 1. Three principles that should drive every table

### P1 — Danger is stratified by species, not by level `CANON_DERIVED`

Argonians are immune to poison and disease and cannot drown; non-Argonians die of
swamp rot in a month, cannot drink the ground water, and are eaten alive by
insects. The Empire sited its worst prison on the principle that **the climate
itself kills non-Argonians past a certain point in the marsh** (Lore:Blackrose
Prison, explicit).

→ The interior is lethal to outsiders **as terrain**. An Argonian player should
experience a materially different world there, exactly as the acceptance rules
already require for underwater play. This is canon's own version of fixed danger.

### P2 — The baseline is attrition; monsters are punctuation `CANON_DERIVED`

Canon's travel accounts are overwhelmingly about **flies, water, mud, tide,
disease and rot**, with set-piece creatures appearing rarely and decisively. A
region's danger should be expressed first as an **ambient attrition field**
(insect load, water potability, disease pressure, footing) and only second as
encounter density. Raising spawn counts is the wrong lever here.

### P3 — Nearly everything is useful, and Argonians use it `CANON_EXPLICIT`

Canon consistently pairs a creature with what is made from it: hackwing hide into
armour and furniture, wamasu hide into tarps and organs into lanterns, crocodile
hide into armour and travel bags, turtle shell into platters and shield bosses,
giant-snake hide to protect textiles, baby haj mota shells into bowls, marsh
giant blood into paint and ink, death hopper vocal sacs into Sul-Xan armour,
swamp jelly gas bladders to ferment daril, the Naga-Kur's own dead into weapons.
→ **Loot in Argonia is harvest.** Treat creature drops as materials with a named
downstream use, not as vendor trash.

## 2. Regional encounter composition

`EXTRAPOLATED` from canon distributions. Each region gets a **signature** (the
thing you remember), a **staple** (what you actually fight), an **ambient
hazard**, and an **apex** (rare, fixed, lethal).

| Region | Signature | Staple | Ambient hazard | Apex |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Shadowfen** | **Giant snakes** ("Ample Serpents"), colossal from lack of predators | Crocodiles, fleshflies, wamasu juveniles, bog blights near old graves | Blood rot, the droops, swamp fever; crocodile-infested ravines | **Wamasu** (electrifies the water); lamias in the Loriasel caverns |
| **Murkmire** | **Miregaunts** — part of the land, and they come back | Naga-Kur territory patrols, hackwing flocks, swamp jellies, voriplasm | Fleshfly clouds thickening inland; sap-poisoning near heavily-tapped trees | **Swamp leviathan**; sea-drakes offshore; whatever is left in Deepmire |
| **Thornmarsh / east** | **Open ground** — the shock of visibility | Alit, kagouti, hackwings on the grassland edge, smugglers, border patrols | Least hostile region in the province; exposure rather than rot | Wamasu drawn down from the marsh edge |
| **Middle Argonia** | **The Hist forest itself**, and rootworms passing beneath | Tribeless Naga, Paatru and Sarpa territory (hostile on sight), giant snakes | Heart-Mud; undrinkable water; "pinnacled mountains" that collapse at a ripple | **Feather-serpents** (canon-legendary, unelaborated — our one free apex); swamp leviathans |
| **Blackwood / Gloommire** | **Death hoppers** (canon: notably large population) | Bog dogs, hackwings, crows, Sul-Xan raiders, bandits on the old road | Monsoon road closure; peat-fire gas vents and subsidence from the Great Burn | **Marsh giants** (avoid, per the Hist); Sul-Xan war parties |
| **Deep basin / Lost City approach** | Everything above, at once, with no settlement to retreat to | — | Total: water, air, ground and light all hostile to outsiders | Hand-authored only |
| **Coasts and bays** | **Sea-drakes** in the deeps off Lilmoth | Slaughterfish, dreugh, ripper eels in the Lilmoth canals, pirates | Tide; long tidal reach up the rivers | Sea-drake; whatever the Southern Sea washes in |

### Creature notes that change how a fight plays

`CANON_EXPLICIT` unless marked.

| Creature | Behaviour that matters |
|---|---|
| **Wamasu** | Lightning. A Nord account: the champion "fell, flashes and sparks of energy consuming him **and our party**, and **cursing all the water to deadly convulsions**." It makes an entire body of water lethal. Handlers use **Hist-sap-insulated prods**. Eggs **emit lightning while the embryo grows and are ready to hatch when the lightning stops** — a readable, diegetic timer. Adult males cannibalise juveniles. |
| **Hackwing** | Flocks. "They strike quickly in an attempt to wound or knock down their prey. They will fly off after wounding their victim, only to come back **when the victim is almost dead from blood loss**." → A bleed-and-withdraw enemy that punishes attrition rather than burst. Trainable with a padded bludgeon and a wooden whistle. |
| **Death hopper** | "Lurk beneath the surface of water, waiting for their prey to wander above them", **swallow large prey whole**, are **extremely poisonous** and **spit globs of toxic matter over great distances**. Found in packs. → Ambush-from-water plus ranged poison. |
| **Miregaunt** | "**Part of the land: when one is struck down, it will inevitably come back later.**" Each has a **midsection cavity** which may be empty, may hold rubble, or may hold a gemstone, an ancient relic **or a living creature** — locals believe such things were taken in to protect or imprison them. → The perfect fixed guardian: respawning by canon, and its loot has provenance built in. |
| **Marsh giant** | Ecologically load-bearing (fells dead trees, turns soil, suppresses invasive fungi, hinders loggers). **The Hist have a long acrimonious history with them and tell Argonians to avoid them.** → Killing one should be an ecological and religious offence, not a bounty. |
| **Voriplasm** | "It'll strip you to the bone by the second syllable." Leaves small bones behind it. Moves across a pool as if it were a pool. |
| **Bog blight** | An Argonian corpse whose **grave-stake came loose**. "Dead-and-not-dead things that eat Nagas whole", dangerous in numbers. Warded with **pahnjees** (racks of oily spoiled meat masking a living scent). → **A player-caused hazard.** Pulling stakes makes a region worse permanently, which serves "the world never softens" better than any spawn table. |
| **Giant snake** | Abundant prey and no competent predators → "a dozen cattle laid end to end". Swallows whole; canon has a victim cutting his way out from inside and dying of fleshflies afterwards. |
| **Crocodile** | Jel: **rormasu**, "big-mouthed reptile that blinks above water". Two men long, fast, attacking **inside town limits** at Bogmother. Grow throughout life. **Domesticated by Argonians** — the Dead-Water tribe keeps them. |
| **Ripper eel** | **Trained** to hunt Argonians illegally crossing the canals of Lilmoth — "though they do not attack people who have rubbed themselves with **eel-slime**." → A security system with a known, obtainable, disgusting counter. Built-in stealth design. |
| **Swamp jelly** | Mostly **docile** and "make excellent travel companions for the marsh"; Argonians name breeds after flavours (cantaloupe, mint, blue, plum). The **plum** is rarest and seeing one is **good fortune**; its feeler is worn as a "Good Luck Tentacle". **Deepmire breeds can kill you on brief contact.** → Same silhouette, opposite meaning, by region. Excellent for teaching players to read locality. |
| **Bog dog** | Pack hunters that **grow camouflaging vegetation in the fur on their backs**; tameable and loyal. Their dung is an ingredient in a traditional Argonian good-luck concoction. |
| **Sea-drake** | Crocodilian with paddles for legs, in the deeps off Lilmoth — "making those waters incredibly dangerous for swimmers". → The reason casual open-water swimming in Oliis Bay is not safe. |
| **Rootworm** | Not an enemy. Transport. Eight feet, translucent, swallows travellers whole, digests very slowly. |

## 3. Fixed populations, not spawns

`CANON_DERIVED` from the fixed-danger rule and from canon's own territorial
language ("the Naga-Kur **control** much of the inner swamps and are **greatly
feared** by surrounding villages"; the Tum-Taleel **own** the Keel-Sakka bridge;
Sul-Xan **raid** Blackwood from three named holds).

**Rule**: hostile humanoid encounters in Argonia should be **territorial and
attributable** — you are in someone's ground, and it is always someone specific.

| Territory-holder | Where | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| **Naga-Kur (Dead-Water)** | Inner northern Murkmire | Not bandits. A polity. They will let you pass if you understand the rules; they will not explain them. |
| **Tribeless Naga** | Old plantation fringes, the roads when roads carry merchants | The province's actual highwaymen. Canon: they leave the roads when merchants stop using them. |
| **Sul-Xan** | Blackwood — Xi-Tsei, Rockgrove, the Silent Halls | Dagon-worshipping Naga cult. Sacrifice outsiders and Argonians alike. The one straightforwardly evil faction by Argonian standards too. |
| **Blackguards' heirs** | Blackrose Prison and northern Murkmire | Prison-descended, three generations deep, with a claim to their own legitimacy (owner decision Q3). |
| **Lut-Eileel** | Blackwood | "A tribe of Argonian warmongers." Besieged Stonewastes. |
| **Veeskhleel (Ghost People)** | Veeskhleel-Tzel / Blight Bog, Murkmire | Nocturnal, pale, dwell entirely in the dark, stalk and abduct. Steal eggs — that is how they reproduce. Horror, and local. |
| **Xit-Xaht** | Mazzatun | Stone-builders under a mad Hist. Enslave neighbours to keep building. |
| **Fenlords and their undead** | The depths of Barsaebic ruins | Ayleid necromancer kings who executed their rebelling slaves and raised them as guards. |
| **Four Winds** | Stonewastes and its region | The one hostile-territory entry that is *friendly* — hereditary defenders who will assume you are the problem until shown otherwise. |

## 4. Loot provenance

The acceptance rule is "loot has provenance". Canon supplies four clean sources,
and everything placed should belong to one.

### S1 — Harvest `CANON_EXPLICIT`

Materials off creatures and plants, with named downstream uses:

| From | Yields | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Wamasu | Hide; **luminescent organs**; black bones that hold a charge for years | Tarps, **lanterns**, armour; the bones tingle and are alchemically live |
| Hackwing | Thick supple strong leather; leathery head "plumage"; feathers | Armour that allows quick movement while withstanding brutal attacks — "invaluable to the Argonian hunters"; furniture |
| Crocodile | Hide; teeth; tongue | Armour and travel bags; **maturation-trial tooth necklaces**; baked tongue is a common dish |
| Giant snake | Hide | Protecting delicate textiles; Shadowfen armour |
| Turtle / haj mota | Shells; baby haj mota shells | Platters, mugs, dishes, **shield bosses and whole shields**; bowls |
| Death hopper | Vocal sacs; bile | Sul-Xan armour; the traditional good-luck concoction |
| Marsh giant | Blood | Paints and inks |
| Swamp jelly | Gas bladder; feelers | **Fermenting daril**; the plum feeler as a luck charm |
| Moon adder | Venom | **Daril** — fatal to non-Argonians, transformed by an agent in Argonian blood |
| Fellrunner | Pale down | An armband that lets a hunter move **undetected through Murkwood** |
| Hist | Sap; rarely **Hist amber** | Ritual; medicine; **rubber** (insulated wamasu prods); Zaht stones; and the province's most valuable contraband |
| Jassa Red slug | Acidic mucus | **Slug shaping** — carving channels in wood and stone |
| Bog blight / grave-stake | Life energy clinging to the stake | Fuels necromantic ritual. Taking one is a desecration with mechanical consequences. |

### S2 — The dead and their stakes `CANON_EXPLICIT`

Every Argonian burial is a **xul-vaat** carved with the dead one's whole story —
victories, defeats, childhood, friends, dreams, nightmares. This is the province's
best environmental storytelling and it is free: a legible biography on a post,
placeable anywhere, readable without a journal entry, and **actively dangerous to
disturb**. Grave goods are minimal; the *stake* is the artefact.

### S3 — The ruin layers `CANON_DERIVED`

Four distinguishable strata, and an item should sit in exactly one:

| Layer | Contents | Tell |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-Duskfall Argonian** (xanmeer) | Volcanic glass weaponry, layered cloth, leather, bone and **bronze**; feathers, dyed yarns, jewels, bronze emblems, gold; **vakka stones**; ceremonial Sithis altars and staves | Metal and permanence — both alien to modern Argonians |
| **Barsaebic Ayleid** | **Keystones** (distilled from tormented Argonian captives), Ten Ancestors material, welkynd-adjacent stonework, Fenlord tombs | Elven, and *tainted* rather than merely old |
| **Dunmer colonial** | Ornate stone, slave-camp fittings, saltrice apparatus, House Dres marks | Northern, recent, resented |
| **Imperial** | Penal, fiscal and military only — prisons, forts, a customs house, toll-towns, a governor's mansion, a courthouse. **No temples-and-markets legacy except at Gideon.** | Sunk, mouldering, reclaimed |

### S4 — The three depopulations `CANON_DERIVED`

The Great Burn (1E 2828), the Knahaten Flu (2E 560–603) and Umbriel (4E 48) each
left **whole settlements' worth of goods in place with nobody to inherit them**.
An abandoned house with the table still laid is not lazy set-dressing here — it is
the documented history of the province three times over. Date the abandonment by
its layer: burned and gas-vented (west, 1E), emptied by plague with Kothringi or
Lilmothiit material culture (2E), or stripped of life with Argonian and Imperial
goods intact and undead nearby (4E, Murkmire and the two overrun cities).

## 5. Underwater ecology

`CANON_EXPLICIT` anchors for the swimming/underwater programme:

- **Lakemire Xanmeer** — "a picturesque **underwater** xanmeer", once an Argonian
  water mage's. Our flagship submerged ruin.
- **Murkmire "was swallowed by the Southern Sea"** by legend — scattered ruins
  across the drowned southern coast.
- **Lilmoth's sunken Imperial villas** and the **sunken shrine of Xhon-Mehl the
  Fisher, Ancestral Organ Lord of Lilmoth**.
- **Sea-drakes** in the deeps off Lilmoth make Oliis Bay genuinely dangerous.
- **Ripper eels** patrol Lilmoth's canals, countered by eel-slime.
- **Dreugh**, slaughterfish, and the named rare fish of each water (Fat Sleeper,
  Longlure Eelfin, Spotted Seatrout in Oliis Bay; Ivory Darter, Onkobra Muskie and
  the prized **Pantherfish** in the Panther River).
- **Glowgill Guar** of the Archon coast — navy-blue, bright blue stripes,
  reportedly **breathe underwater**.
- **Archon's children play underwater**: Jaw Beetle Fight, Marsh Tag (tagging by
  sound). Underwater competence is ordinary there, which is the right place to
  teach it.
- **Blackrose's drowned Hist** (see [hist-placement.md](hist-placement.md)) — a
  sacred site reached by diving.

`EXTRAPOLATED`: the underwater layer should be **culturally normal in the south
and east, and lethal in the deep basin** — the same species-stratified rule as
overland. An Argonian swims to work; an outsider drowns or is eaten.

## 6. Seasonal keying

`CANON_DERIVED` — canon establishes a monsoon that **closes the Gloommire roads**,
rivers that tide hard enough to swamp a bridge for four days, a marsh that
"inevitably floods" (the grave-stake exists because of it), offerings made to the
wamasu **Haynekhtnamet** to stop "the seasonal flooding", and a **wintertide
rootworm migration** running south to Gideon.

Minimum viable seasonal model for Phase 13:

| Season | Water | Routes | Ecology |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wet** | High; channels navigable that are not otherwise; grave-stakes tested | Land legs close; boat legs open; tidal crossings unreliable | Egg-laying (**Thtithil**); insect load peaks; bog blights surface |
| **Dry** | Low; mudflats; some channels impassable to boats | Land legs open; portages appear | Peat-fire risk in the west; concentrated predators at remaining water |
| **Wintertide** | — | **Rootworm migration south** — the Underground Express runs to Gideon | Canon-named; a seasonal fast-travel window |

## 7. What Phase 13 should take from this

1. Build the **attrition field first** (insect load, potability, disease pressure,
   footing), then place encounters into it. P2.
2. Make every hostile humanoid group **attributable to a named territory-holder**
   from §3. No anonymous bandits.
3. Give every creature drop a **named downstream use** from §4/S1. Harvest, not
   vendor trash.
4. Place **grave-stakes** liberally and make disturbing them matter. They are the
   province's signature interactable.
5. Use **miregaunts** wherever a place must stay dangerous — canon says they come
   back, so they need no respawn justification.
6. Date every ruin's abandonment to one of the **three depopulations**, and let the
   goods left behind match.
7. Key water level, route availability and the rootworm line to **season**.
