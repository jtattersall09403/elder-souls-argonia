# Sithis, the Nisswo and the Shadowscales

Covers quest-plan lore sources L04–L05 (Sithis & Nisswo) and L08–L09
(Shadowscales & Veezara). Sources: Lore:Sithis, Lore:Nisswo, Lore:Shadowscales,
Lore:Xanmeer, Lore:Duskfall, Lore:Argonian, Lore:Kingdom of Black Marsh,
Lore:Glenbridge, Lore:Sul-Xan, Lore:Archon (fetched 2026-08-24).

## The two Sithises — CANON_EXPLICIT

The single most important religious fact about Argonia, and the one most often
got wrong: **Argonian Sithis changed**, and the change *is* the religion.

| | Pre-Duskfall | Post-Duskfall |
|---|---|---|
| Sithis is | **The Destroyer.** Always watching, waiting to return existence to the Void. | **Raj-Sithis the Changer.** "First Creator and Final Destroyer." Change itself. |
| Priests are | **Nisswo-Kings** — the ruling class. Kings in stone citadels. | **The Clutch of Nisswo** — travelling preachers with *no central governance* and *no consistent interpretation*. |
| Practice | Appeasement; ceremonial altars and staves; **live blood sacrifice**; statues of Sithis; his symbols woven into armour and cloth. | Teaching people to let go. Making art and then destroying it. Stealing something and leaving clues so it can be found. Sermons, writing, meditation. Offerings that may still include voluntary blood sacrifice, at that Nisswo's discretion. |
| Architecture | Xanmeers — stone, forever, "built out of a fear of death and forgetting". | Reed and mud, built to be replaced. |
| Named for | — | **shunatei**: "the pain caused by holding on too tightly to that which has come to pass" — the Fear of Death and Forgetting. The thing modern Argonians define themselves against. **vastei**: change. **ku-vastei**: one who brings *necessary* change. |

The Clutch's contradictions are doctrine, not disorder: Nisswos travel precisely
so each village hears *many* interpretations and thus more of the whole truth.
They **look down on the Dark Brotherhood** for preaching a "single untruth" that
humanises Sithis. The Brotherhood, for its part, has been known to **refuse
writs when the target is protected by a Nisswo**.

Modern Argonians regard their ancestors' xanmeer-building and fear-worship as a
**flawed belief system** — but still watch over and maintain the xanmeers "so that
the mistakes of the past would never be repeated". They are not nostalgic ruins;
they are cautionary monuments under active care.

### Named Sithis sites

- **Teeth of Sithis** (Murkmire) — the largest known temple; site of mass
  sacrifice; still tended by the Clutch though it no longer serves its purpose.
  Its High Priestess **Xulneihavu Shuxaltsei** survived Duskfall as a **vampire**
  and briefly retook it in 2E 582.
- **Xul-Thuxis** (Deepmire) — where the Nisswo priest-kings made their sacrifices.
- **Xal Uthix**, **Glenbridge's xanmeer** — Sithis monuments; Glenbridge's Nisswo
  read the ruin's collapse *as the god's own sermon*.
- Nisswo material culture: **black onyx** meditation stones; **Nisswo fabric
  wraps** (patterned, high-status; the same wrapping used on a bog blight).

### The Sul-Xan heresy — CANON_EXPLICIT

A Naga tribe/cult who worship **Mehrunes Dagon as "the Razor Prince" and the
"True Egg-Child of Sithis"**, holding "a one-sided view of Argonian religion,
embracing only the darkest, cruelest beliefs, and scorning the rest as weakness".
Jade death masks, green raider tattoos, ritual sacrifice of outsiders and
Argonians alike, wide soul snares to summon spirits of destruction. Based in
Blackwood (Xi-Tsei, Rockgrove, Silent Halls).
→ Canon's own answer to "what does an evil Sithis cult look like in Argonia" —
and note that it is a **Daedric** heresy grafted onto Sithis, not a Sithis sect.
Any project-original radical cult should be measured against this: the Sul-Xan
are the extreme, and they are still a *tribe with a village*, not a conspiracy.

## The Shadowscales — CANON_EXPLICIT

- Argonians **born under the sign of the Shadow** are taken at birth and given to
  the **Dark Brotherhood** to be trained in stealth and assassination. Any who
  live to come of age become full Brotherhood family — **or leave** to serve
  Black Marsh as **ku-vastei**: agents of needed change, **arbiters** and
  peacekeepers who "act with impunity" and bring "swamp law" down on unruly
  foreigners and natives alike. Retired Shadowscales are the elders people in
  Lilmoth turn to for guidance.
- Identified by **brass medallions** inscribed with Sithis's likeness.
- Distinct rules from the Brotherhood's Five Tenets: **a Shadowscale may not kill
  a fellow Shadowscale**, Brotherhood member or not. Tenet-breaking is treason and
  is punished by execution, traditionally carried out by an unaffiliated assassin
  sent by the "Argonian Royal Court". Prolonged or torturous kills are frowned on.
- **All tribes of Murkmire participate**; the **Black-Tongues (Kota-Vimleel)**
  dedicate almost all their resources to producing them.
- **The Nisswo view Brotherhood training as a necessary evil** and urge
  Shadowscales to abandon Brotherhood theology once trained.
- Some Shadowscales **opposed the Ebonheart Pact** and fled Black Marsh as
  traitors; others fought for it.

### The 4E collapse — CANON_EXPLICIT, and it is total

| Date | Fact |
|---|---|
| **4E 187** | The **Archon training facility is shut down**. Listener **Alisanne Dupre** (Cheydinhal) and **Rasha** plan to revive it in response to the Brotherhood's decline. |
| **4E 201** | **The last known living Shadowscale dies.** A Shadowscale in Skyrim states the order "was no longer a fully functioning group". UESP: "it is possible that the Shadowscales have become extinct." |

→ In our production year the Shadowscales are **a dying or dead institution with
living memory**: old retired ku-vastei arbiters in the cities, an abandoned
facility at Archon, a fourteen-year gap since the last hatchling was taken, and
Black-Tongue tradition with nowhere to send its children. That is a far better
dramatic situation than a functioning assassin corps, and it is the canon one.

## The Scalded Throne — CANON_EXPLICIT

Almost every mention of a **King of Black Marsh** is a mention of the Shadowscales
as his personal assassins and the protectors of the Argonian Royal Court. But:

> "Some believe this is simply misinterpreted information, and that **no such king
> has ruled since the pre-Duskfall days**… The so-called **'Scalded Throne'** has
> supposedly been **empty for centuries** (if it existed at all), and the monarch's
> role as commander of the Shadowscales is said to be overstated."

A common late-3E Argonian oath is **"By the Throne of Aphicles"** — significance
unknown. → The kingship is a *vacancy with institutions still pointing at it*.
That is exactly the shape our Eye-of-Argonia and Lost-City material wants, and it
is why the Eye "grants no kingship" is the right project reading: canon has
already decided the throne is empty and probably always was.

## Other Argonian religion — CANON_EXPLICIT

- Argonians "do not formally recognise or worship any type of Tamrielic deity"
  except the more deeply assimilated. They revere the **Hist** — a bond, not
  bondage; "their servitude is voluntary."
- **Hist-magic** is real and does not follow conventional spellcraft: a marsh-born
  Argonian can summon **a cloud of spores** to incapacitate a target. A Keeper at
  the Hatching Pools does exactly this to an intruder.
- **The dream-wallow**: a shared practice across tribes — isolation plus potent
  mind-altering herbs to see beyond the physical; it is said to sit "between the
  lines of a vision and reality" and **can physically manifest objects**.
  Bright-Throats use **deep sap**; the Naga-Kur paint their faces red, inhale
  burning **Starblossom** fumes and must kill a **kaju**, a dream-beast chosen by
  their chief, unassisted. Tree-minders and naheesh dream-talkers are consulted.
- **Death**: the body is staked into the mud with a **xul-vaat** (grave-stake)
  carved with the dead one's whole story — victories, defeats, childhood, friends,
  dreams and nightmares — so it cannot float up when the marsh floods and cannot
  rise as a **bog blight**. The **grave-singer** tends the corpse, stakes it, reads
  the tribe's stakes and sings the fallen one's final song. Pull a stake loose and
  you get a bog blight; **pahnjees** (racks of oily spoiled meat) mask a living
  scent from them. **Life energy clings to grave-stakes** and can fuel necromancy.
- Argonian **souls return to the Hist**; a Hist's sap *is* its soul, and an
  Argonian's blood, essence and soul are made from it. Drain a Hist and its
  tribe's souls have nowhere to go — they linger in the living world, mad. Boil or
  distil the sap and the Hist is in anguish the tribe can feel. Blasphemers are
  punished by being **bound to an urn** and denied return.
- **No Argonian buried by Hist rites receives Arkay's Blessing**; foreign
  necromancers rarely bother with Black Marsh because corpses rot too fast.
- The **Adzi-Kostleel creation myth** (Atak the Great Root, Kota the serpent,
  their fusion **Atakota**, the shed **Shadow** that swallows creation to protect
  it) is the Argonian monomyth and reads nothing like the Imperial one.
- **Z'en**, the Bosmeri God of Toil, is thought to have originated in Argonian and
  Akaviri myth before Kothringi sailors carried him to Valenwood.
- A **Y'ffre heresy** arose among Argonian refugees in Valenwood who, having cut
  their Hist ties, hoped Y'ffre and Valenwood's trees would let them reproduce;
  ex-Shadowscale refugees assassinated its leaders in 2E 582.

## Build implications

- Sithis worship in Argonia should be written as **serene, plural and public** —
  a travelling priest teaching a village to let go of a burned hut — with the
  Dark Brotherhood's version explicitly marked as a foreign misreading. Any cult
  in our quest lines that treats Sithis as capital-E Evil must be *characterised
  as wrong by the Nisswo*, or it breaks canon tone.
- The xanmeer is not "the ruin the locals ignore"; it is a **maintained
  cautionary monument**. Argonian NPCs care for xanmeers and don't care about the
  trinkets inside them ("they would prefer fresh slugs and sturdy tools").
  Grave-robbing them is a foreigner's crime, and locals object on principle, not
  property.
- Grave-stakes are the single best piece of environmental storytelling canon
  gives us: **every Argonian burial is a legible biography on a post**, and
  pulling one up spawns an undead. That is a placeable, repeatable, diegetic
  system for the whole province.
- The Shadowscale collapse in 4E 187/201 is a ready-made faction premise: an
  order in its last generation. See
  [../extrapolation/quest-plan-deltas.md](../extrapolation/quest-plan-deltas.md).
