# Hist placement — the rule, and the tree for every settlement

Closes gap **G2.10** (every settlement's Hist named and its state described) and
**G5.7** (how many Hist there are and where). This was flagged as blocking Phase
11: no Argonian settlement can be given a causal record without answering "which
Hist, in what state, and who speaks for it".

Canon in [hist-and-sap.md](hist-and-sap.md); tiers per
[../extrapolation/PROGRESS.md](../extrapolation/PROGRESS.md).

---

## 1. What canon fixes

`CANON_EXPLICIT`

- The Hist "grow in the **innermost swamps**"; a traveller crossing the deep
  interior sees "the great **forest** of full-grown Hist trees" (The Argonian
  Account Bk 4). The interior holds a *forest*, not scattered individuals.
- "**Most, though not all**, Argonian tribes revere and build their lives around a
  Hist tree… constructing their settlements around it" (Lore:Argonian). Some
  tribes have none.
- **Cities have trees too, and not only tribal ones**: Lilmoth "possesses its own
  Hist Tree" **despite having no native tribe**; Stormhold's stands "in the town
  square, in the middle of a pond, surrounded by ancient pillars, broken arches
  and a small stone wall"; Stonewastes' is "in the centre of town"; the Hatching
  Pools has the great Hist of Shadowfen; Heita-Meen received her vision from
  "the **Archein Hist tree**" in an Archein village.
- **Every xanmeer had a Hist chamber**: "Inside each xanmeer was a room that
  housed the Hist of its respective tribe, keeping it safe from any outside
  invaders, albeit separating it from its tribe in the process."
- **All Hist are connected at the root and are of one mind** — but individuals can
  isolate, sleep, go mad, be enslaved or be killed by the others.
- **Roots stretch "very far, deep into the heart of Nirn and out into distant
  lands"**, and **rootworms travel the tunnels those roots form** — the Underground
  Express reaches as far west as Gideon, whose stop is marked by red clay. So the
  root network demonstrably spans at least the interior and the western frontier.
- **Hist propagate by root and can be moved.** Lilmoth's second tree "grew from a
  single fragment of the root that survived the elder's killing more than 300
  years ago"; the Blackwood Company **smuggled an entire Hist to Leyawiin**;
  scam artists sell fake "Hist seeds" to gullible foreigners, which implies real
  ones are a known concept.
- **A tree's death produces a Mnemic Egg** holding all its and its people's
  memories.

`CANON_DERIVED`

- A settlement's Hist is a **hard constraint on that settlement's people**: gloor
  means the population's morphology, and therefore its craft, dress and reputation,
  follow the tree. Two settlements sharing a tree share a look.
- **A settlement with no Hist has no hatchery, no Naming Day, no soul-return and
  no grave rite that works.** That is not a minor absence; it defines the place.

## 2. The placement rule

`EXTRAPOLATED` — derived from the anchors above. Use this wherever a settlement
is placed and no canon tree is named.

### R1 — Density follows the interior gradient

| Zone | Hist density |
|---|---|
| **Middle Argonia / deep basin** | A continuous **forest**. Individual trees are not landmarks here; the *absence* of one is. |
| **Inner rim (outer central rim, Deepmire, deep Murkmire)** | Dense — effectively one per tribal territory, often several within a day. |
| **Regional interiors (inner Shadowfen, inland Murkmire, inland Blackwood)** | One per tribal settlement, and each is a **landmark**. |
| **Coasts, frontiers and cities** | Sparse and precious. Each is named, walled, tended, and politically significant. |
| **Beyond the roots** (foreign soil, deep sea islands) | None, and canon says the people there cannot hear the Hist at all (lukiul; the Izta-Ahuak of Solstice). |

### R2 — Four kinds of tree, and they behave differently

| Kind | Definition | Who speaks for it | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tribal Hist** | A tree with one tribe built around it. The default. | **tree-minder**; a **sap-speaker** if the tribe is pious enough to have earned one | Determines the tribe's gloor. Canon examples: Bright-Throat, Naga-Kur, Root-Whisper, Xit-Xaht, Archein villages. |
| **Hatchery Hist** | A tree that incubates eggs for a whole *region*, not one tribe. | **Keepers of the Shell** / egg-tenders, not a tree-minder | Canon example: the **Hatching Pools**, Shadowfen. Strategically the most important site in its region, and the first thing any enemy attacks. |
| **City tree** | A tree serving a mixed, non-tribal, often multiracial urban population. | Contested — a civic office, a tribal claimant, or nobody | Canon examples: **Lilmoth** (explicitly, despite no native tribe), **Stormhold**, **Stonewastes**. `EXTRAPOLATED`: city trees are the province's flashpoints, because a tree with no single tribe has no agreed mouth. This is exactly the quest plan's MR03 premise. |
| **Sealed / xanmeer Hist** | A tree enclosed in a xanmeer's Hist chamber. | Nobody, if the tribe is gone | Canon: safe from invaders but **separated from its own people**. A xanmeer whose tribe died contains a tree that has been alone for millennia. |

### R3 — Every tree has a state, and the state is the story

`EXTRAPOLATED` state vocabulary, each anchored on a canon instance:

| State | Canon precedent | What it means on the ground |
|---|---|---|
| **Speaking** | The norm | Communion works; eggs hatch; names are given; the dead return. |
| **Young / regrown** | Lilmoth's second tree, from a root fragment | Speaks faintly or not yet. A tribe with a young tree is in a decades-long interregnum. |
| **Sleeping** | Root-Whisper's **Dreaming Tree** | Alive, unreachable. Its people's souls are held elsewhere or lost. |
| **Isolated / rogue** | Lilmoth, twice (3E 173, 4E 8) | Cut off from the collective. Speaks to a *selected* few. Extremely dangerous — this state caused Umbriel. |
| **Mad** | Mazzatun's **Tsono-Xuhil**, weeping Amber Plasm | Gives orders that harm its own tribe. Neighbours know to stay away. |
| **Enslaved / tapped** | **Haj Uxith** (Molag Bal); the Blackwood Company's Leyawiin tree | Sap harvested industrially. The tribe feels the anguish. |
| **Dead** | Any Hist that dies | Leaves a **Mnemic Egg**. Its tribe's souls have nowhere to go and may linger mad unless a Hist-relic calls them back. |
| **Transplanted cutting** | The Blackwood Company's smuggled tree; quest-plan MR03 | Grows, but its provenance is a legal and spiritual claim someone else holds. |

### R4 — Absence must be justified, and is always political

`CANON_DERIVED` — "most, though not all" tribes have one. A settlement without a
Hist is one of:
- **foreign-founded** (Blackrose Prison, Hereguard Plantation, Slough Point,
  Castle Giovesse, the Imperial forts);
- **outlaw or transient** (Alten Corimont, Blackguard holdings, pirate camps);
- **lukiul or mixed-urban and dependent on a neighbour's tree** — the condition
  the League of Open Water exists to argue about;
- **bereaved** — its tree died or was taken, which is the strongest single
  backstory a ruined Argonian village can have.

### R5 — The root network is continuous, and it is traversable

`CANON_DERIVED` from rootworm travel reaching Gideon. Treat the Hist root system
as a **province-wide subsurface layer** connecting all speaking trees, with:
- **rootworm lines** along the major root trunks (the Underground Express), with a
  **wintertide southward migration** and known **bubble-spire exits**;
- **isolation as a discrete, rare, catastrophic condition** — a rogue tree has
  functionally severed itself from that layer;
- and therefore a diegetic reason why the interior is navigable to Argonians and
  impassable to everyone else.

### R6 — One tree, one look

`CANON_DERIVED` from gloor. When placing a distinct-looking population, place its
tree. When placing a tree, decide what its people look like. Traits fade with
distance and return on coming home, so **exiles and long-distance traders from a
tribe read as washed-out versions of their own kin** — a free, diegetic visual
marker for a character's relationship to home.

### The interior tribes

`EXTRAPOLATED` under R1/R6 — the Agaceph, Paatru and Sarpa each have their own
Hist, and their radically different morphologies (needle-faced and
rainbow-scaled; toad-like; winged) are canon proof that those trees' *gloor* are
strongly divergent and geographically close. Recommend the deep basin be authored
as **a small number of very old, very strange trees whose peoples look nothing
alike**, rather than a uniform forest — which is a far better realisation of the
canon forest than a repeated asset.

## 4. Lilmoth's tree — the open sub-question

Owner decision Q4 left one point open: is Lilmoth's third Hist **trusted, feared,
or quarantined?**

**Recommendation: feared, and tended anyway.** `EXTRAPOLATED`

Reasoning:
- **Quarantined is unavailable in practice.** A city cannot quarantine the tree
  its own eggs, names and dead depend on without ceasing to be an Argonian city.
  Isolating it is also precisely what went wrong last time: the An-Xileel's
  monopoly *was* the quarantine, and it produced Umbriel.
- **Trusted is not credible.** The same lineage has gone rogue **twice** — 3E 173
  and 4E 8 — and the second time killed the city. Canon establishes the pattern,
  and Argonians pay attention to patterns even if they do not dwell on the past.
- **Feared-and-tended** is the position canon's own culture produces: Argonians
  maintain the xanmeers "so that the mistakes of the past would never be
  repeated" — they do not destroy what frightens them, they **keep watch on it**.
  Applied to a Hist, that is a vigil.

Concretely: the tree is walled and open; anyone may approach; the tree-minder's
post rotates so that no one person ever holds sole communion again; a second
tender is always present when the first listens; and the city's law — such as it
is — forbids any single faction from claiming exclusive access. That last clause
is a **direct, legible, diegetic reaction to 4E 48** and is exactly the kind of
"institution visibly bent around the wound" the owner's Q5 addition calls for.

## 5. What this unblocks, and what it costs

- **Phase 11** can now write causal records for Argonian settlements: the tree,
  its kind, its state, its speaker and its people's gloor are all determinable.
- **Phase 12** gets a dungeon rule: a xanmeer with a living sealed Hist is a
  different place from one without, and only two or three exist.
- **Phase 13** gets an ecology anchor: red bioluminescent flowers as the standard,
  blue for the Naga-Kur, and root-tunnel systems beneath every speaking tree.
- **The quest plan** gets MR03 ("The Borrowed Tree") sited at Gideon with a canon
  mechanism, and the League of Open Water's "Citizen of No Hist" grounded in a
  province where Hist-less settlements are catalogued rather than hypothetical.
- **Cost**: the Gideon cutting, the Thorn field-edge tree, the Soulrest absence,
  the Blackrose drowned tree and the Rockpark death are `EXTRAPOLATED` — defensible
  and least-inventive, but they are our decisions and should be re-checked if
  UESP ever publishes 4E Black Marsh material.
