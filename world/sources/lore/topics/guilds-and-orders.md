# Guilds, orders and organisations in 4E 201

Closes gaps **G4.10** (Mages Guild / Synod / College of Whispers — quest-plan
sources L24–L26), **G4.11** (Thieves Guild / Dark Brotherhood), **G4.12**
(Cyrodilic Collections) and **G4.5** (the Wild Ones).

Sources: Lore:Mages Guild, Lore:Synod, Lore:College of Whispers,
Lore:Fighters Guild, Lore:Thieves Guild, Lore:Dark Brotherhood, Lore:An-Xileel,
Lore:Lilmoth, Lore:Hissmir, Lore:Alten Corimont, Lore:Archon, Lore:Thorn,
Lore:Soulrest, Lore:Cyrodilic Collections, Lore:Argonian, Lore:Ripper Eel
(fetched 2026-08-24).

## 1. The governing fact: a magical people with no magical institutions

`CANON_EXPLICIT`

- Argonians "are naturally adept spellcasters and excel in the art of stealth"
  (Lore:Argonian).
- "**Most simple day labourers in Black Marsh**" are "accomplished Illusionists" —
  two casual fishermen paralyse an Imperial clerk on the road without breaking
  conversation (The Argonian Account Bk 3).
- In the late 3E "some inhabitants of Thornmarsh would learn **basic magic just to
  help them survive** in the rough conditions" (Lore:Thorn).
- Marsh-born Argonians wield **Hist-magic**, which "doesn't operate on the same
  rules as conventional spellcraft" — a Keeper summons a cloud of incapacitating
  spores by invoking the mire.
- Argonian alchemists hold that "the moon phases dictate the precise positioning
  of calcinators"; **Kothringi spellcraft** was "unusual to the point that an
  experienced mage may miss its presence"; the ancient Argonians built magical
  defences still functional after millennia (**vakka stones**, **Zaht stones**,
  **keystones**, **Stormhold Crystals**).

`CANON_DERIVED` — **Magic in Argonia is folk literacy, not a profession.** The
guild-hall model — pay dues, learn spells, take a rank — is a foreign import that
answers a problem Argonians do not have. This is why every magical institution
canon places in the province is **foreign-founded** (Mages Guild halls, the
Conclaves, Cyrodilic Collections) and why none of them ever became native.

**Design consequence**: do not staff Argonia with mage NPCs in robes. Staff it
with tree-minders, sap-speakers, alchemists, grave-singers, Nisswo and ordinary
people who can paralyse you, and reserve robed institutional magic for the
handful of foreign remnants below. That is both canon-true and far more
distinctive.

## 2. The Mages Guild and its heirs — G4.10

### What was here, and when — `CANON_EXPLICIT`

Mages Guild halls are attested in Black Marsh at **Archon**, **Thorn**,
**Soulrest**, **Hissmir** and — remarkably — even at the smuggler port of **Alten
Corimont**. The **Fighters Guild** likewise kept halls at **Hissmir** and **Alten
Corimont**. All of these are **Second Era** attestations.

**The Mages Guild dissolved at the start of the Fourth Era**, splitting into the
**Synod** and the **College of Whispers**, Imperial-sanctioned rivals who compete
for the favour of the Elder Council.

### The hard datum — `CANON_EXPLICIT`

> "The **Synod had no conclave in Lilmoth, nor any presence at all for roughly
> 400 miles**." — Lore:Lilmoth, of the 4E 40s

Four hundred miles from Lilmoth covers essentially the entire province. This is
the single most decisive fact we have about magical institutions in 4E Argonia,
and it says: **there were none.**

### The Synod — `CANON_EXPLICIT` + `EXTRAPOLATED`

Canon: continued the Guild's **ban on necromancy** and banned at least some
Conjuration; does **not** share knowledge freely (years of dues might earn you a
spell); headed by a **Grand Council**; ranks include **Attendant** and **First
Adjunct**; the **Binder's Conclave** is one of its divisions; uses a distinctive
**encryption system** built on Tamrielic symbols where a passage's key lies in the
next passage and larger blocks stand for whole ideas — a spell, an artefact, an
energy. Helped defend the Imperial City from Umbriel c. 4E 48. In **4E 201** it is
trying to use the **Oculory of Mzulft** to locate artefacts of great magical
power, and is embittered toward the College of Winterhold.

**And the connection that matters most to us**: *"**Hierem**, the former minister
of Emperor Titus Mede I, was a member of the Synod, and reportedly held vast
influence over it."* Hierem is the man who made the **secret trip to Black Marsh
in 4E 47** to treat with the An-Xileel and sealed the deal with a ritual at the
Lilmoth city tree involving the word **"Umbriel"**.

`CANON_DERIVED`: **the Synod's most influential patron personally set the Umbriel
catastrophe in motion.** Not the institution — one man, secretly, using its
methods and its access. That is a canon-supplied, era-appropriate, deniable link
between an Imperial magical order and the worst day in Argonian history, and it
requires no invention whatever.

`EXTRAPOLATED` 4E 201 position: the Synod has **no chapter, no hall and no
standing** in Argonia, and would be extremely unwelcome if it announced itself.
What it has is **interest**: an artefact-hunting order whose declared 4E 201
project is locating objects of great magical power, in a province full of
vakka stones, keystones, Zaht stones, Stormhold Crystals, Mnemic Eggs and a
priceless king's jewel. Recommend it operate through **buyers, commissions and
third parties** — which is exactly how it would behave anyway, and gives the
world a hostile institutional interest with no visible institution.

### The College of Whispers — `CANON_EXPLICIT` + `EXTRAPOLATED`

Canon: Imperially sanctioned; **summons Daedra and practises necromancy**; had
"the most up-to-date information on Umbriel", reporting that its "wormies" were
more akin to flesh atronachs than to bonewalkers or zombies but did not respond
to the same arcane stimuli; a cynosure at Water's Edge.

`EXTRAPOLATED` 4E 201 position: also absent, but its interest is **specific and
worse**. It is the surviving institution that studied Umbriel most closely, in a
province where Umbriel's dead still lie under the Murkmire mud and in the lower
levels of Blackrose Prison. A College scholar seeking Umbrielic remains in
Argonia is doing exactly what the College does, and is committing — from the
Argonian side — grave desecration on a mass-grave scale. **Recommended as the
province's most legible foreign magical antagonist**, ahead of the Thalmor,
because their motive is scholarly rather than genocidal and therefore harder to
refuse cleanly.

Note also: **Argonians buried by Hist rites receive no Arkay's Blessing**, and
foreign necromancers usually avoid the province only because corpses rot too
fast. Umbriel's dead are the exception — preserved, animated, and already proven
to work. `CANON_DERIVED`, and quietly horrifying.

### The Fighters Guild — `EXTRAPOLATED`

Canon: chartered **by the Emperor** to regulate mercenaries and protect commerce;
by the Fourth Era it had **no active chapters in Skyrim**, its work absorbed by a
local institution (the Companions); its Interregnum **Council of Province
Generals** seated only five provinces, and Black Marsh was not among them.

`EXTRAPOLATED` 4E 201: the charter died with the Empire's authority here, and the
function was absorbed by native institutions that were already doing it — the
**Four Winds of Stonewastes**, city **shellback** levies, tribal **kaals**, and
whoever a village hires to deal with a wamasu. The old Hissmir and Alten Corimont
halls are lodging and warehouse.

This is precisely the space the quest plan's **Marsh Charter** line occupies, and
canon supports it well: a martial tradition arguing about whether to accept a
charter is arguing about whether to become foreign.

### The Conclaves and the cult of Seth — `CANON_EXPLICIT` (thin) + `EXTRAPOLATED`

Canon names religious houses in the province's cities without explaining them:
the **Conclave of Baal** (Stormhold — which can locate Murkwood by reading the
Elder Scrolls with an ancient tablet), the **Conclave of the One** (Archon and
Thorn), the **Conclave of Riana** (Thorn), the **Conclave of Charity**
(Glenbridge), the **Brotherhood of Seth** (Soulrest), and Gideon as "the **black
city** of the followers of **Seth**", who "held vast control over the city" in the
late 3E while "a foreboding chill engulfed the town".

`EXTRAPOLATED`: these are **foreign or syncretic urban cults**, not Argonian
religion — Argonians "do not formally recognise or worship any type of Tamrielic
deity". Recommend treating them as **the religious life of the colonial cities**,
now two centuries orphaned: small congregations, decayed halls, one or two that
still matter locally. The **Conclave of Baal at Stormhold is the exception and
should survive**, because canon gives it a unique, useful and entirely
non-transferable function — it is the only body in Tamriel that can find
Murkwood, and it needs Elder Scrolls access and a specific tablet to do it. That
is a quest-giver, a library and a dungeon key in one.

The **Seth** cult is the province's best unclaimed religious hook: canon gives it
a city ("the black city"), a period of dominance, a chill, a brotherhood at
Soulrest, and no doctrine at all.

## 3. Crime and the shadow — G4.11

### The Thieves Guild — `EXTRAPOLATED`

Canon: the Guild "operates along provincial lines, with **little if any apparent
coordination** between provincial chapters", each with its own style; and
crucially, "**Guilds in some of the provinces have been unheard of for centuries,
though it's unclear whether it is because they no longer exist or if it's because
they are very, very good.**"

`EXTRAPOLATED` 4E 201: there is no *Imperial* Thieves Guild in Argonia, and there
never really was. What exists is native and canon-attested:

- **Pusbottom**, Lilmoth — "the ancient, gangrenous heart of Lilmoth", where the
  criminal underground operates and where the An-Xileel's political enemies hid.
  `CANON_EXPLICIT`
- **Alten Corimont** — pirates and smugglers, institutionally neutral.
  `CANON_EXPLICIT`
- **The Blackguards** — Murkmire's largest criminal organisation, prison-descended,
  highwaymen. `CANON_EXPLICIT`
- **Tribeless Naga** — "the majority of the native brigands and highwaymen".
  `CANON_EXPLICIT`
- **The mangrove river** — Bramman's concealed channel from Topal Bay to the
  Blackrose lakes. `CANON_EXPLICIT`

The quest plan's **Night-Reed Chapter** should therefore be read as a *native*
organisation that a Cyrodiilic guild would recognise as a peer, not a franchise.
That is more distinctive and it is what canon implies.

**A canon detail worth building a heist around**: in Lilmoth, **ripper eels are
trained to hunt Argonians illegally crossing the canals** — "though they do not
attack people who have rubbed themselves with **eel-slime**" (Lore:Ripper Eel).
A city whose security system is a trained animal with a known, disgusting,
obtainable counter is a gift to stealth design, and it is canon.

### The Dark Brotherhood — `CANON_EXPLICIT` + `EXTRAPOLATED`

Canon: "The next two hundred years did not treat the Brotherhood well." The
**Great War** strained it; riots in Bravil, Bruma and Cheydinhal became serious
problems; sanctuaries across Tamriel were destroyed or evacuated; its presence in
whole provinces was eliminated. Its Argonian arm, the **Shadowscales**, lost the
Archon facility in **4E 187**, and the last known living Shadowscale dies in
**4E 201**.

`EXTRAPOLATED` 4E 201: the Brotherhood's only route into Argonia was the
Shadowscale pipeline, and that pipeline is closed. Its live interest is
**reopening Archon** — canon names the Listener (**Alisanne Dupre**, Cheydinhal)
and an associate (**Rasha**) as wanting exactly that. Recommend the Brotherhood
appear in Argonia as **an outside party trying to restart a school in a country
that has moved on**, negotiating with the Black-Tongues, with the state, and with
whoever now claims the *ku-vastei* role. That is the quest plan's **Empty Cradle**
premise with a canon-named antagonist already in place.

Full treatment of the order itself:
[sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md](sithis-nisswo-shadowscales.md).

## 4. Cyrodilic Collections — G4.12

`CANON_EXPLICIT`: an **Imperial** organisation dedicated to retrieving, restoring
and **returning Argonian antiquities to Black Marsh**, funded by benefactors, with
headquarters at **Lilmoth** and an alcove at **Stormhold**; it dealt directly with
local tribes, hired adventurers, published scholarship (*Crafting Motif 70: Elder
Argonian Style*; *On Voriplasms*) and hoped to open **a museum in Black Marsh**.
It uncovered the Root-Whisper mystery and helped resurrect that tribe. It was
also betrayed from within by **Kassandra**, a Dunmer-raised Argonian who had never
been able to hear the Hist and hoped a relic would give her what her birth had
denied her.

`EXTRAPOLATED` 4E 201: **revive it.** Reasoning:

- It is the **only foreign programme in the whole corpus whose stated purpose an
  Argonian government could endorse**, which makes it the natural legal cover for
  any foreign presence our world needs.
- Its Lilmoth headquarters was in the intact Imperial buildings near the walls —
  it was in the city when Umbriel came, and 4E 48 would have destroyed it. A
  refounded Collections that has spent a century and a half trying to recover
  what it lost *and what the province lost* is dramatically strong and requires
  no invention beyond continuity.
- The **museum** was never built. A foreign society still trying to build a museum
  of Argonian history in Argonia, in a country whose religion is about **letting
  go of the past**, is a genuine and interesting argument in which neither side is
  wrong. That is worth more than another hostile faction.
- Kassandra's betrayal gives the modern chapter an institutional scar and a
  standing rule about Hist-less members.

Recommend siting the refounded Collections at **Gideon** rather than Lilmoth:
Gideon is the province's records city, it kept its Cyrodiilic legal habits, its
Lukiul majority makes it the one place a foreign scholarly society can operate
without constant friction, and it is where the archives that survived would be.

## 5. The Wild Ones — G4.5

### What canon says — `CANON_EXPLICIT`, in full

Two mentions, both about Lilmoth:

> "Only the **An-Xileel and wild Argonians** have complete access to the Lilmoth
> city-tree." — Lore:An-Xileel
>
> "The rogue Hist stopped talking to the people of Lilmoth by 4E 8; it only spoke
> to the **An-Xileel and the Wild Ones**." — Lore:Hist
>
> "The An-Xileel and the **Wild Ones** went away, intending to come back when the
> ordeal was all over and the Imperial taint had been scrubbed from the province."
> — Lore:Argonian

That is everything. No definition, no leader, no territory, no fate.

### The least inventive reading — `EXTRAPOLATED`

Lore:An-Xileel's phrasing — "**wild Argonians**", lower case — and Lore:Hist's
"**the Wild Ones**", capitalised, are the same referent. So:

**The Wild Ones are not an organisation. They are a category that the An-Xileel
turned into a constituency.**

They are the un-assimilated, marsh-born, Hist-hearing Argonians of the deep
interior and the tribal territories — everyone who is the opposite of *lukiul*.
The An-Xileel gave them a capitalised name, claimed to speak for them, counted
them as a bloc, and in 4E 48 announced that they would "go away" with them and
return when the province was clean.

Reasoning for preferring this over an invented secret society:
1. It is what the words say, in both cases, with the lower-case instance
   effectively glossing the upper-case one.
2. It explains why the term appears **only** in An-Xileel contexts and never
   elsewhere — it is *their* word, exactly as **Lukiul** is their word
   (`CANON_EXPLICIT`: the An-Xileel coined Lukiul). A movement that names one
   category to despise names its opposite to claim.
3. It requires no new institution in a province canon shows as institutionally
   thin.

### What this gives us in 4E 201 — `EXTRAPOLATED`

- **The state claims a constituency that has never agreed to be claimed.** The
  successor administration inherited the An-Xileel's rhetoric along with its
  offices, and still speaks of "the Wild Ones" as its base. The deep-interior
  tribes it means have opinions about that, and mostly the opinion is
  indifference — "you're in the middle of everywhere, and near nowhere."
- **It is now also a slur, in both directions.** In the cities, calling someone a
  Wild One means backward and dangerous. In the interior, accepting the name means
  accepting the An-Xileel's frame. Most tribes simply give their own name instead.
- **A small number of people took the name seriously.** `EXTRAPOLATED` and flagged:
  the plainest consequence of "they went away, intending to come back when it was
  clean" is that **some of them are still waiting**, three generations on, in
  interior communities founded on the promise of a purified province that never
  arrived. A settlement of these is a superb, sad, non-villainous antagonist
  faction: people whose parents' parents were told to wait, and who are still
  keeping the vigil, and who are now confronted with a province that rebuilt
  itself full of exactly the people they withdrew from.

`AGENT_INVENTED` flag: the waiting communities are ours. The category reading is
`EXTRAPOLATED` and safe; the surviving vigil-settlements are an invention on top
of it and should be owner-visible. Raised as **Round 2 Q6** in
[../extrapolation/owner-questions.md](../extrapolation/owner-questions.md).

## 7. Build implications

- **Argonia has almost no guild content, and that is the point.** Where other
  provinces offer halls to join, Argonia offers tribes to be adopted by, a
  priesthood with no doctrine, an assassin order in its last year, and a foreign
  museum project nobody asked for. Lean into it.
- **Every foreign institution in the province is either extinct, absent, or
  operating through intermediaries.** That is a consistent, canon-supported rule
  and it keeps the world feeling closed without needing border guards.
- **The two live foreign magical interests are artefact-hunting (Synod) and
  Umbrielic necromancy (College of Whispers)**, and both are deniable. Neither
  needs a chapter house.
- **The Hierem link is free and excellent**: the Imperial minister who set Umbriel
  in motion was the Synod's most influential patron. Any Argonian who knows this —
  and the Veiled Reed's records would — has a permanent reason to refuse the
  Synod anything.
