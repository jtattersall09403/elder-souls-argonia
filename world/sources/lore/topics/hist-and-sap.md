# The Hist

Sources: Lore:Hist, Lore:Hist Sap, Lore:Argonian, Lore:Lilmoth, Lore:Mazzatun,
Lore:Deepmire, Lore:Duskfall, Lore:Xanmeer, Lore:Hissmir, Lore:Hatching Pools
(fetched 2026-08-24).

## What the Hist are — CANON_EXPLICIT

- Giant **sapient spore trees** of the innermost swamps, "more ancient than all
  the races of man and mer". Argonians claim they were **the original life forms
  on Tamriel**. They acknowledge **Sithis** as the Original Creator.
- Bystanders in the Ehlnofey war; **most of their realm was destroyed** and sunk
  into the Padomaic, "leaving only a small corner left standing, later known as
  Black Marsh".
- **All Hist are connected at the root — one mind.** "Usually, when speaking
  directly to the Hist, what one says is what they all say." Roots stretch "deep
  into the heart of Nirn and out into distant lands". Sap from deep roots is
  **deep sap**.
- But **individual Hist can isolate themselves**, go mad, or go rogue — and the
  collective can **kill one of its own** (Lilmoth, 3E 173).
- Life stages give the Argonian calendar its middle months: **Hist-Deek**
  (sapling), **Hist-Dooka** (mature), **Hist-Tsoko** (elder).
- Hist usually bear **red bioluminescent flowers**; the Naga-Kur's bears blue.
- A **Hist-Tsoko may mark an individual** for a sacred task: wooden skin, veins
  glowing amber. Sap-speakers know to aid them.

## Sap — CANON_EXPLICIT

- **A Hist's sap is its soul.** When an Argonian is created the sap becomes their
  blood, essence and soul; their spirit is made of it and returns to it at death.
- Licking sap lets the Hist communicate in **visions**; it **increases combat
  prowess**; it can **alter living organisms** (the likely origin of Argonians and
  of the lizard-steeds).
- **Overdose is dangerous even for Argonians.** Miredancer sap-speakers suffer
  **sap-poisoning**: "gold tongue" (permanent golden mouth pigmentation), "bark
  scale" (thickened, darkened scales), unwanted hallucinations.
- **On non-Argonians it is a hallucinogen that induces dangerous bloodlust**
  (Blackwood Company, late 3E). Mistreat the tree and the sap does the same to
  Argonians.
- **Amber Plasm** looks like sap but is not: it is **chaotic creatia from Oblivion
  leaking into Mundus through the Hist like blood from a wound**. Mazzatun's mad
  Hist has wept it since time immemorial; ingesting or bathing in it drives you
  mad, and Tree-Minder Na-Kesh learned to use it to make her people **grow faster
  and stronger**.
- Industrial use is attested and always villainous: the **Blackwood Company**
  smuggled a whole Hist to Leyawiin and built an extraction mechanism; the
  Aldmeri Dominion **boiled sap** and fused it with a Dremora's essence to make
  **skin-stealers**; Molag Bal ran a **mechanical sap-collection facility** at
  Haj Uxith. Hist-sap **rubber** is a mundane Murkmire product (insulated
  wamasu prods).

## Gloor: why Argonians differ tribe to tribe — CANON_EXPLICIT

An Argonian's appearance and physiology are **almost solely determined by their
Hist**, via the sap ingested as a hatchling on their **Naming Day**. Argonians are
"adaptable-by-induction to their Hist's **gloor** — the pervasive will/desire/need
of the Hist to engender multiple inevitabilities."

- Ancestry does not matter: a hatchling raised under a tribe's Hist will show
  **that tribe's** traits. The **Veeskhleel (Ghost People)** are all infertile and
  perpetuate themselves entirely by **stealing eggs** and hatching them under
  their own Hist — and every Veeskhleel looks alike regardless.
- Traits **fade with distance** from the tribe's Hist and **return** on coming home.
- Argonians living away from sunlight lose their scale colour.
- Argonians can **ask their Hist to change their gender**, usually followed by a
  celebration.
- → **Gloor is the mechanism that makes regional Argonian variety legitimate.**
  Every distinct-looking population we place needs a Hist to explain it, and every
  Hist we place licenses a distinct-looking population. This is our single best
  tool for cultural density: tribe = Hist = morphology = craft = danger profile.

## Reproduction and the hatchery — CANON_EXPLICIT

Eggs go to an **uxith** (nest), tended by **egg-tenders / egg-nurses** (Shadowfen:
the **Keepers of the Shell**), who read nursery rhymes and play flutes to them,
crush biting bugs, and log every egg — parents, hatch date, **star sign**. Signs of
an unhealthy egg: dry shell, no lustre, thin membrane, an empty shadow. An egg not
destined to hatch **sinks into the roots and fades away**. **If the Hist's
connection to the eggs is broken, the unborn die swiftly** — which is exactly the
weapon the Dominion tried at the Hatching Pools.

Argonians in cold provinces or small nomadic bands tend to **live birth**; warm,
stable, permanent communities lay eggs in numbers.

### Silence vs severance — keep the two mechanisms distinct

Canon gives two different failures of the bond, with different consequences:

- **Silence/withdrawal** — the Hist stop *speaking*. The post-Duskfall silence
  lasted an indeterminate time and canon records **no egg die-off** from it
  (`CANON_EXPLICIT`; Lore:Duskfall, Lore:Hist).
- **Severance of the egg-connection** — the link between a Hist and its unborn
  is *cut*, and "the unborn die swiftly": the weapon the Dominion built from
  the **Mnemic Egg** at the Hatching Pools (`CANON_EXPLICIT`; Lore:Hatching
  Pools, Lore:Hist).

The game's opening image — withdrawal *and* a dead clutch the same night — is
therefore `CANON_DERIVED`, not explicit: silence alone would not kill a clutch.
The project's causal reading is that **the cult's harvest interference severs
the egg link** (the Mnemic-Egg mechanism), with the withdrawal as the visible
symptom; quest prose must attribute the deaths to the severance, not the
silence.

## Communion, and who can do it — CANON_EXPLICIT

- Marsh-born Argonians **hear** the Hist: hands on bark or resting beneath the
  boughs brings whispers, songs, faint smells, soothing tones.
- **Argonians hatched outside Black Marsh cannot hear the Hist at all.** These are
  the **lukiul** ("assimilated"). They struggle to read marsh-born body language,
  are treated as outsiders, and are pitied by those who never left. The term was
  coined — and is used as a slur — by the **An-Xileel**.
- **Hissmir is canon's answer**: its **Trials of the Burnished Scales** let
  Hist-less Argonians *and even non-Argonians* speak with the Hist. Lukiul pilgrims
  come from all over Tamriel to find "that missing piece of themselves".
- Some tribes have a **sap-speaker**, a direct intermediary who spends days or
  weeks among the roots eating the tree's otherwise-forbidden fruit — "Saxhleel
  blessed by the Hist with second-eyes and second-ears".
- Communication styles vary by tree: the **Bright-Throat** Hist speaks **through
  the wind**, so the tribe hangs chimes to hear it; others send **dreams**.

## The Hist as political actor — CANON_EXPLICIT

This is the fact most relevant to our era. The Hist have repeatedly acted, and
their actions have repeatedly gone wrong:

1. **Pre-Duskfall**: forewarned of a cataclysm and **disagreed among themselves**.
   One sold its tribe to Molag Bal. Mazzatun's told its people to build forever.
2. **Post-Duskfall**: **stopped speaking to Argonians altogether** for an
   indeterminate time until Jaraleet found a way back in.
3. **3E 433**: foresaw the Oblivion Crisis, **called Argonians home from across the
   Empire** and physically **rebuilt their bodies for war**.
4. **3E 173 and 4E 8**: Lilmoth's tree went rogue twice; the second time it spoke
   only to the **An-Xileel and the Wild Ones**, took counsel from no other Hist,
   and used them to **summon Umbriel**. The An-Xileel sang to it "in a long, awful
   cacophonous chant".
5. The Hist claim they can invoke the **"Songs of Sithis"** to "bring the Void"
   when driven to vengeance, making transgressors "taste fear, then nothing".

→ The Hist are neither a benign hive nor a monolith. They are **a council of
ancient individuals that can be isolated, poisoned, maddened, enslaved, killed
and lied to**, and one of them nearly destroyed Tamriel a century and a half
before our era. Any project material about coordination between Hist (e.g. the
Root of Accord) sits comfortably inside that, provided it is **limited,
voluntary and contestable** — which is how the quest plan already frames it.

## Artefacts that touch the bond — CANON_EXPLICIT

| Artefact | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Mnemic Egg** | A Hist's death "births it backwards" into a seed of pulsing sap holding all that tree's and its people's memories. Because the Hist share one mind, the Egg is **a gateway into the whole collective** — and in the wrong hands, **a tool for severing the Argonian–Hist connection**. Used by the Dominion in 2E 582 to stop a generation of eggs hatching. |
| **Remnant of Argon** | Amber vessel made by the Root-Whisper Hist at the cost of its own waking life, holding an entire tribe's souls. Freeing them required someone to stay behind and die. |
| **Rootmender's Staff** | Leads directly to the Remnant. |
| **Vakka Stones** | Magic stones that power pre-Duskfall relics and defences; also used to open portals. |
| **Hist amber** | Exceptionally rare Hist "fruit" of solid sap; used to carve the **Zaht stones** that defend Hissmir. The Hist no longer produce it. |
| **Tribal Hist relics** | A staff made from the tribe's own Hist branch can **call the tribe's spirits and the tree's stolen sap back**, letting the souls pass on and the tree survive. |

## Build implications

- **Every settlement needs its Hist answered for.** Canon is explicit that even
  Lilmoth, which has *no native tribe*, has a city tree — and that tree's history
  is the city's history. "Which Hist, what state is it in, who speaks for it" is
  a required field in every Argonian settlement's causal record.
- Hist placement is a **hard spatial constraint on tribes**: gloor means a
  distinct-looking tribe cannot exist far from a distinct Hist, and traits fade
  with distance — a natural, diegetic reason for tribal territories to be compact
  and for exiles to look washed-out.
- Sap gives us a **fully canonical consumable economy**: legitimate ritual use,
  medical/industrial use (rubber, prods), a black market to outsiders (bloodlust
  hallucinogen), and an atrocity (boiling). Smuggling sap is the single most
  lore-appropriate crime in the province.
- Underwater/root traversal: Hist roots form the **rootworm tunnels** of the
  Underground Express, so the root network is literally a traversable layer.
