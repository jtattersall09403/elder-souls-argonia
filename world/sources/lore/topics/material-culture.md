# Argonian material culture — building, craft, food, dress, sport, calendar

The reference for anything an agent has to *place*: what a hut is made of, what
is on a table, what a village sounds like. Sources: Lore:Argonian,
The Improved Emperor's Guide to Tamriel/Black Marsh (2E 581), Lore:Xanmeer,
Lore:Xit-Xaht, Lore:Bright-Throat Village, Lore:Jel, Lore:Jasper,
Lore:Lizard-Steed, Lore:Rootworm, The Argonian Account (fetched 2026-08-24);
boats: Lore:Sea Transportation, Lore:Salt-Cured, Lore:Tribes of Blackwood:
Riverbacks, Lore:Pirates of Topal Bay, Lore:Lost Tales of the Famed Explorer,
Online:Kothringi Tidal Canoe, Online:Tide-Born Boat, Online:Argonian Houseboat
(fetched 2026-08-28).

## Building — CANON_EXPLICIT

- **The doctrine**: modern Argonians build to be replaced, deliberately, as a
  religious rejection of *shunatei*. Stone is a moral error. They still maintain
  the old xanmeers as warnings.
- **Shadowfen**: **mud huts** — wattle-and-daub over an exposed log skeleton;
  walls sweat in humidity; a strong odour; dry excreta reported in the window
  sealing. Argonians "really like this sun-blessed mud".
- **Murkmire**: settlements **woven from reeds**, on **wooden stilts**, with
  platforms that "sometimes extend high into the air", connecting to one another
  and ascending from ground to hover above water and marsh. **Imperial houses
  built on the same soil sank.**
- **Totems** everywhere: wood, mud and plumage, "rearing up like a malformed
  golem"; crocodile skulls (not lizard-folk skulls). At Hutan-Tzel the totems
  carry runes only that tribe knows and can attract dangerous creatures if
  meddled with.
- **Causeways**: flexible, partially buoyant **timber causeways**; and older
  **stone-flagged causeways** between settlements (Stormhold→Bogmother), firmer
  than a Breton road.
- **Xanmeers**: stepped pyramids linked by **stone bridges**; interiors
  deceptively large and mazelike, full of traps and relics; some powered by
  **vakka stones** with defences still working after millennia. **Each xanmeer had
  a chamber housing its tribe's Hist** — protecting it from invaders while
  separating it from its people. Mechanisms: **bijum-style door release** (push
  the lever to open; *pull* it and the floor collapses and drops you deeper) and
  the **tutan-wei pulley-puzzle** (three columns of symbols per side, counterweights
  levelled to match plaques in the middle of the room). **Flint Vine** — a hardy
  creeper of caves and poorly-lit xanmeers, strong enough to haul heavy objects
  or be made into a rope-ladder.
- **Villages move.** Most Argonian villages "don't stay put for very long"; a
  floating village may drift to a city's gates over a month or two. Alten
  Meerhleel is described as a floating village.

## Craft and materials — CANON_EXPLICIT

- **Post-Duskfall metal use is almost unheard of.** Pre-Duskfall smiths used
  volcanic glass, layered cloth, leather, bone and bronze, adorned with feathers,
  dyed yarns, jewels, bronze emblems and gold.
- Working materials attested: **jadeflint** and **obverdian** (green-tinted
  stones), **bog iron**, steel with a **rough copper alloy edge**, **tamarack
  wood**, **wamasu hide** (hardened or made flexible as needed by ancient
  technique), **crocodile armour**, turtle shell (dishes, shield bosses, whole
  shields), giant-snake hide (to protect delicate textiles), bone, horn, chitin,
  glass, clay, silver, mother-of-pearl.
- **Slug shaping**: place **Jassa Red** slugs on wood or stone and flash flint
  behind them; threatened, they squirt acidic mucus, and steering them with the
  flint carves long smooth channels. A genuinely alien smithing technique.
- **Bone-workers**: specialised **bronze** tools for carving bone, horn and chitin
  — knife, pick and chisel in one.
- Argonians are "masters of crafting jewellery"; polished **blue jaspers** are the
  characteristic stone (Kothringi folklore says **bog-blue jaspers** come from the
  gizzards of geels-ha butcher eels). **Neesak of Scrying**: a multi-faceted green
  swamp crystal used for divination, "not uncommon in Murkmire".
- **Sturdy weaving** of grass and fibre — secrets closely guarded. **Ceramic** is
  commonplace; the **red clay of the Onkobra delta** makes distinctive pottery.
- Argonian alchemists hold that **moon phases dictate the precise positioning of
  calcinators**.
- **Naga-Kur**: weapons and armour from **the bodies of their own dead** — scales,
  hide and bone; frills and spines as decoration; bone handles and heads. Crafting
  a weapon from a fallen warrior is an honour. Their **grave-singer** harvests the
  parts. They do **not** bind spirits into the weapons.
- **Xit-Xaht**: bone plate armour (as effective as steel scale) with a deliberately
  **exposed midriff**, loincloths, sandals or bare feet, layered helms whose layer
  count denotes status; swords that double as machetes and saws; Hist-branch
  staves topped with a skull resembling the wielder; thrown weapons over bows.

## Boats and waterway travel — CANON_EXPLICIT

- **The small-draft doctrine.** Black Marsh's terrain forces travel "through the
  numerous waterways of the region, only navigable by **small-draft ships**,
  which are and have always been **the most common and the fastest form of
  transportation, both of people and goods, inside Argonia**." Argonians rarely
  *build* big ships — they crew and sail **other races' ships, commonly Dunmer
  or Imperial**. Argonian captains are "famous as pirates, privateers and
  smugglers"; coastal *and inland* ports share "a long tradition of sea
  sailing". Port tiers: **Archon, Lilmoth, Soulrest** major seaports;
  **Alten Corimont, Stormhold, Thorn** minor. (Lore:Sea Transportation)
- **Personal rafts are the default vehicle.** When Imperial roads and caravans
  collapsed, "the Argonians began once again to use **the old ways, their
  personal rafts** and sometimes the Underground Express [rootworms] to
  transport the grain in small quantities" (The Argonian Account, Bk 4). The
  Agaceph pole a **razor-thin one-person raft steered and propelled by the
  tail** (Bk 4; see tribes.md).
- **Riverbacks (Naka-Desh, Blackwood, 2E attestation):** their territory is
  "more water than land — a drowned marsh navigable by **small rafts, canoes,
  and little else**"; outsiders arrive by **ferry boats** behind an Argonian
  guide; they fish *from rafts* with **harnessed osheeja gars** (bridled
  predator fish that snatch the catch and cannot swallow it) instead of hook
  and line. (Lore:Tribes of Blackwood: Riverbacks)
- **Tide-Born / Salt-Cured (Solstice, 2E attestation):** fisher Argonians who
  live on the water in "**large twin-hulled canoes, with living platforms
  spanning the space between the hulls**", keep a loose reef flotilla, follow
  fish into deep water, and run **smaller boats** ashore to trade; they sleep
  in **houseboats** even through storms because dreams borne on the tide carry
  omens. Shallow-water fishing boats go out at first light. (Lore:Salt-Cured;
  Online:Argonian Houseboat; Online:Tide-Born Boat)
- **The Kothringi tidal canoe:** "unusual paddle placement, **shallow draft,
  and a sail**" — a sleek hull with a "colorful **doubled sail**" able to
  navigate **both swamps and coastal waters** (Online:Kothringi Tidal Canoe;
  Lore:Kothringi; more in lost-peoples.md). The one *named* native boat type
  canon gives the province.
- **Guided river travel:** multi-day raft journeys behind a hired Argonian
  guide who chooses when to move (sometimes at night), where to shelter, and
  refuses to stop — predators (a Swamp Leviathan) trail boats; passengers
  stand and balance. (Lore:Lost Tales of the Famed Explorer: Fragment III)
- **Piracy afloat:** a 2E pirate flotilla of "three or four fast galleys" hid
  "in the channels of the **Onkobra River's many mouths** on the west coast";
  eastern Topal Bay shipping detoured to avoid it. (Lore:Pirates of Topal Bay)
- **Negative finding (searched 2026-08-28):** canon nowhere attests
  grown/organic/Hist-grown boats, nor any native Argonian boat-class names.
  Any "living boat" or named class we use is EXTRAPOLATED/AGENT_INVENTED.

## Food and drink — CANON_EXPLICIT

Largely **raw**: fish and swamp plants, seasoned with spices and rich sauces and
eaten uncooked; maggots and fermented pond scum are normal ingredients.
Named items: **trodh** (tiny crunchy fish eaten *alive* — cooked they are deadly
poison), **lumberries** (a black berry, harvested Last Seed), **phlegmwine**
(served to Bright-Throat bonding guests), **Hissmir Fish-Eye Rye** (drunk with the
nostrils plugged), **xanmeer brandy**, pickled frog's eyes (a delicacy; also
settles a sour stomach), licorice worms (hatchling food), hardened Hist-sap
pacifiers for teething, saltrice (a Morrowind crop grown at Thorn).
Gideon's Argonians "know what sort of swamp cuisine [outsiders] can stomach and
cater to them accordingly" — the one place a foreigner can eat comfortably.

## Drugs and medicine — CANON_EXPLICIT

- **Daril** ("seeing everything in ecstasy"): from **moon adder venom**,
  instantly fatal to others but transformed by an agent in Argonian blood. Users
  "see sounds, hear tastes, smell sights"; it unfolds in stages. Fermented in a
  swamp-jelly gas bladder; some coat quills in it and insert them around the eyes.
  Ancient, powerful, dangerous, very expensive, **fatal to non-Argonians**.
- Argonian poison resistance makes them **resistant to alcohol and drugs**
  generally; Bright-Throat elders smoke **dried marshweed**.
- **Moon sugar** is smuggled north-to-south through the province by outsiders.
- Field medicine: **dried flower buds as water purifiers**, **depth sticks** to
  sound a pond before wading, a **fleshfly emollient**, chewed dried bark for
  headaches.
- **Grooming is a whole material category**: palm-oil spine balms, spine polish,
  clay lotion, scale lacquer, kwama-wax creams (herbal Argonian moisturisers
  *irritate every other race's skin*), parasols against drying sun, small metal
  scraping blades, rigid under-scale brushes, clam-shell claw files, horn
  whetstones, hollow-centred tooth stones, fin polish, ridge liner, horn polish
  (gold in the expensive kinds), bladder-aged "snake sweat" perfume, and
  **Corimont Mouth-Plovers** kept by high-status Argonians to clean their fangs.
  Argonians moult and sometimes need thick brushes to shed.

## Dress and adornment — CANON_EXPLICIT

Tail bands of coloured leather, sometimes with bells; **tail stockings with
pockets for hot stones**; spines and plumes; **copper horn caps** (a 2E "capping"
trend); **silver frill clips** (a lukiul fashion); **betva-hen feathers** on
naheesh elders; **crocodile-tooth necklaces** for maturity trials. Unruly Gideon
teenagers fashion their head crests into multiple spikes; Thornmarsh coastal
fashion is the **Four-Spike Wattle**.

## Music, sport and play — CANON_EXPLICIT

- **Vossa-satl** ("frog pipes"): a polished wooden clam-shell with valved
  chambers, **a live frog in each**, sizes from jaw-harp to pipe-organ. Tuned by
  a key that alters chamber size; the player squirts **frog musk** in before
  performing to make them sing; the happier and better-fed the frogs, the better
  the music. Frog-catchers select frogs for tone — the Panther River's chirping
  frogs are ideal. Nearly impossible for foreign musicians.
- **Teeba-Hatsei** ("Hip and Tail Ball") — no hands or feet; hips, elbows and
  tails only. **Teeba-Enoo** — ritual and sport at once, with rules that are
  always changing and confuse even Argonians; important enough that building a
  Teeba-Enoo court is how you placate offended tribes (Alten Meerhleel).
  **Shells and Stones**, a board game on a marked reed mat.
- Archon's games: **Jump Stick**, **Ironseed**, **Jaw Beetle Fight** (children
  fight underwater with jaw-sticks on their heads), **Marsh Tag** (underwater,
  tagging by sound), **Sink Tag** (nut-throwing from trees).
- Stormhold's **Fredas Night Stingaree**: lacquered wasp thoraxes on tails, the
  jig "Dancing on Eggs" on a silver slide flute.
- Toys: carved animal and Argonian figurines, **moppet dolls** (rare outside
  Murkmire), frog-shaped squeaky toys, spring-loaded snake boxes,
  **Lilmothiit Fox-Kites** (orange paper kites with a real fox tail, Blackwood).
  Bright-Throat **xeech'kis** (seed dolls) are grown from a nurtured seed whose
  roots are pared into shape, usually depicting local animals — the turtle above
  all, "a powerful, unruly totem" that observes old and new with perfect clarity.

## Calendar — CANON_EXPLICIT

Twelve months, each a social season. Useful for festival placement and for making
time feel Argonian rather than Imperial.

| Month | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Vakka** | Origins. Respect to elders. |
| **Xeech** | Planting; hopeful but melancholy. **First Mourning** — once planted, the nut is lost forever. |
| **Sisei** | Youth and possibility. Sports, competitions, hatchling festivals, romance, bright colours. |
| **Hist-Deek** | **Challenging authority.** Injustices aired, inter-tribal conflict, reflection on one's agency and one's Hist bond. |
| **Hist-Dooka** | Family, tradition, obligation. Most **Chukka-Sei** maturity trials; ends in celebration. |
| **Hist-Tsoko** | The holiest month. Knowledge, wisdom, fullness. **Second Mourning**. The **Root Talk**, when elders recite Saxhleel history. |
| **Thtithil-Gah** | Wonder and simple joy. Travelling entertainers earn most of their year; constant festivals. |
| **Thtithil** | Anticipation, mystery, finality. Many tribes **lay eggs**. |
| **Nushmeeko** | Hard thankless work; the whole tribe labours. |
| **Shaja-Nushmeeko** | Mystery and debate: change, becoming, shifting values. Adolescent gatherings. |
| **Saxhleel** | Creative pursuits — pottery, woodcarving. Elders gather to prepare for the deaths to come. |
| **Xulomaht** | **Third Mourning**, the most significant. Communities mourn the year and hold festivals for the dead. |

Argonians who track time at all are **jekka-wats**; a **jekka-wass** keeps the
**Xinchei-Konu** stone calendar in Murkmire.

| Title | Role |
|---|---|
| **tree-minder** | Tends the Hist, interprets its signs and will; feels when something is wrong with the eggs or the tribe; usually the default arbitrator in disputes. |
| **sap-speaker** | Rare. Direct intermediary; lives among the roots for weeks. |
| **root-herald** | Outward-facing: gathers news, rumours and relationships, negotiates trade. Closer to ambassador than spy — "such a concept is foreign to the Saxhleel". |
| **kaal / raj-kaal** | War-captain / war-chief. **ka-deelith**: war-teacher. |

| **deelith / raj-deelith / raj-nassa** | Teacher / elder teacher / elder leader. The raj-nassa sets the maturity trials. |
| **grave-singer** | Tends and stakes the dead, reads the stakes, sings the final song. |
| **egg-tender / Keeper of the Shell** | Tends the uxith. |
| **chime-maker** | Bright-Throat: makes and reads the Hist's wind-chimes. |

## Language notes for dialogue — CANON_EXPLICIT

**Jel** has **no past or future tense**; word order is fluid to the point of
ambiguity; there is no grammatical difference between an agent and an action; it
thrives on idiom and metaphor and is inseparable from body language. Non-speakers
are **ojel** ("not of the tongue") — not derogatory. Argonians speaking Tamrielic
often preface statements with an emotional qualifier because their body language
doesn't read to outsiders: *"I erect the spine of…"*, *"I extend the claw of
welcome, warrior."*, *"I shake my head in disbelief."*, *"I recoil in mock
disgust."*, *"My rage-quill is engorged!"* Farewell: **"Stay moist."**
Curse: **"Hist piss!"** Exclamation: **"Host of Stormhold."**
Compounds: *kaj-thux* (ample serpent), *deek-beeko* (friend of youth),
*bok-xul* (bowl of death), *vakka-bok* (bowl of the sun), *xal* (sacred),
*xuth* (intruder). Slur for outsiders (Argonian, affectionate-hostile):
"dryskin fools and withered roots".

## Build implications

- This file is the **prop and kit brief**. Anything placed in an Argonian
  interior should come from it: totems, grave-stakes, chimes, reed mats, turtle
  platters, xeech'kis, bone-workers, scale-grooming kits, frog pipes.
- **Three Argonian building kits, and they never blend** (see the section
  below — the interior's kit was ruled 2026-09-02).
- The stilted-platform form in Murkmire is the same form the Blackrose
  boardwalk-city guidance already assumes. Good.
- "Villages move" is a licence for **non-permanent settlement instances** if we
  ever want them, and an explanation for why a map is never quite right.
- **Boat kit brief**: native craft are rafts, canoes and twin-hulled
  platform-canoes — poled, paddled or tail-driven, no keels; sails mark a hull
  as foreign (Dunmer/Imperial) or Kothringi-derived. Design mapping to our
  boat classes: docs/research/marsh-watercraft-and-argonian-boats.md.

## The three building kits — the never-blend rule, made interior-aware

Ruling of 2026-09-02 (Phase 11 lore critique C1: 108 of 110 hist-heartland
catalogue records carried one boilerplate materials line that mixed *all three*
kits, because the old two-kit rule assigned mud→Shadowfen and reed/stilt→
Murkmire and said **nothing about the interior**, so the authoring pass hedged
by including everything). The interior now has its own kit.

| Kit id | Where | Canon status |
|---|---|---|
| `settlement-mud-v1` | **Shadowfen** and the northern marsh | `CANON_EXPLICIT` — wattle-and-daub over an exposed log skeleton |
| `settlement-stilt-v1` | **Murkmire**, the deltas and the coast | `CANON_EXPLICIT` — reed weave on wooden stilts, platforms ascending over water |
| `settlement-root-v1` | **the interior** — Middle Argonia, the Helstrom basin, the deep inner swamps | `EXTRAPOLATED`, grounded below |
| `settlement-imperial-v1` | foreign foundations | `CANON_EXPLICIT` — ashlar, tile, timber frame |

### `settlement-root-v1` — the grown-root kit (the interior)

**The idiom:** the interior does not *build* a house, it *trains* one. Root and
canopy limb are grafted, bent and tied over years into wall, floor, stair and
threshold while still alive and still growing; the joinery is lashing, not
carpentry; the sealant is sap; the roof is bark shingle and layered canopy leaf;
and every threshold carries chimes, because in the interior the tree is the
neighbour you have to be able to hear.

- **Structure** — living root and trained limb, grafted and bent in place; split
  root-wood decking and canopy timber where a member has to be cut.
- **Joinery** — withy lashing and **flint vine** cord; pegs of hardwood; no nails
  (post-Duskfall metal use is almost unheard of, above).
- **Sealing** — **Hist-sap resin** as glue, caulk and waterproofing; sap-rubber
  gaskets on anything that must stay dry.
- **Roof** — bark shingle, layered canopy frond, sap-sealed. *Not* reed thatch.
- **Fittings** — bone-and-tin chimes at every threshold and eave; totems of wood,
  mud and plumage (province-wide, all kits); grave-stakes; woven grass matting.
- **Stone** — none. Xanmeer stone in an interior settlement appears **unquarried
  and untouched**: a warning that is kept, never a material that is used.
- **Forbidden here** — reed weave, stilt platforms over open water, and
  wattle-and-daub panels. Those are the two coastal kits and they do not belong
  inland; if a record needs one, it needs a stated reason (below).

**Grounding** (all from this file and `hist-and-sap.md` unless noted):
root-training is an attested Argonian craft — Bright-Throat **xeech'kis** are
"grown from a nurtured seed whose roots are pared into shape"
(`Lore:Bright-Throat Village`); **flint vine** is canon cordage "strong enough to
haul heavy objects or be made into a rope-ladder" (`Lore:Xanmeer`); **Hist-branch
staves** are canon worked living wood (`Lore:Xit-Xaht`); **Hist-sap rubber** is a
canon mundane product (`Lore:Murkmire`); **chimes** are canon Argonian
Hist-listening equipment, with a **chime-maker** office (`Lore:Bright-Throat
Village`); the **build-to-be-replaced doctrine** and "stone is a moral error"
(`Lore:Argonian`, `Lore:Duskfall`) bite hardest in the interior, which is the
most orthodox ground in the province; and Helstrom, the interior's capital, is
canonically defended by the marsh and the grove rather than by works
(`Lore:Helstrom`, `../../helstrom.md`) — a culture whose fortification is grown.

### `settlement-root-v1/deeps` — the Naga-Kur variant (not a fourth kit)

In the deep inner swamps root gives way to **lashed cane, black bog-oak, hide and
worked bone**; the Naga-Kur, alone in the province, also **reuse dredged xanmeer
block undressed**. That is heterodoxy, and the orthodox interior reads it as
such — it is the same kit with bone substituted for root, not a new grammar.
Grounded in the Naga-Kur's canon habit of making their gear from the bodies of
their own dead (above) and in the **grave-singer** office who harvests it.
**Grave-stakes are the deeps' signature marker but have no mesh** — build stake
fields from cairns, totems and stockade pikes (asset inventory gap 3).

### The rule, restated for three kits

1. **A single building is one kit.** Never two. This is the original rule and it
   is unchanged.
2. **A settlement is one kit** unless the record states why not — a frontier, a
   migration, a contact site, a foreign quarter. "Why not" goes in
   `why.founding`, not in the materials line.
3. **Foreign work layers, it does not blend**: Imperial ashlar *below*, Argonian
   work *above* (the catalogue already writes this well — "stacked, never
   blended").
4. **The materials line is per place**, never a region constant: it says what
   *this* place is grown from, what it has run short of, and what has gone wrong
   with it.
