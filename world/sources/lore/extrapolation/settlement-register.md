## 1. The magnitude ladder

Canon gives **no population figure for any settlement in Black Marsh**, and only
relative sizes ("largest settlement of Shadowfen", "small port town", "medium-
sized settlement"). Counting heads is therefore both unsupported and useless to
us. What Phase 11 actually needs is **buildable scale**, so the ladder is defined
in structures, not people.

`EXTRAPOLATED` — calibrated against the canon descriptions and against our ×3
horizontal scale (decision 0006).

| Class | Structures | Canon calibration | Typical content |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M5 — major city** | 150–400 | The eight. Modest by Imperial standards; canon repeatedly notes Argonians "seem to have no desire to construct sprawling cities". | Districts, walls or a defensible core, a market, a Hist or a substitute, guild/office buildings, a foreign quarter |
| **M4 — town** | 40–120 | Alten Corimont "had grown to become a small town"; Hissmir has "shops, services, and local Mages and Fighters Guild branches"; Stonewastes "medium-sized" | One centre, a specialism, an outsider presence |
| **M3 — village** | 12–35 | Bright-Throat, Dead-Water, Hutan-Tzel, Root-Whisper | A Hist, an uxith, the office-holders, a defensible approach |
| **M2 — hamlet / holding** | 4–10 | Hixinoag "a jumble of huts half-sunk in mud"; Archein plantation quarters | One family, one trade, one landlord |
| **M1 — camp / station** | 1–3 | Fishing camps, toll posts, ferry stages, hunters' stands | Transient or seasonal |
| **S — site** | n/a | Ruins, xanmeers, forts, prisons, monuments | No civilian population, or a non-civilian one |

**Rule**: no settlement in Argonia exceeds M5, and there are only eight of those.
The province's population is **dispersed by design** — canon's economy is
subsistence and its architecture is deliberately impermanent, so density is low
and settlement count is high. Prefer **many M3s** over a few M4s when populating a
region; that is what "at least twelve distinct tribal groups in Murkmire alone …
scores more exist" actually looks like on the ground.

## 5. Two structural questions closed

### G3.3 — drifting villages

`CANON_EXPLICIT`: "Most villages in Argonia don't stay put for very long"; a
drifting village may reach a city's gates "in a month or two"; Alten Meerhleel is
called a floating village.

`EXTRAPOLATED` resolution — **three distinct phenomena, only one of which needs
engineering**:

1. **Rebuilt-elsewhere villages** (the common case). Argonian building is
   deliberately impermanent; when a channel silts, a bank fails or a season turns,
   the village is *rebuilt* a mile away rather than repaired. No motion is
   required — the world just needs **abandoned village footprints near living
   ones**, which is cheap and reads exactly right.
2. **Floating villages** (rafted platforms moored in slow water). Static in play;
   the mooring is the settlement. Alten Meerhleel and the Lilmoth stilt-quarters
   are this.
3. **Genuinely drifting villages** (Hixinoag). Recommend **two** in the whole
   province, hand-authored, each with **two or three fixed mooring sites** it
   occupies at different points in the flood cycle. That gives us the canon
   phenomenon at a fixed engineering cost and makes finding one a real event.
   `DEFER` the implementation choice to Phase 11/14; the lore answer is settled.

   *(Revised from "exactly one" during the Phase 11 Part 2 catalogue
   reconciliation, 2026-09-02. The old number was an engineering-cost
   recommendation, not a canon limit — canon says most villages do not stay put.
   The catalogue holds canon-named **Hixinoag** on the northern caravan route and
   one inner-swamp raft village whose abandoned moorings form a trail of ghost
   villages; two is still a fixed, small cost, and the second is what makes the
   phenomenon read as a practice rather than a curiosity.)*

### G5.8 — the state of the xanmeer network

`CANON_EXPLICIT`: Argonians **actively maintain** the xanmeers "so that the
mistakes of the past would never be repeated"; some tribes **live in them** for
their walls and still-working ancient defences; each had a Hist chamber; many
hold traps, vakka-stone-powered mechanisms and relics; the modern Argonian has
"no use for the more benign trinkets found within".

`EXTRAPOLATED` four-state model for every xanmeer we place:

| State | Share | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Tended** | ~40% | A living tribe maintains it as a monument. Not a dungeon. Entering to loot is a serious offence and the locals will say so. |
| **Inhabited** | ~20% | A tribe lives in it. A settlement with stone walls and working ancient defences — the strongest fortifications in the province. |
| **Sealed** | ~25% | Deliberately closed, by its people or by collapse. Traps live, mechanisms intact, contents undisturbed. The dungeon default. |
| **Broken** | ~15% | Opened, looted, occupied by something else — bandits, Blackguards, Sul-Xan, undead, voriplasm, a Fenlord's reach. |

**Rule for Phase 12**: a xanmeer's state is a *social* fact before it is a level
design fact. Decide who is looking after it, and the interior follows.

## 6. Two free placements now fixed

### White Rose Prison — G3.6

`CANON_EXPLICIT`: named as one of the several prisons the Imperials built after
1E 2837; cited again in funerary lore — Argonians who die away from the Hist,
"even in stone prisons **such as White Rose**", can return if **their bones are
brought to the dirt**. Never located.

`EXTRAPOLATED` placement: **inland, in the western burn country between Soulrest
and the interior** — i.e. in the ground the 3E 427 uprisings cleared. Reasoning:
(a) canon's siting logic for Imperial prisons in this province is "put it where
the marsh is the wall" (Blackrose Prison, explicitly); (b) Soulrest was the
Third-Era capital and its hinterland was mining and timber country worked by
convict-adjacent labour, so a prison serving that economy belongs there; (c) it
gives Soulrest's contested hinterland a hard anchor and explains why the tribes
who burned the mines have a *specific* grudge.

State in 4E 201: **abandoned, structurally sound, and full of Argonian dead who
never got home.** That is the whole quest in one line, it is canon-sourced, and
it gives the Blackrose Chainbreakers a purpose that is not simply "open a prison".

### Fort Swampmoth — G3.7

`CANON_EXPLICIT`: an Imperial Legion fortress of the Third Empire; "during the
Oblivion Crisis, rumours swirled that the Legions had been **recalled** from Fort
Swampmoth." Never located.

`EXTRAPOLATED` placement: **the Blackwood frontier, commanding the Blackwood Road
between Slough Point and Gideon.** Reasoning: it is the only land approach into
the province, canon puts Imperial road and fortification work exactly there
("the Imperial Legion built roads and fortifications around Gideon"), and a fort
that was *rumoured emptied at the Oblivion Crisis* belongs on the route the
Empire would have stripped first.

State in 4E 201: **held, but not by the Empire.** `EXTRAPOLATED` — the least
inventive outcome for an intact stone fort on the province's only land road,
abandoned 200 years ago, is that **somebody moved in and started charging**. Give
it to the state as its western customs post if the owner wants the border legible,
or to a toll-taking outfit with a claim if they want it murkier. Either way the
Imperial eagle is still over the gate because nobody bothered to chisel it off,
which is the most eloquent thing about the place.

## 7. Currency and exchange — G2.12

`CANON_EXPLICIT` anchors: septims circulate at the frontier (a clerk pays "five
gold" for directions and it is accepted instantly); Lilmoth's merchant council
"imposed tariffs"; the Archeins embezzled *money*; the Empire poured *millions in
gold* into the province; Argonians of the deep interior have "no use for the more
benign trinkets found within the ruins — they would prefer **fresh slugs** to feed
themselves, and **sturdy tools**".

`EXTRAPOLATED` three-tier model:

| Tier | Where | Medium |
|---|---|---|
| **Coin** | The eight cities, the ports, the roads, the border | **Septims still circulate**, 200 years after the Empire left, because nobody minted a replacement and the coin is good. Reasoning: this is the ordinary fate of a hard currency in a state with no mint, and it is quietly humiliating in a way that suits the province. Dunmer and Khajiit coin circulates in the north and south-west respectively. |
| **Goods and obligation** | Villages, the inner rim | Barter in tools, salt, sap, hide, worked craft, and **owed labour**. A root-herald negotiating a trade agreement is negotiating *reciprocity*, not price. |
| **Nothing** | The deep interior | The Agacephs' response to an Imperial's coin is to take it and shrug. Value here is food, tools, safe passage and information. Coin is a curiosity. |

**Play consequence**: the further in the player goes, the less money works — a
capability-and-knowledge progression that costs no systems work and reinforces
the fixed-danger model. `AGENT_INVENTED` flag: the specific claim that no Argonian
mint exists is ours; canon neither states nor contradicts it, and it is the least
inventive reading of a state canon never once shows administering anything.
