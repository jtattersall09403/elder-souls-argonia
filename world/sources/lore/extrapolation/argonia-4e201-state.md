# Argonia in 4E 201 — state of the province

Packet 3 output, 2026-08-24. The synthesis the world build and the quest plan
consume when raw canon runs out.

**Every claim is tiered.** `CANON_EXPLICIT` / `CANON_DERIVED` / `EXTRAPOLATED` /
`AGENT_INVENTED` per [PROGRESS.md](PROGRESS.md). Where a claim is
`EXTRAPOLATED`, the reasoning and its anchors are given inline. Nothing here
overrides a cited dossier fact.

**Two things are held back for the owner** and are marked `PENDING Q1` / `Q2` /
`Q3` throughout: the status of the An-Xileel, who speaks for the Hist, and the
state of Blackrose Prison. See [owner-questions.md](owner-questions.md). The rest
of this document is written so that it survives *any* of the offered answers —
the Q-dependent parts are isolated.

---

## 0. The one-paragraph version

`EXTRAPOLATED` — Argonia in 4E 201 is a quiet, inward-turned, poor and extremely
dangerous country that has spent a century and a half recovering from a disaster
it inflicted on itself. It is not an empire, not a rising power, and not a
colony. It is a patchwork of tribal territories, river-ports and city polities
that share a language, a religion of change, and a memory of being purged by
their own government — bound loosely by a state whose legitimacy is contested and
whose reach ends where the water gets deep. Its borders are closed by geography
rather than by soldiers. Foreigners are tolerated at the coasts, licensed in the
cities, and dead in the interior. **Reasoning**: every dated 4E 201 fact canon
gives about Argonia is a fact about withdrawal (thinning frontier patrols, a
closed Shadowscale school, a dying order, a party unmentioned for centuries),
and every structural fact about the province — three depopulations, a failed
colonial economy, a people who do not think of themselves as a nation — points
the same way.

## 1. The name, the idea, and whether there is a state

### The name — `CANON_DERIVED`

The An-Xileel "renamed the land **Argonia**" at secession (Lore:Kingdom of Black
Marsh). Argonians have **no word for their homeland** and "do not consider it a
singular nation" (Lore:Black Marsh). So:

- **Official / diplomatic name**: *Argonia*. Used on documents, by foreigners, and
  by whoever is claiming to speak for the whole.
- **Foreign vernacular**: *Black Marsh*, still, everywhere outside.
- **What Argonians actually say**: the region, the water, the tribe, the Hist.
  *kronka-thatith* — "everything-egg" — and the wide-swamp gesture, when pressed
  by an outsider who insists there must be a name. `CANON_EXPLICIT`
- **Play note**: an NPC who calls the province "Argonia" without irony is telling
  you something about their politics. That is free characterisation and it is
  canon-true.

### Is there a state? — `PENDING Q1`, but the shape holds either way

Whatever the owner decides about the An-Xileel, the following is stable:

`EXTRAPOLATED` — **There is a state, and it is thinner than it claims to be.**
Reasoning: (a) canon requires *something* to have kept the Empire out of southern
Morrowind since 4E 6 and to have maintained "occasional patrols" on that frontier
in 4E 201 — patrols imply a payer; (b) canon requires *something* to have
prevented the Empire returning to the province at any point in 150 years, during
which the Empire fought and lost a Great War; (c) but canon equally requires that
whatever it is generated **no recorded event for 153 years**, which rules out an
energetic centralised power.

The least inventive reading is a **minimal state**: a border force, a customs
regime at the recognised ports, a claim to speak for the province abroad, and
almost no writ inland. Everything else is done by cities, tribes and Hist
communities exactly as it was before.

`AGENT_INVENTED` (flagged for owner) — a serviceable name for what such a state
calls itself, whoever runs it: **the Root Concord**, or simply *the Concord*,
with its officers styled **Archwardens** (the one An-Xileel office title canon
gives us, Lore:Kingdom of Black Marsh) and its council still called **the
Organism** if the An-Xileel line is unbroken. Recommend reusing the canon terms
rather than inventing: *Archwarden* and *the Organism* cost nothing and buy
continuity.

### Where its seat is — `EXTRAPOLATED`

Not Lilmoth. The An-Xileel's council sat at Lilmoth and Lilmoth was destroyed by
their own weapon; no successor would rebuild the capital on the site of its
founding catastrophe, and the city tree there has now gone rogue twice.
Reasoning by elimination against canon's own candidates:

- **Helstrom** is called "the capital" in the Simulacrum, has never been taken,
  is the Hist heartland, and sits where all the rivers drain. It is the only city
  whose claim to primacy is *Argonian* rather than colonial.
- **Soulrest** was the Third Era capital but that is precisely a colonial
  inheritance, and its hinterland burned its own mines down.
- **Stormhold** was the Pact capital and invented the Vicecanon office, but it is
  a border city on the Dunmer river.

**Recommended**: the state's formal seat is **Helstrom**; its *working* offices —
customs, ports, the frontier — are at Stormhold and Archon because that is where
the work is. A capital nobody can reach is a very Argonian arrangement and it
matches "the immortal capital" language exactly. This also serves the quest plan,
which already wants Helstrom as an interior political-religious centre reached
early by rootworm.

## 2. Who actually holds power

`EXTRAPOLATED` throughout, from the anchors named. This is the layer the game
spends most of its time in.

### 1. Tribes and tree-minders — the real government of most of the province

Canon gives the tribal office set in full (tree-minder, sap-speaker, root-herald,
kaal/raj-kaal, naheesh, deelith, raj-nassa, grave-singer, egg-tender) and states
that in Murkmire "local tree-minders and warchiefs often dealt with affairs in
their own territory" with disputes going to a mutually chosen arbitrator.
`CANON_EXPLICIT`. Nothing in the Fourth Era touches this; a purge aimed at
*assimilated* Argonians and foreigners would, if anything, have **strengthened**
the un-assimilated tribal interior relative to the cities.
→ **In 4E 201 the tribal layer is the strongest it has been since before the
Second Empire.** That is the least inventive consequence of 4E 48.

### 2. City institutions — old offices, new occupants

- **Vicecanons** `EXTRAPOLATED`: the office was invented at Stormhold precisely to
  give a mixed marsh city a civilian magistrate when nobody else would, and it
  outlived the Pact that created it as a *title* even though the Pact is gone.
  It is the only urban Argonian governing office canon ever provides. Recommend
  it as the standard title for a city magistrate across the north (Stormhold,
  Alten Corimont, Thorn), with each city having one to three of them, chosen
  locally by whatever means that city uses. Reasoning: reusing a canon office is
  strictly better than inventing one, and the plurality (three at Stormhold in
  2E 582, one per constituent people) is a ready-made mechanism for representing
  mixed populations.
- **Merchant councils** `CANON_DERIVED`: Lilmoth's council of powerful merchants
  is canon for the 2E and 3E and is a *structural* response to a port with no
  ruler. Trade towns reconstitute merchant councils because the alternative is
  nobody setting tariffs. Expect them at Lilmoth and Archon.
- **Captains** `CANON_DERIVED`: Alten Corimont is run by whoever commands the
  harbour; canon names two (One-Eye in 2E, Lord Tibius in 3E). The office is
  "captain of the port", however styled.
- **Archeins** `EXTRAPOLATED`, answering the gap canon itself flags: the Archeins
  were the *rural administration of Black Marsh for thirteen centuries* — hated,
  but functional, and their institutions transfer (Heita-Meen took the Archein
  Guard by duel and marched it to war). A nativist revolution that needed to
  collect anything at all outside the cities could not simply delete them.
  **Recommended reading**: the *name* is now an insult and no one claims it; the
  *families* largely survive, stripped of their Imperial titles, rebranded, and
  now performing the same functions — tolls, levies, land-holding, brokerage —
  in the state's name instead of the Empire's. A minority were killed or
  dispossessed in the secession; a minority fled to Cyrodiil; the rest changed
  their letterhead. This is the least inventive answer *and* it hands the world a
  legible class antagonism that needs no foreign villain — which the quest plan
  already wants for Gideon and Thorn.
- **Hist communities** `CANON_DERIVED`: possess an authority the state cannot
  replace, because eggs, names, souls, morphology and burial all run through the
  Hist. Any city with a city tree has a second government whose writ is
  biological. `PENDING Q2` for whether that authority is coordinated province-wide.

### 3. Armed force — `EXTRAPOLATED`

- **Shellbacks** (Argonian heavy infantry) are a canon formation type, not a
  single unit; canon shows them raised at Stormhold and trained to stand beside
  Nord and Dunmer heavy infantry. Recommend: shellback companies exist as **city
  levies**, small, raised by Vicecanons or captains, not as a standing army.
- **The frontier**: canon requires only "occasional patrols" in 4E 201 and "a few
  tribes" holding southern Morrowind. That is a **screen, not an army** — a few
  hundred, mostly tribal irregulars with a state stipend, watching a border
  nobody currently wants to cross.
- **Guerrilla capability is the deterrent.** Argonians are "the foremost experts
  in guerrilla warfare throughout Tamriel" `CANON_EXPLICIT`, and the province has
  destroyed or exhausted every army sent into it since 1E 2811. Argonia does not
  need a field army because **no one believes an invasion would work**. That is
  the whole national-security posture, and it is canon.
- **No navy.** Canon never gives Argonia one. Alten Corimont's freebooters went to
  Thras in 1E 2260 and never came back; the Emperor's fleet policed Topal Bay and
  is gone. → The coasts are unpoliced. See §5.

### 4. Ku-vastei and the Shadowscale vacancy — `CANON_DERIVED` + `EXTRAPOLATED`

`CANON_EXPLICIT`: the Archon facility closed in **4E 187**; the **last known
living Shadowscale dies in 4E 201**; the order is "no longer a fully functioning
group" and may be extinct.

`CANON_DERIVED`: that removes an institution that was doing real civic work —
Shadowscales served as **arbiters and peacekeepers with impunity**, brought "swamp
law" down on unruly foreigners and natives alike, and were the elders Lilmoth
looked to for guidance.

`EXTRAPOLATED`: **the province is in the last years of losing its judiciary and
does not have a replacement.** Concretely, in 4E 201:
- A handful of very old ku-vastei are still alive and still arbitrating, and
  everyone knows there will be no more.
- The **Black-Tongues** still identify Shadow-born hatchlings and have had nowhere
  to send them for fourteen years. What they do with them instead — keep them,
  train them themselves, or refuse to acknowledge the sign — is a live and
  entirely canon-consistent question, and an excellent faction premise.
- The **Clutch of Nisswo** partially fills the gap: they are already welcome in
  almost every village including the insular ones, already regarded as *ku-vastei*,
  and already itinerant. But they have **no central governance and no consistent
  doctrine** by design, so they cannot become a judiciary — which is exactly the
  tension worth playing.
- The Dark Brotherhood's Cheydinhal Listener wanted Archon reopened in 4E 187
  `CANON_EXPLICIT`. **Foreign interest in the empty school is live**, and hostile.

## 3. The interior, the villages and the tribal map

`CANON_DERIVED` — The Umbriel purge was aimed at *foreign taint and assimilated
Argonians*. Its victims were therefore concentrated exactly where those people
were: the coastal and frontier cities. The interior tribes — Agaceph, Paatru,
Sarpa, the deep Naga, the Hist-forest communities — were the least affected and
had the most reason to feel vindicated afterwards.

`EXTRAPOLATED` consequences for 4E 201:

- **The centre is culturally dominant and geographically unreachable.** Interior
  tribes did not gain power; they gained *prestige*, and they are indifferent to
  it. "You're in the middle of everywhere, and near nowhere."
- **The village baseline is unchanged and should be built from canon directly**:
  a Hist, an uxith, a tree-minder, a naheesh, a grave-singer, a kaal if the tribe
  fights, a root-herald if it trades, grave-stakes in the mud, totems of wood and
  mud and plumage, mud-and-wattle in the north or reed-and-stilt in the south, and
  the whole thing built to be replaced.
- **Local powers persist because nothing has removed them** (`CANON_DERIVED` —
  each is 2E-attested and never contradicted): the **Sul-Xan** still raid
  Blackwood from Xi-Tsei, Rockgrove and the Silent Halls; the **Blackguards** or
  their heirs still hold ground in northern Murkmire; the **Naga-Kur** still
  control the inner swamps and are still feared; the **Four Winds** of Stonewastes
  still defend their region because they have done it "since their ancestors
  erected their first hut"; **tribeless Naga** are still the province's brigands.
- **Toll-taking is the normal form of rural revenue.** `CANON_DERIVED` from the
  Tum-Taleel owning the Keel-Sakka bridge and the Imperial-era toll-towns: where a
  route crosses water and cannot be bypassed, *somebody owns the crossing*. This
  is our answer to "how do rural powers extract" and it needs no Imperial
  apparatus. Every bridge, ford, mangrove channel and portage in our route
  network should have an owner.
- **Xanmeers are inhabited, maintained and defended.** `CANON_EXPLICIT` — tribes
  live in them for their walls and still-working ancient defences, and Argonians
  maintain them deliberately as warnings. A xanmeer is not a dungeon by default;
  it is a dungeon when its tribe is gone.

## 4. The eight cities in 4E 201

Each entry: **condition · who runs it · what it lives on · who lives there ·
posture to outsiders**. Tiers inline. Demographic notes reconcile canon against
the quest plan's community priors ([docs/quests/10-political-frame.md](../../../../docs/quests/10-political-frame.md) §9).

### Helstrom — the interior capital

- **Condition** `CANON_DERIVED`: intact. Never besieged, untouched by Umbriel
  (it was off the path and inland), and the least Imperialised city in the
  province. The **3E interior settlements** built around it by the fourth century
  are the part that decayed — a ring of half-swallowed 3E villages on the
  approaches is the right ruin texture here.
- **Who runs it** `EXTRAPOLATED` `PENDING Q2`: a coalition, not a monarch. The
  Scalded Throne is empty and canon doubts it ever wasn't. Recommend: a standing
  assembly of **interior tribes' naheesh and tree-minders** plus the city's own
  offices, meeting in the month of **Hist-Tsoko** for the Root Talk and
  functioning by consensus the rest of the year — with the state's formal seat
  lodged here and largely ignored by it. The "**dangerous city guard**" is
  `CANON_EXPLICIT` and should be a *tribal* levy answerable to the assembly, not
  a professional force: dangerous because it is not accountable to strangers.
- **Economy** `EXTRAPOLATED`: it does not trade outward. It receives — the rivers
  all drain here — and redistributes. Rootworm and raft traffic; the Panther River
  fork; pilgrimage and Root Talk traffic.
- **People** `CANON_DERIVED`: overwhelmingly Argonian, drawn from the exclusive
  interior breeds; a very small, protected, licensed outsider presence. The quest
  plan's ~97% Argonian prior is well supported.
- **Outsiders**: reach it or don't. There is no border to cross, only marsh.

### Stormhold — the layered north-west

- **Condition** `CANON_EXPLICIT`+`EXTRAPOLATED`: overrun by Umbriel's undead in
  4E 48 with "light" destruction relative to those on the path. 153 years is ample
  to rebuild; the scar that persists is **demographic**, not architectural. The
  waterfall, the ravine-severed mounds, the Hist in its pond and the Silyanorn
  substructure are all intact.
- **Who runs it** `EXTRAPOLATED`: **Vicecanons**, plural, the office it invented —
  and this is the city where the state's working offices sit (customs on the
  border river, the frontier screen).
- **Economy**: river traffic to Morrowind and to Alten Corimont; the crystal trade
  out of the tunnels beneath (`CANON_DERIVED` — Varus's prisoners dug for them and
  they have market value); the causeway to Bogmother; guild halls in the Ayleid
  compound.
- **People**: the largest **Dunmer** minority in the province, and the oldest —
  descended from a colonial population, not from Accession-War refugees. The quest
  plan's ~22% is defensible. The old **western Argonian quarter** is where the
  collaborators lived; that is a live piece of local memory.
- **Outsiders**: pragmatic. This is the city that has always had to live with
  them.

### Gideon — the western frontier and the records city

- **Condition** `CANON_EXPLICIT`: overrun by Umbriel with "light" destruction, and
  **explicitly "an active settlement" later in the Fourth Era**. The only major
  city canon guarantees is alive in our era.
- **Who runs it** `EXTRAPOLATED`: the **Governor's Mansion is occupied by whoever
  won the argument** — recommend a civic council seated in the old mansion, with
  the courthouse still functioning as a courthouse because Gideon is the one place
  in Argonia where written records and Cyrodiilic legal habits survive. That is
  precisely what the quest plan wants Gideon for, and canon supports it better
  than it supports anything else about the city.
- **Economy** `CANON_DERIVED`: the Blackwood Road when the monsoon allows, the
  Leyawiin trade, hunting and herb dealing, the **rootworm terminus** (red clay),
  Onkobra pottery. The Archein plantations are **derelict and were already
  derelict in the 3E**; do not rebuild them.
- **People** `CANON_EXPLICIT`: majority **Lukiul** Argonian plus **Nibenese**-
  descended families who have now been here two centuries without an Empire.
  Dibella worship persists (canon ties it to the Kothringi substrate, which makes
  it *local*, not merely imported). Khajiit traders on the Leyawiin road. The
  quest plan's ~22% Imperial-descended is high but defensible for the city proper.
- **Outsiders**: the easiest city in Argonia for a foreigner — canon says its
  Argonians literally cook for foreign stomachs.
- **The 4E 48 wound**: the An-Xileel's weapon killed Gideon's Lukiul majority.
  This city has the strongest possible reason to distrust nativists, and it is
  the reason the quest plan's Lukiul/sovereignty argument belongs here.

### Lilmoth — `PENDING Q1`

- **Condition** `CANON_EXPLICIT`: **destroyed in 4E 48**. Canon says nothing after.
- **Recommended reading** `EXTRAPOLATED`: rebuilt, but not restored. Reasoning:
  the site is the best natural harbour on the south coast, has a Hist, and sits at
  the mouth of a bay whose whole hinterland must trade *somewhere*; abandonment of
  such a site for 150 years is less likely than reoccupation. But the *city* that
  died was an An-Xileel capital of merchant villas and Imperial ruins, and its
  killer was its own Hist. So: **a smaller, lower, wholly Argonian town on stilts
  over a mass grave**, with the Old Imperial quarter left drowned and unrepaired
  because nobody will build there, Pusbottom repopulated because slums always are,
  and a **third Hist** grown from the second's root — young, watched with open
  fear, and attended by a tree-minder whose job everyone regards as a vigil.
  The **Ixtaxh Xanmeer** the An-Xileel pumped dry has flooded again.
- **Who runs it**: a merchant council again, `CANON_DERIVED` from the fact that a
  ruler-less trade port reconstituted one twice before.
- **People**: Argonian-majority with a working foreign dock population. The quest
  plan's ~80% Argonian is right; its "large An-Xileel and commercial centre"
  framing is `PENDING Q1`.
- **Outsiders**: the "unlicensed foreigners stay on the docks" rule is the single
  best piece of political texture canon gives us here. Recommend keeping it as a
  **lapsed law that some officials still enforce** — which is more playable than
  either enforcing or repealing it.

### Thorn — the grassland border city

- **Condition** `EXTRAPOLATED`: intact, prosperous by provincial standards, and
  the closest thing Argonia has to normal agriculture — because canon put
  temperate grassland here and saltrice in it.
- **Who runs it**: Vicecanon(s) and the field-owners. `EXTRAPOLATED`: with Dres
  gone, the **saltrice fields passed to whoever was standing in them** — freed
  labourers' descendants and the ex-Archein families who managed them. That
  unresolved question of title is the city's central domestic politics and it
  requires no invention beyond the transfer itself.
- **Economy**: saltrice (the crop the East Empire Company used to export), the
  Tear road, border tolls, smuggling.
- **People**: ~75% Argonian with a large **Dunmer** minority, many from mixed
  families whose ancestors were on both sides of the coffles. Small Nord and
  Imperial presence.
- **Outsiders**: transactional and unsentimental. "The guards always turn a blind
  eye" `CANON_EXPLICIT` — this is a city where money moves.

### Archon — the eastern port

- **Condition** `EXTRAPOLATED`: intact, off Umbriel's path, and the province's
  best functioning harbour.
- **Who runs it** `EXTRAPOLATED`: a harbour authority in the EEC's old buildings —
  pilots, wharfingers and a merchant council — with the state's customs officers
  alongside. Reasoning: the canon trade line (Archon–Lilmoth–Soulrest–Tear) needs
  a shore organisation, and the physical plant is standing and unowned.
- **Economy** `CANON_DERIVED`: **the province's principal legal port.** With no
  Imperial fleet and no Argonian navy, the difference between the harbour trade
  and the pirate trade is paperwork, and Archon is where the paperwork is.
  The **letters-of-marque tradition is the natural precedent** for how a
  shipless state polices its water: it charters the pirates it cannot beat.
  `EXTRAPOLATED`, anchored on 1E 2833.
- **People**: ~79% Argonian with a substantial **Dunmer** minority (Tear is a short
  sail), a small Imperial and Khajiit presence, and a visible sailor population.
- **The Shadowscale facility**: closed since 4E 187, standing, and wanted by the
  Dark Brotherhood. `CANON_EXPLICIT`. Recommend it be **sealed by the city and
  quietly watched**, with at least one very old ku-vastei living near it.
- **Signature**: the amphibious civic culture. Children play underwater games;
  guar breathe underwater; the harbour works in three dimensions. Archon is our
  showcase for the swimming systems in a *safe* setting.

### Soulrest — the ex-capital

- **Condition** `EXTRAPOLATED`: an administrative city with nothing left to
  administer. Imperial government quarter half-empty and repurposed; the mines and
  timber concessions behind it **still derelict since 3E 427** because canon says
  the tribes destroyed them and nothing says anyone rebuilt.
- **Who runs it** `EXTRAPOLATED`: the most contested of the eight. Canon records
  an unexplained "**struggle for regional hegemony**" here, which is a licence to
  make Soulrest's politics genuinely unresolved: a merchant council, an
  ex-Imperial civil apparatus with no sovereign, the interior tribes who burned
  the mines and have never been reconciled, and the mangrove-river interests.
- **Economy**: the Southern Sea trade, salvage, and — decisively — **Bramman's
  river**, the concealed navigable route from Topal Bay to the Blackrose lakes.
  Whoever controls that channel controls the province's smuggling.
- **People**: the most mixed city: ~60% Argonian, a high **Khajiit** presence
  (canon: a large share of Southern Sea seafarers are Khajiit), Imperial-descended
  administrators' families, Bosmer and other maritime minorities. The quest plan's
  priors fit here better than anywhere.
- **Outsiders**: welcome, watched, and expected to be carrying something.

### Blackrose — the city that swallows people

- **Condition** `EXTRAPOLATED`: functioning but diminished, on its lake island
  with stilt and boardwalk districts over the water (the Phase 6 gate guidance
  already on file). Visible ruined-and-rebuilt quarters of several different ages
  — the 2E "in ruins" period and the late-3E rebellion both left marks — plus
  abandoned siege earthworks in the surrounding marsh from Welloc's campaign.
- **Who runs it** `EXTRAPOLATED`: whoever holds the island core. Recommend a
  narrow, defensive, self-perpetuating city government whose legitimacy is
  possession — because a city that rebelled against an empire and was broken by a
  battlemage does not produce trusting institutions.
- **Economy**: the three-river confluence makes it the **hinge of the south** —
  everything moving between Murkwood, Oliis Bay, eastern Blackwood and Bramman's
  river passes here. Tolls, transit, and whatever comes out of the Rose.
- **People**: Argonian-majority with the province's oddest mix — prison staff and
  their descendants, ex-prisoners, deserters, refugees, coerced migrants. The
  quest plan's "unusual diversity" framing is exactly right and is well anchored.
- **The Rose**: `PENDING Q3`.

### Alten Corimont — the neutral port

- **Condition** `CANON_DERIVED`: a small town (it had grown to one by the late 3E),
  on its waterway, with the **beached ship** still serving as its social hall
  because Argonians rebuild everything else and would not bother replacing that.
- **Who runs it**: a **captain of the port**, by whatever means captains are made
  there.
- **Policy** `CANON_DERIVED`: **principled neutrality with a price list.** Canon
  shows it hosting both sides of a world war, refusing to pick, and killing the
  faction that tried to make it. That is a durable institutional stance and
  should survive.
- **Economy**: smuggling, transit, salvage, and the northern trunk route
  (Stormhold → Tenmar Wall → Alten Corimont → Riverwalk → Thorn).
- **Memory**: the All Flags Navy dead. A memorial, a grudge against the sea, and
  foreign naval heirlooms in Argonian hands.

## 5. Argonia and the world

`EXTRAPOLATED` from the anchors in [../topics/foreign-powers.md](../topics/foreign-powers.md).

| Power | Position in 4E 201 |
|---|---|
| **The Empire** | Absent. It has been absent for two centuries, lost the Great War in 4E 175, and has no fleet in Topal Bay. Recommend: **no official Imperial presence**, but Imperial *people* everywhere — descendants, merchants, scholars, and at most a trade factor at Archon with no diplomatic status. The Empire's last act here (Hierem, 4E 47) preceded the worst disaster in the province's modern history, which is not a relationship anyone is eager to renew. |
| **Morrowind** | A cold, settled frontier. Argonians hold parts of southern Morrowind and Scathing Bay with "occasional patrols"; the Dunmer consider the war long over and are not pressing. Trade across the Tear road and the Stormhold river is real and unsentimental. Grievance runs both ways and neither side is acting on it. `CANON_EXPLICIT` for the 4E 201 state. |
| **The Aldmeri Dominion / Thalmor** | No presence, and canon gives them none. But they incited the uprising that made Argonia independent, and in the 2E they tried to end Argonian reproduction. Recommend: **Thalmor interest in Argonia is real, deniable, and conducted at arm's length** — through third parties, scholars and money — because that is how the Thalmor operate and because a visible Thalmor presence would contradict the province's whole posture. Any Hist-directed threat plot is *strongly* precedented. |
| **Elsweyr / Khajiit** | The most present foreigners after the Dunmer, and the least political: Southern Sea seafarers are largely Khajiit, moon sugar moves through the province, and Khajiit traders work the Leyawiin road. |
| **Cyrodiil / Leyawiin** | The Blackwood Road and the Gideon–Leyawiin trade are the only functioning land trade in the province, and canon says the roads close in monsoon season. Slough Point's customs house is on the *Cyrodiil* side; the Argonian side of that crossing is ours to place. |
| **East Empire Company** | Gone from the province; its wharves and bonded stores are not. |
| **Cyrodilic Collections** | Recommend **revival** as the legitimate foreign presence in our era. It is Imperial-founded but its stated purpose — returning Argonian antiquities to Argonians and building a museum in Black Marsh — is the only foreign programme in canon an Argonian government could endorse, and it was betrayed from within last time, which is a good memory to work against. `EXTRAPOLATED`, low risk, high utility. |
| **Pirates** | Two grounds, two characters. **Archon coast**: ex-privateers with paperwork, chartered or half-chartered, the state's substitute for a navy. **Eastern Topal Bay and the mangrove river**: Bramman's heirs, hidden channels, genuinely outlaw. Southern Sea pirates keep low profiles to avoid Altmer and Maormer warships. `CANON_DERIVED`. |

**The border regime** `EXTRAPOLATED`: Argonia has three legal entries — the
**Blackwood Road** at Gideon (seasonal), the **Tear road** at Thorn, and the
**ports**, principally Archon. It has one deliberate loophole, **Alten
Corimont**, which will take anyone. Everything else is marsh, and the marsh does
the work no border guard could. This satisfies the acceptance rule of "canon exit
roads, closed playable edges" without inventing a customs service.

## 6. Economy, trade and routes

### The rule — `CANON_EXPLICIT`

Subsistence, not surplus. Large-scale cash-cropping is impossible here and canon
says so in as many words. Nothing about independence changed the soil.

### What actually moves — `EXTRAPOLATED` from canon commodities

- **Internally**: food, reeds, clay, timber for building, fish, sap, salt, tools.
  In **small quantities, by raft and rootworm**, because canon proved that beats
  bulk freight 5:1 on time and absolutely on spoilage.
- **Outward, low volume, high value**: worked jewellery and **blue jasper**;
  **xeech'kis** seed dolls (canon: "highly sought after"); woven work (secrets
  closely guarded); **wamasu hide and luminescent organs** ("valuable across
  Tamriel" — tarps and lanterns); **Hist-sap rubber**; **Onkobra red-clay
  pottery**; alchemical reagents and rare swamp flora; **saltrice** from Thorn;
  rice from Hereguard; hunting produce and herbs through Gideon.
- **Outward, illegal**: **Hist sap** (a bloodlust hallucinogen to non-Argonians —
  the single most lore-appropriate crime in the province), **daril**, antiquities
  looted from xanmeers, and **moon sugar** in transit.
- **Not exported**: gold and timber at scale (the Soulrest workings have been
  derelict since 3E 427), and slaves (ended).

### Routes — reconciling canon with our acceptance rule

Our acceptance rules require all eight cities connected by road, "legs may be
flood-damaged, bridged or ferried, never absent". Canon supplies:
Blackwood Road (Leyawiin–Gideon), the Gideon–Leyawiin trade route through old
Kothringi land, the muddy Onkobra road east from Gideon, stone causeways
(Stormhold–Bogmother), the Tear road north from Thorn, and the reed-field
Imperial Commerce Road. It does **not** supply a complete internal network.

`EXTRAPOLATED` reconciliation: **the network exists, and most of it is water.**
A "road" in Argonia is a *route with a maintained crossing at every water*: a
causeway where the ground is old and firm, a boardwalk where it isn't, a ferry
or a toll-bridge where the channel is deep, and long stretches that are simply
navigable water with markers. Legs close seasonally (monsoon, tide) exactly as
canon describes. This satisfies the acceptance rule honestly rather than by
painting Imperial highways across a swamp, and it makes **every crossing an
owned, tollable, contestable point** — which is how rural power works here (§3).

Recommended trunk structure, using canon adjacency
([../regions/secondary-settlements.md](../regions/secondary-settlements.md)):

- **Northern trunk**: Stormhold → Tenmar Wall → Alten Corimont → Riverwalk → Thorn.
- **North-east link**: Thorn → Branchmont → Archon (via Portdun Mont / Seafalls).
- **Western trunk**: Cyrodiil → Blackwood Road → Gideon → Glenbridge/Stonewastes.
- **The Onkobra line**: Gideon → east along the Onkobra → Middle Argonia.
- **Interior spine**: Hutan-Tzel/Rockguard → Greenspring → Helstrom → Tenmar Wall,
  on the Panther River fork.
- **Southern water**: Soulrest → Bramman's mangrove river → Blackrose → Oliis Bay
  → Lilmoth → Alten Meerhleel / Rockpark.
- **Root network** (not a road, and not on any map): the Underground Express, with
  Gideon as a canon terminus and a wintertide southward migration.

## 7. Danger, season and the shape of a journey

`CANON_DERIVED` — **The province's danger is stratified by species, not by level.**
Argonians are immune to poison and disease and cannot drown; non-Argonians die of
swamp rot within a month, cannot drink the water, and are eaten alive by insects.
The Empire sited its worst prison on the principle that *the climate itself kills
non-Argonians past a certain point in the marsh*. This is canon's own version of
our fixed-danger model and it should be implemented literally: the interior is
lethal to outsiders **as terrain**, and an Argonian player experiences a
materially different world there. That directly serves the acceptance rule that
"Argonian physiology materially changes underwater play" — it should change
overland play too.

`EXTRAPOLATED` danger layers, in the order a traveller meets them:

1. **Attrition** — insects, water, mud, heat, disease. Constant, everywhere,
   worse inland. Countered by knowledge and kit (repellent emollient, depth
   sticks, purifying flower buds, pahnjees), not by combat.
2. **Terrain and season** — monsoon road closures, tidal crossings that shut for
   days, flood that lifts the unstaked dead out of the ground, peat-fire gas vents
   in the west, "pinnacled mountains that fall apart at the slightest ripple".
3. **Social danger** — tolls, tribal territory, Naga brigands, Sul-Xan raiders,
   Blackguards, the Naga-Kur's feared inner swamps, and cities whose guards are
   themselves the hazard (Helstrom, Thorn).
4. **Set-piece creatures** — wamasu (which electrify whole ponds), swamp
   leviathans, miregaunts (which come back), giant snakes, crocodiles, sea-drakes,
   voriplasm, bog blights the traveller may have caused themselves.

**Seasonality** `CANON_DERIVED`: there is a monsoon; rivers tide hard and far
inland; the marsh floods on a cycle the burial rite is designed around; a
wintertide rootworm migration runs south. Recommend the world model carry at
minimum **wet season / dry season**, with route availability, water level and
some encounter tables keyed to it.
