# Owner questions — decisions the lore workstream will not take alone

Per the workstream charter: province-scale politics and anything that reshapes a
major city's identity goes to the owner. Each question gives the canon position,
the options, and a recommendation. **Nothing here has been applied.**

Raised 2026-08-24. Ordered by how much else depends on them.

---

## Q1 — Do the An-Xileel still rule Argonia in 4E 201? **(blocks the most)**

### What canon actually says

- The An-Xileel formed during the Oblivion Crisis, took the credit for repelling
  Dagon, "successfully united most of the Argonian people under their rule",
  declared independence, sacked the Imperial colonies and invaded Morrowind.
  Peak: **4E 1–6**. `CANON_EXPLICIT`
- After Redoran halted them (4E 6): "they **haven't shown the slightest interest
  in doing anything since then**." `CANON_EXPLICIT`
- In **4E 48** they were **being controlled by the psychotic Hist of Lilmoth**,
  which they had monopolised. On its direction they summoned **Umbriel** to purge
  foreigners **and assimilated Argonians**. It slipped their control and destroyed
  Lilmoth — their own seat — killed everyone in Murkmire beneath it, and overran
  Gideon and Stormhold. `CANON_EXPLICIT`
- Lore:An-Xileel: "**The status of the An-Xileel following the Umbriel Crisis is
  unknown.**"
- Lore:Accession War: "This disaster appears to have **broken the hold the
  An-Xileel held over the Argonian people**, and the mysterious party **receives no
  mention in the following centuries**."

So: canon does not say they fell. It says nobody heard from them again, and
editorialises that their hold was broken. **153 years** separate 4E 48 from our
production year.

### Where the quest plan currently stands

`docs/quests/10-political-frame.md` §5: the An-Xileel are "the strongest known
Fourth-Era nationalist movement", "a powerful province-wide movement and
security/state network" in 4E 201, with the project-original **Veiled Reed** as
their covert intelligence and internal-security office, and the main quest's
handler structure built on it.

### Options

**A. Continuity.** The An-Xileel are the government, essentially as the quest plan
describes. *Pro*: no quest-plan churn; "status unknown" technically permits it.
*Con*: requires the province to have handed sustained power to the party that
summoned the thing that killed a fifth of it, and to have done so for 150 years
without generating a single recorded event. Reads against the grain of both
quoted sentences.

**B. Successor state (recommended).** The An-Xileel as a *movement* broke at
4E 48. What survived is a **successor administration that inherited its offices,
its titles and its name** — Archwardens, the Organism, the customs and frontier
apparatus — but not its mandate. It is a state because someone had to keep
holding the Morrowind line and the ports, not because anyone still believes in it.
Its legitimacy is contested everywhere and openly denied in Gideon and Lilmoth,
the two cities its predecessor's weapon killed. Its reach ends where the water
gets deep.
*Pro*: honours both canon sentences; keeps **every structure the quest plan
needs** (An-Xileel offices, the Veiled Reed, nativist-vs-Lukiul politics, the
Unbound Root's critique that "the An-Xileel cannot claim to speak for all Hist or
all Argonians" — which becomes *demonstrably true* rather than merely arguable);
and gives the main quest's handler organisation a far better internal
contradiction than the plan currently has. The Veiled Reed protecting a state
whose founding crime is public record is a much sharper version of the
"protecting an independent province while using coercive secrecy" tension the
plan already wants.
*Con*: requires rewording `10-political-frame.md` §5 and touching §6.

**C. Collapse.** The An-Xileel are gone; Argonia is a stateless patchwork of
cities and tribes. *Pro*: most literal reading of "receives no mention". *Con*:
removes the province-scale antagonist and handler structure the quest plan is
built on, and struggles to explain the 4E 201 frontier patrols and the Empire's
continued exclusion. Would force a large quest-plan rewrite.

### Recommendation

**Option B.** It is the least inventive answer that satisfies canon, it preserves
the quest plan's architecture, and it *improves* the plan's central theme rather
than costing it anything. Proposed wording in
[quest-plan-deltas.md](quest-plan-deltas.md) D1.

### If B is chosen, one follow-on to confirm

Does the successor state still call itself **An-Xileel**, or has it taken a new
name with An-Xileel as the historical root? Recommendation: it still calls itself
An-Xileel officially — states rarely rename themselves after a disaster they
would rather not discuss — while ordinary people use the office titles
(**Archwardens**, **the Organism**) or a neutral word for "the Concord". That
asymmetry is itself characterisation.

## Q2 — Who speaks for the Hist, province-wide?

### Canon

- All Hist share one mind and speak to one another — **but individuals isolate,
  go rogue, go mad, and can be killed by the others** (Lilmoth, 3E 173 and 4E 8).
  `CANON_EXPLICIT`
- The offices that mediate the bond — **tree-minder**, **sap-speaker** — are
  strictly *tribal*. `CANON_EXPLICIT`
- The An-Xileel's method was to monopolise **one** tree and take counsel from **no
  other**, and it destroyed them. `CANON_EXPLICIT`
- The Hist "guides them, and its people serve it in kind"; the bond is voluntary,
  not bondage; Hist communities hold authority no state can replace.
- Canon never describes any province-wide Hist institution.

### Why it matters to us

The quest plan's **Root of Accord** is a limited, voluntary, renewable
coordination graft among *some* Hist, later treated as inherited and compulsory
by officials — which is a good fit for canon. But the world build still needs an
answer to the practical question: **when something threatens the Hist as a whole,
who convenes?** That question determines whether Helstrom has a religious
authority, whether the state has a Hist policy, and who the player negotiates
with in the endgame.

### Options

**A. Nobody.** There is no province-wide Hist voice. Tree-minders speak for their
own tree only; the trees themselves are one mind but have no mouth for outsiders.
*Pro*: most literal. *Con*: leaves the endgame without an interlocutor.

**B. The Root Talk (recommended).** No standing institution, but a **recurring
occasion**: canon already gives us the **Root Talk**, held in the holiest month
(**Hist-Tsoko**), at which the naheesh of a tribe recite Saxhleel history. Extend
it, minimally, to a **great Root Talk at Helstrom** where interior tribes' naheesh
and tree-minders gather — a convocation, not a council; it has no executive power
and issues no orders, but it is where the Hist's alignment becomes audible.
*Pro*: reuses a canon institution rather than inventing one; gives Helstrom the
interior political-religious character the quest plan wants; gives the endgame a
place to be argued in; and being a *convocation* rather than a hierarchy is
faithful to a people who do not consider themselves a nation.
*Con*: a modest extension of a canon practice.

**C. A standing council of sap-speakers.** *Con*: invents a hierarchy the Hist
have never had and that canon's whole Duskfall arc argues against.

### Recommendation

**Option B.** Cheap, canon-rooted, and it gives both the world build and the main
quest what they need without creating a Hist government.

## Q3 — What is Blackrose Prison in 4E 201?

### Canon

Built in a single day by stone atronachs in the early 2E; **abandoned** when the
Second Empire fell; taken over by the **Blackguards**, the inmates' descendants,
who became Murkmire's largest criminal organisation; **reclaimed** by the Third
Empire; held Jagar Tharn's associates; **broken open in 3E 432**, releasing "the
worst murderers, thieves, and political revolutionaries" into the swamp. Canon
then goes silent. It sits **a day's march south of Blackrose city in northern
Murkmire** — i.e. directly in Umbriel's 4E 48 path.

### Why it needs a decision

The quest plan uses Blackrose as an infamous prison city for the main treasure
hunt and the **Chainbreakers**, and calls the prison "a contested institution".
Whether it is *operating* changes what Chainbreakers are: prison reformers, tomb
robbers, or an abolitionist movement against something that no longer exists.

### Options

**A. Still a prison, run by the Argonian state.** *Pro*: gives the Chainbreakers a
live target and the state a visible coercive apparatus, which suits the Veiled
Reed's contradiction. *Con*: requires a people who were themselves the Empire's
prisoners for 1,300 years to keep the Empire's prison running — a strong claim
that needs deliberate authorial intent, not a default.

**B. Ruin held by the Blackguards' heirs (recommended).** It was abandoned once
and occupied by inmates' descendants once; Umbriel then passed directly over it.
The least inventive 4E 201 state is **the same cycle again**: a stone fortress in
lethal marsh, emptied by catastrophe, reoccupied by whoever had nowhere else to
go — outlaws, exiles, prison-born families three generations deep, and the
undead Umbriel left. It is a **dungeon with a resident population and a claim to
its own legitimacy**, which is far more interesting than either a working prison
or an empty ruin.
*Pro*: doubly precedented; keeps the Chainbreakers meaningful (they free people
from *this*, and from the informal bondage that replaced the formal kind); avoids
making the Argonian state a jailer by default, while leaving that available as a
darker discovery.
*Con*: the quest plan's "contested institution" framing needs a light reword.

**C. Sealed and shunned.** Argonians wall it up as they maintain xanmeers — as a
warning. *Pro*: very Argonian. *Con*: least playable.

### Recommendation

**Option B**, with the option of a **state-run annexe or work-camp elsewhere**
(perhaps at the unlocated **White Rose Prison**) if the owner wants a live
coercive institution for the Chainbreakers to oppose. That splits the two
functions cleanly and keeps both.

## Q4 — Does Lilmoth exist, and if so as what? *(lower stakes, but city-identity)*

Canon: **destroyed in 4E 48**, no further mention. The quest plan makes Lilmoth
"a large An-Xileel and commercial centre" with a major Thieves Guild heist,
Pusbottom, and civic disputes.

Recommendation (developed in
[argonia-4e201-state.md](argonia-4e201-state.md) §4): **rebuilt but not restored**
— a smaller, lower, wholly Argonian stilt town over a mass grave, the drowned
Imperial quarter left unrepaired because nobody will build there, Pusbottom
repopulated, and a **third Hist** grown from the second's root, young and watched
with open fear. This keeps every quest-plan element (commerce, Pusbottom, heist,
civic disputes) while making the city's identity its own catastrophe rather than
a generic port. Flagged here because it materially reshapes a major city.

**Owner decision needed on one point only**: is Lilmoth's Hist trusted, feared,
or actively quarantined? All three are defensible; it changes the city's tone
considerably.

## Q5 — Tone check: how dark is the province's self-image?

Not a lore question, a direction question, but it propagates everywhere.

Canon hands us a province whose government tried to exterminate a section of its
own people within recorded memory, whose religion is about accepting loss, and
whose landscape is a graveyard with the biographies of the dead written on stakes
in the mud. That can be played as **melancholy and dignified** (recommended — it
matches the Nisswo's actual doctrine, which is about *letting go* rather than
despair) or as **grim and haunted**.

Recommendation: **melancholy and dignified, with horror kept local.** The
province-wide register should be a people who have survived three extinctions and
are calm about it; the horror should live in specific places (Veeskhleel
territory, Mazzatun, Deepmire, the Rose, Stillrise) so it stays frightening.
Flagging because it affects music, light, dialogue register and encounter design
across the whole build.
